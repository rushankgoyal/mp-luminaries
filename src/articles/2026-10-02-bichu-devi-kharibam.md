When Bichu Devi Kharibam left her village in Manipur in 2015, she was fourteen years old and had never trained at a professional facility. On October 2, 2026, she was part of the Indian women's hockey squad standing in Nagoya after winning the country's first Asian Games gold in the sport in 44 years.

India beat China **1-0** in the final at Gifu Prefectural Green Stadium, with Lalremsiami's 10th-minute goal deciding the match. The win ends India's absence from the continental top step since the 1982 Asian Games and earns India **direct qualification for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics**. It is one of the most significant results in Indian women's hockey since their bronze at the Paris 2024 Olympics.

The result would not have happened without depth. Goalkeeper Savita Punia started in goal across the tournament and made a crucial penalty stroke save in the second quarter of the final. Behind her was Bichu Devi, the squad's second goalkeeper — training, preparing and staying ready for every minute that India might need her. In the demanding cycles of international tournament hockey, that role is what separates squads that contend from those that falter under pressure.

## The Gwalior Road

Bichu Devi Kharibam was born on **December 3, 2000**, in Manipur. In 2015, she was spotted by a coach and umpire who recognised her potential in goal, and made the move to the **Madhya Pradesh Women's Hockey Academy in Gwalior** — the residential programme under the state's sports development authority that has become one of India's most reliable pathways into women's hockey.

The Academy system works through full-time residential training, exposure to national-level coaches and, over time, integration into national age-group camps. For a young goalkeeper from Manipur, arriving in Gwalior at fourteen meant leaving home, adjusting to a new environment, and competing for position from day one. Bichu Devi built a national career through that process.

By her mid-twenties, she had established herself as India's first-choice backup goalkeeper — a position that put her inside the 20-member squad for Asia's biggest multi-sport event. Three of the players in India's Asian Games gold-medal squad this October trained at the Gwalior Academy: Bichu Devi alongside senior defender Sushila Chanu and midfielder Ishika Chaudhary.

At twenty-five, Bichu Devi is entering the years when most goalkeepers reach their peak. With Savita Punia in the later phase of her career, the path from the Academy bench to India's starting position is visible. The Olympic qualification that came with October 2's gold medal means Bichu Devi will be in that picture when India prepares for Los Angeles.

For Madhya Pradesh, the Gwalior programme's value is easiest to measure in moments like this one: three players from a single residential academy in a 20-member squad winning a gold. The state's investment in women's hockey, sustained over two decades, produced that number. On October 2, 2026, in Nagoya, it produced a medal too.
