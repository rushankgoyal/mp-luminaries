At the end of three days of pavilions, product displays and policy showcases at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi, Madhya Pradesh walked away with the first prize for overall performance in agriculture and horticulture. The International Agriculture and Horticulture Expo 2026, which concluded on 27 July, drew roughly 25,000 participants — farmers, scientists, food technology entrepreneurs and government officials from across India. Seven states competed in the same contest: Karnataka, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab and Rajasthan. MP finished first.

Narayan Singh Kushwah, the Cabinet Minister for Horticulture and Food Processing in the Madhya Pradesh government, said the result "reflects the collective efforts of farmers, entrepreneurs and departmental officials." Chief Minister Mohan Yadav also congratulated the state's farming community and the department's teams. The judges had assessed each state's pavilion on the quality of its innovative initiatives, the effectiveness of its government scheme implementation, and the strength of its work connecting growers to food processors — a set of criteria that amounts to asking whether a state's horticulture policy has actually reached the field.

For Kushwah, who has held the portfolio since December 2023, the expo award marks a moment that is partly his and largely belongs to two decades of accumulated effort by farmers and officials across the state. He represents the Gwalior South constituency in the Madhya Pradesh Legislative Assembly and has made the push toward value-added agriculture and food processing a central part of his ministerial brief.

## From Wheat Belt to Horticulture State

The numbers behind the award are substantial. Over the past twenty years, the area under horticultural crops in Madhya Pradesh grew from **4.67 lakh hectares to more than 27 lakh hectares** — nearly a six-fold expansion. Production over the same period climbed from 35 lakh metric tonnes to **417 lakh metric tonnes**, a tenfold increase that did not happen in one policy cycle or under one government. It happened because the soil, the climate and the consistent extension of scheme coverage all pointed in the same direction.

Today, Madhya Pradesh's horticultural productivity stands at **15 tonnes per hectare** — roughly 23 percent above the national average of 12.19 tonnes per hectare. The state is now among India's significant producers of guava, mango, banana, orange, green peas, potatoes, onion, tomato, coriander, chillies and garlic. Districts that once grew little beyond wheat and soybean now supply national and export markets with high-value produce.

The expo pavilion communicated this change through products, verified scheme outcomes and the stories of farmers who moved from staple crops to commercial horticulture. That it stood first among eight major agricultural states in the eyes of the expo jury indicates that the case was made convincingly.

## Why the Award Matters

Agricultural expo prizes are easy to dismiss as ceremonial. But the framework this one uses — evaluating innovation, implementation and market linkages, not just production volume — makes the award a substantive measure of whether a state's horticulture department is doing the harder work of connecting farm output to processing and markets.

Madhya Pradesh is committed, in Kushwah's words, to "further strengthening the agriculture and horticulture sectors and creating better opportunities for farmers through food processing and value addition." The first prize at Bharat Mandapam is evidence that at least on national examination, the approach is being recognised as one worth following.
