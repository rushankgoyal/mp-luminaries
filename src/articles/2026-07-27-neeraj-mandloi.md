On the night of July 25, 2026, Madhya Pradesh issued orders transferring 20 IAS officers. Buried midway through the list, one line made the state's investment community take notice: ACS to Chief Minister Neeraj Mandloi, already the officer who sits at the interface of the state's political and administrative machinery, was given additional charge of the Industries and MSME Department and, with it, the full responsibility of organizing the Global Investors Summit 2027.

GIS, scheduled for January 2027 in Bhopal, is the largest economic event Madhya Pradesh stages. Its 2025 edition drew **₹26.61 trillion** in investment proposals from domestic and international investors across two days in February — a national record for a single-state investors' summit, and one that reset expectations for what an Indian state could pull off. That number is now the floor Mandloi must clear.

He is a 1993-batch IAS officer who has spent more than three decades building his career in Madhya Pradesh. He has served as District Magistrate of Rajgarh, held charge of the Energy Department and the Public Works Department, and since July 2025 has been ACS to the Chief Minister — the officer who translates the CM's directives into daily administrative action across the government. He continues to hold the Energy and Public Service Management portfolios even as the new charges add to his brief.

## The Pre-Event Roadshow Has Already Produced Results

The GIS 2027 outreach campaign launched in July 2026 with a series of investor events in Delhi. On July 16, an interactive session on investment opportunities in Madhya Pradesh attracted proposals worth **₹18,601 crore** across defence manufacturing, data centres, transformer manufacturing, electronics, renewable energy, engineering, and the toy industry — with a projected employment potential of more than **27,592 direct and indirect jobs**. An earlier Bharat Tex 2026 participation added ₹1,592 crore in textile investment commitments. Taken together, the state secured more than **₹20,193 crore** in proposals in the run-up to an event that is still six months away.

That number sits alongside individual investment announcements that signal the kind of sector diversification Madhya Pradesh is chasing. Paras Semiconductors' agreement in July 2026 to build a **₹6,200-crore advanced IC packaging facility** on the Indore-Ujjain corridor is one example — the sort of high-technology investment that would have seemed improbable for the state a decade ago and that the GIS pipeline is now expected to multiply.

Mandloi will spend the months between now and January convening similar conclaves across India and in select overseas markets, assembling the commitments that will give GIS 2027 its opening-day numbers.

## The Breadth of the Brief

The Industries and MSME Department he now leads covers **25 lakh MSME units** across Madhya Pradesh, of which 4.41 lakh are run by women — a constituency with needs and concerns quite different from the large companies that fill an investors' summit stage. Managing both simultaneously, alongside the Energy portfolio, is a measure of the trust the Chief Minister's office is placing in Mandloi at this moment.

The benchmark is not just ₹26.61 trillion. It is whether the investment proposals that flow through the January summit actually take root as factories, facilities, and jobs across the state in the years that follow. That conversion — from promises made on a Bhopal stage to tangible economic life in Indore, Jabalpur, Ujjain, and beyond — is the harder half of the task, and it starts with organizing an event that makes serious investors want to be in the room.
