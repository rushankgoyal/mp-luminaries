The semifinal had barely started when India's plans changed. Raj Kumar Pal, the drag-flicker the team counted on for dead-ball set pieces, left the field with an injury and did not return. Against Pakistan on **October 1**, with a place in the final at stake, India needed its midfielders to hold the structure and keep the attack moving without their first-choice specialist. Nilakanta Sharma was on the pitch when it mattered most. India won **4–3**.

Two days later, in the final against Malaysia, Nilakanta was part of an Indian side that played with the composure of a team that had already survived the hard part. **India beat Malaysia 5–1** to take the gold medal at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games on October 3, 2026.

## From Manipur's Hills to Bhopal's Astroturf

Nilakanta Sharma was born in Imphal East, Manipur, in May 1995. He grew up in a state with a passionate hockey culture and early coaching roots, but the path to international hockey ran through facilities that Manipur alone could not offer a teenager. In **2011**, at around sixteen, he moved to the **Bhopal Hockey Academy** in Madhya Pradesh — a residential programme run by the state that has produced a pipeline of national-level players over two decades.

He has been associated with Bhopal since. The Free Press Journal's accounting of MP's eight-medal haul at Aichi-Nagoya includes Nilakanta among the contingent that the state could legitimately call its own — the kind of athlete who came as a teenager, built his game at a state-funded facility, and went on to represent India at the highest levels.

His international record bears that out. He was part of the Indian team that won **Olympic bronze at Tokyo 2020** — India's first Olympic hockey medal in 41 years. At the **Hangzhou Asian Games in 2023**, he was in the squad that won gold, making Aichi-Nagoya his second Asian Games gold medal. He received the **Arjuna Award in 2021**. His trophy shelf also includes multiple Asian Champions Trophy titles.

None of it came easily. Nilakanta plays the midfield — the zone that determines tempo without getting much of the credit for goals. His game is built on positioning, interceptions, and the kind of defensive work that shows up in the team's shape rather than the scoresheet. The September 1 semifinal, where India absorbed a Pakistan attack that drew level twice before India pulled clear, was exactly the kind of test a midfield anchor earns his place in.

## A Medal with a Bhopal Postcode

For Madhya Pradesh, the Aichi-Nagoya Games produced eight medals across disciplines — a haul the state's Directorate of Sports and Youth Welfare will point to when making the case for continued investment in residential academies. Nilakanta's gold sits inside that count, a reminder that a teenager who left Imphal for Bhopal in 2011 and stayed through the grind of domestic and international hockey can still come back with a continental championship medal around his neck.

The gold is India's. The roots of the player who helped win it are, at least in part, Madhya Pradesh's.
