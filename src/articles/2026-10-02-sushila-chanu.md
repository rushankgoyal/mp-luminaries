For nineteen years Sushila Chanu Pukhrambam has anchored the Indian women's hockey defence. On October 2, 2026, at Gifu Prefectural Green Stadium in Nagoya, she added the title her generation had never won: Asian Games gold.

India beat China **1-0** in the final, ending a 44-year absence from the top of the podium since the country's last gold at the 1982 Asian Games in New Delhi. Lalremsiami's 10th-minute strike gave India the lead; goalkeeper Savita Punia's penalty stroke save in the second quarter kept it. India's defence — with Sushila Chanu at its spine — held firm for sixty minutes against one of Asia's most clinical sides. The victory also secures India a direct qualification slot for the **Los Angeles 2028 Olympics**.

Sushila Chanu was not born in Madhya Pradesh. She grew up in Kakching, Manipur, in a state synonymous with producing hockey talent. But it was the Madhya Pradesh Women's Hockey Academy in Gwalior that shaped her into an international player. She joined the Academy in 2007, at roughly fourteen, and has spent more of her adult life in Gwalior than anywhere else.

## More Than 250 Matches at the Highest Level

The numbers are straightforward. Sushila Chanu has earned **more than 250 senior international appearances** for India — a milestone reached during the FIH Pro League 2024–25 season. She plays as a halfback and central defender, the position that demands positional reliability over individual highlight-reel moments. Her **Arjuna Award in 2023** recognised that kind of sustained, essential service: years of showing up and holding the line.

In that span, India won the 2022 Commonwealth Games bronze, reached the Paris 2024 Olympic quarterfinals, and captured the 2025 FIH Nations Cup. What was missing was a continental title. The squad arrived in Aichi-Nagoya carrying four decades of that absence.

The group stage offered encouragement — a 17-1 hammering of Indonesia among the results. The knockout matches were tighter. In the final, China, which had beaten India in the 2025 Women's Hockey Asia Cup, pressed hard in the second quarter and earned a penalty stroke opportunity. Savita stopped it. From that moment, India controlled the game.

When the final horn sounded, the squad gathered in a huddle on the Gifu turf. Sushila Chanu was among the first to embrace teammates. For a defender who has spent her career protecting goals in the most unshowy way possible, it was a fitting finish.

For Madhya Pradesh, the result is a direct return on two decades of investment in its Hockey Academy programme. Three women from that programme — Sushila Chanu, Bichu Devi Kharibam and Ishika Chaudhary — were in the gold-medal squad. The Gwalior system has always pointed to outcomes like this one. On October 2, 2026, it had its clearest proof yet.
