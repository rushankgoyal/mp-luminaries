On the morning of August 28, the Mandakini had already risen two metres above its danger mark. Within hours, the river — which rises in the Vindhya hills, passes through the Chitrakoot pilgrimage town and flows into Uttar Pradesh — surged past every recorded high of the previous two decades. The catchment area, Satna Collector Sathish Kumar S told PTI as the situation was still developing, had received 10 centimetres of rain in just three hours.

The five-day flood that followed was the worst the Mandakini had delivered in 23 years.

Across the Satna district stretch of the Chitrakoot flood zone, more than 50 villages were inundated. Roads into low-lying hamlets disappeared under water. Residents were stranded on rooftops and in upper floors. The district administration's response drew in multiple agencies at once: Indian Air Force helicopters airlifted families from cut-off locations; National Disaster Response Force teams deployed boats into flooded streets; State Disaster Response Force personnel coordinated ground evacuations; and district staff managed the logistics of running relief camps on higher ground.

When the Mandakini level finally began dropping on September 2, the count stood at **at least 1,438 people evacuated**.

## A Doctor Turned Collector

Before he cleared the civil services examination, Sathish Kumar S completed an MBBS from The Tamil Nadu Dr MGR Medical University — one of a small number of practising-doctor-turned-IAS officers in the country. He joined the **2013 batch** of the Indian Administrative Service under the **Madhya Pradesh cadre**, and has served the state since.

He took charge as **Satna Collector on January 31, 2025**. Satna district encompasses the Chitrakoot pilgrimage belt, the historic fort town, and a broad agricultural hinterland in eastern Madhya Pradesh. It is not typically associated with the scale of flooding the Mandakini delivered this year.

His posting drew notice even before the floods. Local reporting documented his practice of cycling to the collectorate rather than arriving in the official convoy — an administrative gesture that became a small emblem of his approach.

The August–September flood required something more demanding: five days of sustained coordination across dozens of cut-off villages, continuous communication with field teams, IAF helicopter scheduling, and NDRF wave-pattern management, all simultaneously. The scale of what the river delivered — breaking a 23-year-old record — meant there was no playbook for this exact situation.

For Madhya Pradesh's Chitrakoot region, better known for its place in the Ramayana than for emergency management, this was an extreme and rare event. The 1,400-plus people who reached safety during those five days got there because a district administration was prepared to deploy every available resource and did so.

Sathish Kumar S, now in his second year as Satna Collector, was the officer whose coordination made that response work.
