On September 11, at the Yaatri Parivahan Vision Summit inside Indore's Brilliant Convention Centre, Madhya Pradesh Transport and School Education Minister Uday Pratap Singh announced a date that many residents of the state had been waiting to hear: September 25, when the Mukhyamantri Sugam Parivahan Bus Seva will roll out and state-backed public buses will return to MP's roads for the first time in 21 years.

That gap opened in 2005, when the Madhya Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation wound down operations under the weight of accumulated financial losses and could not sustain its fleet. For two full decades since, commuters across the state's towns, small cities, and villages have been left to piece together travel from private operators, overloaded tempos, and improvised shared transport arrangements — often unreliable, rarely safe, and entirely absent from many rural areas. Women travelling alone, the elderly, and students sitting hours from the nearest college have felt that absence most sharply.

> "The new service will focus on safe, accessible and technology-enabled transport and help connect rural areas with cities," Singh said at the summit.

The ministry spent the preceding months doing the foundational work: route surveys across all **55 districts** of the state, mapping demand, identifying unserved corridors, and building the administrative architecture through Madhya Pradesh Yatri Parivahan aur Infrastructure Limited (MPYPIL), the public-private partnership company that will manage the service.

## A Network of More Than 1,000 Routes and 15,000 Buses

The blueprint is substantial. The initial rollout covers approximately **150 city routes**, **450 inter-city routes**, and **436 inter-state routes** — over 1,000 routes in total, spread across a state with roughly five lakh kilometres of road. Singh's ministry has mapped scope for operating up to **15,000 buses** across that network as the service grows.

Every bus will carry CCTV cameras, GPS trackers, panic buttons, and real-time monitoring systems. The fleet will prioritise electric and CNG vehicles from the outset, making MP's returning bus service also a step toward cleaner transport. The two-day summit also launched the MP Mobility Innovation Challenge 2026, inviting researchers, academic institutions, and private innovators to contribute solutions for smart ticketing and AI-based fleet management.

Singh, who represented Narmadapuram in the Lok Sabha for three consecutive terms before joining the state cabinet, has a constituency-level grasp of the problem he is now solving. In the Gadarwara area he represents in the assembly, as in hundreds of similar towns across MP, daily connectivity to the nearest city is not an abstraction but a practical difficulty for thousands of families.

The return of public buses to Madhya Pradesh — backed by technology, built on partnership, and aimed at communities that have had no reliable alternative for two decades — is the most direct infrastructure commitment the Mohan Yadav government has made to ordinary commuters. The September 25 launch will test whether the architecture Singh has assembled can be turned into a service that actually runs on time.
