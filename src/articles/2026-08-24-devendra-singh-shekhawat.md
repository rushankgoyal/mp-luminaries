On the morning of August 21, 2026, Madhya Pradesh Chief Minister Dr. Mohan Yadav and Union Minister Gajendra Singh Shekhawat joined Pratap Group Chairman and Managing Director Devendra Singh Shekhawat at the Smart Industrial Park in Pithampur to inaugurate two factories that represent a **₹850-crore** bet on India's digital and clean-energy future. The ribbon-cutting was not a ceremony in name only: both plants had already begun production, and orders for their output were in hand from the day the gates opened.

Pithampur, the industrial hub on the edge of Dhar district near Indore, has hosted vehicle and pharmaceutical manufacturers for decades. These two units add something different — high-technology manufacturing aligned with the demands of 5G buildouts and the country's accelerating push toward grid-scale battery storage.

## Two Plants, Two Sectors, One Group

The first facility, operating under the brand **Corsis Industries**, manufactures advanced optical fibre cable. With an annual production capacity of approximately **6.5 million fibre-kilometres** and an in-house R&D centre, Corsis Industries is designed to supply the fibre backbone for broadband networks, FTTH last-mile connections, 5G tower links, data centres, smart-city systems and defence communications. The plant uses technology developed in partnership with German engineering firms, making it one of the few such collaborations at this scale in central India.

The second facility produces **Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)** and lithium-ion battery packs under the Voltters Technologies brand. As India adds solar and wind capacity, the gap between generation and reliable round-the-clock supply increasingly falls to battery storage — Voltters Technologies is positioning itself in that gap, manufacturing cells and integrated storage systems for industrial and renewable-energy clients.

Together the two units are expected to generate more than **3,500 direct and indirect jobs**, with the group already running skill-development centres that have trained over 2,000 young people from the region. The total proposed investment in Pithampur now exceeds ₹1,140 crore.

Pratap Group, built on foundations laid by the late Thakur Pratap Singh, spans multiple industries and today directly and indirectly employs around **30,000 people** across its ventures. Under Devendra Singh Shekhawat's leadership the group has moved deliberately into sunrise sectors — first technology infrastructure, now energy storage — without stepping away from the communities in Madhya Pradesh where it has long operated.

## Why This Investment Matters to MP

Madhya Pradesh has worked hard to attract manufacturing capital in recent years. The Pithampur inauguration is a marker that the effort is producing compound results: an established MP business group choosing to expand within the state, rather than chasing incentives elsewhere, and doing so in sectors — digital infrastructure and clean energy — that the national government considers strategic.

For Pithampur, the arrival of a domestic optical fibre manufacturer with German-grade equipment and a battery storage plant puts the industrial park in a conversation it was not part of five years ago. For Madhya Pradesh's wider economy, every 3,500-job announcement in a high-technology sector is a step toward the kind of industrial diversification the state has been seeking since the days when its name was synonymous only with agriculture and mining.
