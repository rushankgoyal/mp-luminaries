On 16 September 2026, NITI Aayog published the India Electric Mobility Index 2025 — its annual scorecard ranking every Indian state on how far and how fast it is moving toward electric transport. Madhya Pradesh appeared at number seven. One edition earlier, it had ranked twenty-third. No other state improved by more.

The minister who holds the energy portfolio in Bhopal is Pradhuman Singh Tomar. He has held that position since December 2023, and the policy architecture that produced this result was assembled on his watch.

## From Last Tier to Frontrunner: How the Shift Happened

The IEMI scores states across sixteen indicators grouped into three themes: Transport Electrification accounts for fifty per cent of the total score, Charging Infrastructure for thirty per cent, and EV Research and Innovation for twenty per cent. A state moving from 23rd to 7th in this index has to improve across most of those dimensions simultaneously — no single intervention produces a sixteen-place climb.

Madhya Pradesh's jump traces to a package of fiscal and regulatory decisions that Tomar's energy ministry pushed through from late 2023 onward. The state now offers **100 per cent road tax exemption** on electric vehicles and charges **zero registration fees** — removing two of the costs that most often deter first-time EV buyers. On top of that, the state disburses direct subsidies: **₹20,000 for two-wheelers** and **₹50,000 for three-wheelers**, amounts that move purchase decisions for buyers in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities where average incomes make the upfront EV premium meaningful.

The results are visible in the registration data. EV registrations in Madhya Pradesh grew by **150 per cent** in the period the index covers. That growth rate places the state among a small group — Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Uttar Pradesh — that NITI Aayog now categorises as frontrunners, rather than the aspirer or laggard tiers where MP had previously sat.

At the city level, Indore has taken the most visible step. The city deployed a fleet of **200 electric buses**, the kind of large-scale public-transit commitment that moves the Transport Electrification score more than any number of individual vehicle registrations. The Indore bus fleet reduces diesel consumption across one of Madhya Pradesh's most congested urban corridors, and it gives commuters regular contact with electric transport in a way that accelerates the broader cultural shift toward EV adoption.

Madhya Pradesh has long been a coal-producing state. The electricity sector is central to its economy, and its relationship with fossil energy is structural rather than incidental. A state-level energy minister who pursues EV adoption is, in that context, doing something that goes beyond transport policy: it is a signal that the state is prepared to diversify its economic identity alongside its energy mix. For investors, manufacturers and logistics companies making long-horizon location decisions, a 7th-place NITI Aayog ranking in electric mobility sends a clearer message than any promotional pitch.

The 2025 index will be read against the 2026 edition when that is published. Maintaining a frontrunner position is harder than reaching it. But for a state that was not in the conversation two years ago, 7th place is a number that changes the conversation entirely.
