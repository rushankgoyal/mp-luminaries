When the BCCI announced the six squads for the 2026-27 Duleep Trophy, the name Arshad Khan appeared under Central Zone — and ESPNcricinfo published a feature on him the same week: *Arshad Khan wants to stamp his authority in red-ball cricket too.* The "too" is doing real work. In white-ball cricket, Arshad had already stamped enough to force selectors to think seriously about him across formats. The Duleep Trophy call-up, to join a squad captained by Rajat Patidar and defending champions from the previous season, confirms the thinking is turning into action.

Arshad Khan made his first-class debut barely ten months before this selection and had played just five Ranji Trophy matches before the Duleep Trophy squads were announced. For a left-arm pacer to reach the sport's most prestigious domestic red-ball tournament on such a slim first-class record speaks entirely to what he has done in the white-ball game.

## The Figures That Rewrote a Record Book

In the 2024-25 Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy, playing for Madhya Pradesh against Chandigarh, Arshad Khan bowled four overs — one of them a maiden — and returned figures of **6 wickets for 9 runs**. The previous best bowling performance in the tournament's entire history was 6 for 13. Arshad did not merely break the record; he reset it to a level that may stand for decades. In the same SMAT campaign he also won a Player of the Match award against Maharashtra, contributing **30 not out off 17 balls** with the bat alongside two wickets — the kind of all-round impact that gave selectors a second reason to watch him.

That tournament performance earned him a place in India A's squad for the Sri Lanka A Tri-Series in 2026, a first exposure to international-level preparation. He used it to demonstrate that his SMAT figures were not a one-match accident against modest opposition.

Central Zone enter the 2026-27 edition having pre-qualified for the semi-finals as defending champions. The squad Rajat Patidar leads carries the weight of expectation that comes with a title to defend; Arshad enters it as a pace option with a style — left-arm swing, tight lines, a natural ability to angle the ball across right-handers — that produces a different set of problems from the right-arm seamers around him.

For Madhya Pradesh cricket, Arshad's Duleep Trophy inclusion arrives as the latest evidence that the state's domestic pipeline is running deep. MP has produced Ranji Trophy champions, India internationals and, now, a holder of white-ball records who is beginning to make the case in red-ball cricket too. The tournament runs through to September 10. The semi-finals await Central Zone. Arshad Khan will want to be the reason they go further.
