On the opening day of the 2026 Asian Para Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Kapil Parmar made it look easy. The judoka from Sehore, Madhya Pradesh, stepped onto the tatami on Monday morning and put Indonesia's Rafli Ahnaf Shidqi on the mat with a clean ippon throw in **69 seconds** — winning bronze and extending India's para-judo medal run to a third consecutive major tournament.

Earlier in the day, Parmar had reached the semifinal but lost 0-1 to Indonesia's Junaedi. The bronze-medal bout was different. He entered it with purpose and finished it before the first minute was up.

Parmar competes in the J1 class, for athletes with visual impairment. He has lost **90 per cent of his sight**. The loss dates to his childhood in Sehore, where a severe electric shock left him in a coma for about six months. When he regained consciousness, the world was largely shadows and outlines. He was, by most measures, far from the track that leads to an international sporting career.

## From Sehore's Streets to the Nagoya Tatami

Parmar grew up in Sehore helping his father at a tea shop. Sport interested him, and when he was introduced to para-judo in **2017** — nearly a decade after the accident — he found an environment that matched both his physicality and his focus.

He joined coach Munawar Anzar Ali Siddiqui at the Indian Para Judo Academy in Lucknow. Results came steadily. He won gold at the **2019 Commonwealth Championships**, bronze at the **2023 World Games** in Birmingham, and silver at the **2022 Hangzhou Asian Para Games**. Then came the biggest stage: the **Paris 2024 Paralympics**, where he won bronze in the men's J1 -60 kg event and became the **first Indian to win a Paralympic judo medal** in the nation's history. He is currently ranked **eighth in the world** and is supported under the Target Olympic Podium Scheme (TOPS).

Monday's bronze in Nagoya is his second Asian Para Games medal and the third major international medal of his career. He entered these Games with hopes of gold, having fought his way through one of the more competitive J1 -70 kg brackets in recent memory.

For Madhya Pradesh, the result matters beyond the podium. Para-sport infrastructure in the state is thin — most of its serious competitors, including Parmar, train elsewhere. What the state has produced, year after year, are athletes willing to travel the distance: from Sehore to Lucknow to Paris, and now to Nagoya. Kapil Parmar is 26 years old and still building toward Los Angeles 2028. The pattern of results suggests the bronze in Japan will not be the last.
