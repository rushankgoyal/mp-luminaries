The M3M Hurun India Rich List 2026, released on September 23, placed Vinod Agrawal of Indore at the head of Madhya Pradesh's wealthiest — a net worth of **₹9,500 crore** built across more than four decades in the coal trade. His company, Agrawal Coal Corporation, now handles operations at **more than 20 ports** across India and generates annual revenues exceeding **₹14,000 crore**, making it the country's largest coal importer by volume.

The recognition caps a trajectory that began far from any port or boardroom.

## From a 500-Square-Foot Home to 20 Ports

The Agrawal family migrated to Indore after Partition in 1947 and established the coal trading business in 1966. Vinod Agrawal grew up in a modest 500-square-foot home with eight siblings and joined the family enterprise in his teens. What started as a local coal distribution firm gradually built relationships with steel plants, power utilities, and industrial consumers that domestic supply alone could not meet.

As India's industrial appetite outpaced Coal India's production through the 1990s and 2000s, Agrawal Coal Corporation positioned itself to fill the gap through imports. The company developed berths and logistics infrastructure at ports in Mundra, Vizag, Paradip, and elsewhere — a footprint that gave it the scale to negotiate directly with Indonesian, Australian, and South African suppliers. Today it handles both thermal coal for power generation and coking coal for steel, serving clients whose combined demand runs into tens of millions of tonnes annually.

Vinod Agrawal has kept the company private and Indore-headquartered throughout its growth. The city is not a natural base for a maritime commodity business, but it places the firm at the logistical centre of a hinterland where the steel foundries, textile mills, and sponge-iron plants of Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Chhattisgarh all converge.

## MP's Philanthropic Anchor

The Hurun India Rich List is not the first time Agrawal has appeared in a national ranking for something beyond business. He was recognised as **Madhya Pradesh's top philanthropist** on the Hurun India Philanthropy List, with charitable giving that has supported education and healthcare in the Malwa region around Indore.

That combination — a business built on unglamorous but essential supply-chain infrastructure, sustained over decades from a single city, combined with visible local giving — is what distinguishes Agrawal from the category of wealth that accumulates in financial centres and parks itself in global assets.

## Why Indore, Why Now

There is something worth noting in the timing. Indore has, in recent years, become the shorthand for a resurgent MP business story: India's cleanest city seven years running, a growing startup ecosystem, and an investor-conference circuit that the state government has leaned into heavily. Agrawal Coal Corporation predates all of that branding by decades, but its persistence in the city adds texture to the narrative.

At ₹9,500 crore, Vinod Agrawal sits well outside the circle of names that dominate India's financial press. But for a landlocked state with no seaport, producing the man who imports more coal than anyone else in the country is a particular kind of achievement — and one that arrived long before anyone thought to celebrate it.
