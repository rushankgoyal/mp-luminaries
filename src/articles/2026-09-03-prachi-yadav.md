Prachi Yadav cannot use her legs. In a kayak, this is not a disadvantage she overcomes; it is simply the condition in which she learned to paddle, first in a Gwalior swimming pool as an eight-year-old, and then across two Paralympics, a World Championship final and the podium of the Asian Para Games. By the time she reached the gold-medal ceremony at Hangzhou in 2023, she was paddling faster than every other woman in her class in Asia.

She grew up in Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh. Her father Jagdish Singh Yadav worked as a Deputy Director in the Agriculture Department. Her mother died of cancer in 2003, when Prachi was eight years old. Aqua therapy — water exercise prescribed for children with disabilities — was how she first came to a pool, and in it she found something she was good at. She became a national champion para-swimmer. But a coach told her that her upper-body strength pointed toward a different boat entirely: the sprint canoe.

## From National Champion to World Medallist

She began training in para canoe in 2018 under senior national coach Mayank Thakur, working out of the facilities run by the Madhya Pradesh sports department. Progress was rapid. In **2020**, she became the **first Indian para canoeist ever to qualify for the Paralympics** — a milestone that arrived before most of the country had heard the word paracanoe. She competed at Tokyo and returned to train harder.

At the **2023 Asian Para Games in Hangzhou**, she won **gold in the KL2 event** and silver in VL2 — the first Indian to win a para canoe gold medal at the Asian Para Games. The Indian government recognised her with an **Arjuna Award** that year, para sport's highest national honour.

She qualified for the **Paris 2024 Paralympics**, reached the final, and finished eighth — 10 seconds from the winner, but a final in the world's biggest sporting event. After Paris, she reset her goal.

> "Earlier, I was happy to aim for a bronze. Now, it's gold or nothing."

In 2025, competing at the **ICF Canoe Sprint World Championships**, she finished **third in the Women's KL2 event** — a world bronze, placing India on the para canoe podium for the first time at a world championship. She trains now under coach Pijush Kanti Baroi at the State Kayaking Canoeing Academy.

The **2026 Asian Para Games** in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan open on **18 October** — six weeks away. They are the next chance for Prachi to defend the continental title she won in Hangzhou. As India's most decorated paracanoeist and the reigning KL2 champion, she arrives as the gold-standard benchmark other nations must beat.

For Madhya Pradesh and for Indian disability sport, she represents something straightforward: a woman from a state sports programme who turned aqua therapy into a world medal through discipline, a strong upper body, and the refusal to be satisfied with eighth.
