When Dr. Archana Shukla walks through the campus of Government CM Rise Mahatma Gandhi Sandipani Higher Secondary School in Bhopal's BHEL township, she is walking through fifteen years of accumulated classroom effort made visible. Wooden boxes sit wedged in tree branches — sparrow houses her students built and placed, one project cycle at a time. Small tags on tree trunks carry QR codes she designed, linking any scanning phone to a digital profile of that specific tree. On 5 September 2026, President Droupadi Murmu recognised what that campus represents: a National Teachers' Award, presented at Rashtrapati Bhavan on Teachers' Day to 82 educators across the country. Six of those awards went to teachers from Madhya Pradesh. Shukla was one of them.

She teaches science and biology. She has done so for fifteen years at the same government school in Bhopal. But what she has built extends well past the classroom walls.

## A School Campus as a Working Conservation Zone

The sparrow project grew from a specific scientific problem her biology curriculum kept raising: the house sparrow, common across Indian cities for centuries, has been disappearing from urban environments as old buildings, open grounds and traditional nesting spots give way to construction. Shukla turned that documented decline into an active class assignment. Students did not just study sparrow ecology — they built nesting boxes and placed them in trees and homes in the school's surrounding neighbourhood. The programme has now placed **over 1,000 sparrow houses** in total, a number that extends significantly beyond the campus itself.

The QR-code tree-mapping project gives the campus its second layer. Every tree has been profiled and tagged — species, age, location — and that data is accessible to anyone with a phone. The system turns an ordinary school garden into a live database that students can expand as they observe and record. New students inherit a working dataset that previous classes built.

The Plant Emotion Project takes the most conceptually ambitious approach of the three: it uses simple equipment and sensors to make plant responses observable in the classroom, treating plants not as diagrams in a textbook but as organisms actively reacting to light, touch and water. The effect is a shift in how students relate to the biological world — from memorising facts about plants to watching them respond.

ETV Bharat reported that Shukla has guided students who spent their mornings doing manual labour before arriving at school, and helped them prepare for the competitive examinations that lead to IIT and AIIMS admissions. That combination — environmental-science innovation and measurable student advancement — is what the National Teachers' Award selection process looks for.

The 2026 batch of six Madhya Pradesh recipients — also including Reena Gupta, Sangeeta Yadav, Sourav Datta, Jyotibala Rathore and Shailendra Pratap Singh — came from different schools and disciplines. Shukla's profile is specifically urban: a science teacher at a large government school in a public-sector township, building conservation infrastructure through class time over the course of a decade and a half. The school is there for any family in the BHEL area whose children attend a government institution. The sparrow houses, QR trees and plant-emotion apparatus are all accessible without any entrance exam. That is the point.
