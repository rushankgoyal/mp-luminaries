When Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan called the representatives of India's best-performing districts to the stage at the National Agricultural Science Complex in New Delhi on 12 October 2026, a small, forested district in eastern Madhya Pradesh claimed an unlikely spot on the podium. **Umaria ranked third** among 100 districts in the first-year review of the PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana — a scheme designed for exactly the kind of district Umaria had long been: low agricultural productivity, limited institutional credit, and cropping intensity that left fields fallow far too often.

The collector who shaped that outcome is Rakhi Sahay. She arrived in Umaria as its IAS collector in April 2026, joining an administration already six months into the scheme's first cycle. The district she took charge of sits in the Vindhya range near Bandhavgarh — better known to tourists than to agricultural planners. Its farming households are largely tribal, its fields mostly paddy and minor millets, and its access to formal banking historically thin.

The PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana offered districts in that position a focused intervention. Thirty-six existing central programmes — crop insurance, soil health cards, irrigation subsidies, the Kisan Credit Card, post-harvest storage — were converged into a single district-level implementation plan and tracked through a public monthly dashboard measuring **121 performance indicators**. Every district's score is visible to everyone, updated regularly, and creates a straightforward competitive record.

## The Numbers Behind the Third-Place Finish

The scheme covers the **100 districts** across India with the lowest combined scores in agricultural productivity, cropping intensity, and credit disbursement to farmers. When the first annual review was conducted, national average performance across the six core objectives had risen from **18.85 points in April 2026 to 46.89 in August** — a sign that the convergence model was moving the needle. Umaria's overall score placed it third nationally, after Gaya district in Bihar and Nagaur in Rajasthan.

Eighteen additional districts were recognised for outstanding performance in individual objectives — covering agricultural productivity, crop diversification, post-harvest storage, irrigation, credit access, and governance. Umaria's third-place overall finish placed it above all of them in the combined ranking.

Presenting the recognition, Agriculture Minister Chouhan noted that the scheme's first year had been about establishing the foundations — getting the 36 programmes to work together at the district level rather than arriving as separate government pipelines.

> "The first year was about setting strong foundations. The coming year must prioritise improving agricultural productivity, cropping intensity and access to agricultural credit," Chouhan said at the event.

The ministry also announced the Khet Bachao Abhiyan, a campaign to promote soil health through balanced fertiliser use, running from 22 October to 30 November 2026.

## A Different Kind of Recognition for an Overlooked District

Umaria is among Madhya Pradesh's smaller districts. It rarely appears on lists of agricultural achievers, and its name is more likely to come up in conversations about tiger conservation than about food security policy. A national third-place finish in a ranking this size — across a field including districts from Punjab, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, and Rajasthan — is a different order of attention altogether.

For Rakhi Sahay, the recognition belongs to the village-level officials, agriculture extension workers and farmers who engaged with the scheme's programmes month after month. The district collector is the face at the ceremony; the score is built in the fields.
