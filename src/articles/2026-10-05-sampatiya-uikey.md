For families in Mandla's forested villages, mornings have long begun before sunrise — walking to seasonal handpumps, waiting at a shared tap, hauling clay pots back along dirt paths. On October 5, 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi stood in Jabalpur and laid the foundation stone of **₹2,350 crore** worth of Jal Jeevan Mission water supply projects covering Mandla, Jabalpur, and Dindori districts. The minister whose sustained effort at the negotiating table made that investment possible: Sampatiya Uikey, Madhya Pradesh's Public Health Engineering (PHED) Minister.

The water supply component was part of a larger set of launches in Jabalpur totalling over **₹12,600 crore** across roads, railways, and civic infrastructure. But the Jal Jeevan Mission projects stand apart for their reach into three of MP's most historically underserved districts — Mandla and Dindori among them, home to large tribal communities where piped water remains a novelty in many gram panchayats.

## From Signed Agreement to Foundation Stone

The October 5 event marks the visible end of a longer institutional process. Uikey signed a formal MoU between the Madhya Pradesh government and the Union Ministry of Jal Shakti to formalise the state's entry into Jal Jeevan Mission Phase 2. The agreement locked in central funding tranches tied to measurable delivery milestones — overhead tanks constructed, pipelines laid, household tap connections activated.

Under CM Mohan Yadav, the state set a firm deadline of December 2028 to complete JJM works across Madhya Pradesh. Uikey has been the cabinet face of that commitment: coordinating with district administrations, resolving contractor bottlenecks, and making the case in New Delhi for allocations to districts that lack the political visibility of urban constituencies.

The ₹2,350 crore now sanctioned for the three districts will fund distribution pipelines, water treatment units, storage tanks, and what the programme calls Functional Household Tap Connections — each one representing a family, typically a woman, who no longer needs to begin her day fetching water from far away.

## Why This Matters for MP's Tribal Districts

India's Jal Jeevan Mission set a national target of universal rural piped water by 2024. Progress was slower in states with difficult terrain and dispersed populations — and Madhya Pradesh, with its wide tribal belt and geography that ranges from Vindhyan plateaus to Satpura forests, was among those that required additional cycles of infrastructure work.

Mandla and Dindori are districts where routine government announcements often dissipate before reaching the last village. The foundation stones laid today are not yet taps that run. But they are **₹2,350 crore** committed, with a minister whose department signs the progress reports and who stood next to the Prime Minister when the work was formally begun. For the families in the forest, that proximity to power — and to accountability — is something new.
