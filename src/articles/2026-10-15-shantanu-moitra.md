When Shantanu Moitra walked onto the stage of the Lata Mangeshkar Auditorium in Indore on 28 September 2026, he was standing in the birthplace of the singer whose name the award he was about to receive carries. The Madhya Pradesh government conferred its National Lata Mangeshkar Award for 2026 on the composer whose melodies have quietly scored much of the last two decades of Hindi cinema.

Moitra, born in Lucknow in 1968, found his footing writing advertising jingles before moving into films. His breakthrough came with two films released in 2005: *Parineeta*, whose compositions revived the flavour of vintage Bengali melody in Hindi film, and *Hazaaron Khwaishein Aisi*, which matched music to political urgency. He followed with the instantly recognisable soundtrack to *Lage Raho Munnabhai* (2006) and the record-breaking score for *3 Idiots* (2009). Over the years he has worked across genres — the sparse sound design of *Madras Cafe* (2013), the weight of *Sardar Udham* (2021), and the understated score for *12th Fail* (2023), about an aspirant from rural Madhya Pradesh who clears the civil services exam against every odd.

## An Award Forty Years in the Making

The National Lata Mangeshkar Award was instituted by the Madhya Pradesh government in 1984 to honour excellence in Sugam Sangeet — Hindi light music. It carries a tax-free cash prize of **₹5 lakh** and a citation. The award is conferred alternately for music composition and for singing; earlier recipients include Naushad, Kishore Kumar, and Asha Bhosle — names that defined the sound of Indian popular music across the twentieth century.

Water Resources Minister Tulsiram Silawat and Indore MP Shankar Lalwani presented the award at the ceremony. Chief Minister Mohan Yadav joined via video conference. Moitra described receiving the honour in Mangeshkar's birthplace, on her birthday, as an emotional moment. He recalled working with Mangeshkar and said she always arrived to a recording session having prepared every note, and she sang with a seriousness that felt like prayer.

What makes Moitra's recognition fitting is the breadth of what he has composed. He has not chased commercial formulas — he has written music for films that few expected to work and that then stayed with audiences for years. The soundtrack for *12th Fail*, in particular, resonated across Madhya Pradesh: it tells the story of Manoj Kumar Sharma from Morena, a district in the state, and Moitra's score gave that story the emotional texture it needed. In honouring him, Madhya Pradesh is acknowledging a composer who, for two decades, has found poetry in ordinary Indian lives.
