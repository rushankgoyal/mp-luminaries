The announcement on 6 August 2026 was straightforward in its numbers: **102 students** from Madhya Pradesh's schools, colleges and universities would each receive **Rs 1 lakh** under a new state merit scholarship, the Bhagwan Shri Krishna Medhavi Chhatravritti Yojana. Behind that figure is Uday Pratap Singh, the state's Cabinet Minister for School Education and Transport, who has made student welfare and education modernisation the visible priorities of his tenure.

Singh has represented Gadarwara in the Madhya Pradesh assembly since the 2023 elections, and before that served three consecutive terms as a Member of Parliament from Narmadapuram. He assumed the School Education and Transport portfolio in December 2023, within weeks of the Mohan Yadav government taking office. The Medhavi Chhatravritti Yojana is his most direct investment in rewarding student achievement at scale.

The scheme's name and its internal architecture both reach for something beyond a standard merit award. Lord Krishna, in classical tradition, is associated with **14 Vidyas** — branches of learning that span grammar, logic, medicine, astronomy and law — and **64 Kalas**, the arts, ranging from music and dance to storytelling, archery and metalwork. The scholarship asks recipients to draw inspiration from that breadth: academic excellence is the entry point, but the scheme frames it within an older idea of what a well-rounded mind should contain.

## The Stakes for a Government-School Student

Recipients come from all levels — secondary school to postgraduate university. For a student at a government school or college in Seoni or Betul, a scholarship of Rs 1 lakh is not a symbolic gesture; it is a meaningful financial contribution to the cost of continuing education. Tuition, study materials, coaching and travel to competitive examinations all carry real costs, and merit scholarships at this level can be the difference between a student who persists and one who does not.

Madhya Pradesh has, over the past several years, steadily built out the infrastructure beneath these students. SWAYAM online courses are mandatory across the state's universities. Mission Buniyad, launched earlier this year, is bringing AI-assisted teaching tools into 500 government schools. Both initiatives address inputs: what students are given to learn with, and how. The Medhavi Chhatravritti Yojana addresses outputs: it looks at what students have already achieved and rewards it.

The National Education Policy 2020 explicitly calls for reconnecting India's classrooms with the country's classical knowledge traditions — not as nostalgia but as a resource. Singh's scholarship scheme puts money behind that principle. A student who wins it receives Rs 1 lakh and a formal acknowledgement from the state that their work was seen. In a system where recognition for government-school students is rare, that acknowledgement matters almost as much as the amount.
