On July 2, 2026, at the 29th National Conference on e-Governance in Jaipur, Union Minister of State Dr. Jitendra Singh presented the National e-Governance Silver Award for Cyber Security to Commissioner Sanket Bhondve of Madhya Pradesh's Urban Development and Housing Department. The award recognised something specific: the state's e-Nagar Palika portal, rebuilt using a "Security by Design" approach, now serves residents across **413 urban local bodies** — from Bhopal, Indore, and Jabalpur down to the smallest municipal councils in the state's eastern districts — all on a platform designed to be safe by architecture, not just by policy.

The e-Nagar Palika is MP's central digital window for urban civic services. Through it, a resident in Sagar pays their property tax; a Rewa household applies for a building permit; a Shahdol water consumer checks their bill and raises a complaint online. At present the portal operates **more than 20 modules** covering the full range of municipal services, with **24 citizen-facing services** available entirely online. All of this runs on a backend the department has systematically reinforced.

## Zero Trust Across 413 Cities

The architecture Commissioner Bhondve's team deployed rests on three pillars. **Zero Trust Architecture** verifies every user and device at every access point — no assumption that anything inside the network perimeter is automatically safe. **End-to-end encryption** protects data in transit across all transactions. And **GIS-based property management** ties citizen records to geographic data for accurate and auditable assessment. The system is integrated with the central government's **UMANG** platform and uses artificial intelligence for several service-delivery functions.

What made this work notable at the national level was scale. Implementing a consistent, verified cyber security framework across 413 separate bodies — each with its own staff, legacy data, service load, and operating conditions — is a substantially harder problem than securing a single large city portal. The department's work involved re-engineering processes at the municipal level across the entire state, not merely updating a central server.

Commissioner Bhondve has said the department has always given top priority to transparent, secure, and reliable digital services, describing the Silver Award as recognition of a continuing focus on risk management and data protection using global standards.

The National Awards for e-Governance are assessed annually by an expert panel against defined criteria. The 2026 edition selected **17 projects across seven categories**, drawn from state governments and central departments across India. A Silver Award in Cyber Security — presented at a national conference, evaluated against that field — is a reliable signal of where a state stands in the harder end of digital governance: not the ease of launching a portal, but the discipline of keeping it trustworthy at scale.

Madhya Pradesh's urban population has expanded considerably over the last decade, and digital service delivery has become the primary interface between most citizens and their local bodies. A city resident who needs a building certificate or a water connection does not go to the municipal counter first; they go online. An architecture that keeps that interface safe — across all 413 of MP's urban bodies simultaneously — is not a technical footnote. It is the foundation on which everything else the portal promises can actually be delivered.
