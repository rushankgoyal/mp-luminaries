On 12 October 2026, Chief Minister Mohan Yadav released eight wild water buffaloes into a 17-hectare enclosure inside the Supkhar range of Kanha Tiger Reserve — the first time the species had lived in Madhya Pradesh in nearly a century. The ecology, field operations and months of painstaking logistics that made this moment possible came largely from Dr. Sonali Ghosh, Field Director of Kaziranga National Park in Assam.

Dr. Ghosh, an IFS officer of the Uttarakhand cadre, became the first woman to lead Kaziranga when she took charge on 1 September 2023. The park is among Asia's most celebrated wildlife sanctuaries — home to the world's largest population of Indian one-horned rhinoceros, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and a last stronghold for several threatened species in northeast India. Taking charge of Kaziranga was itself a milestone for the Indian Forest Service. Organising a cross-country translocation of its rarest large herbivore became the defining achievement of her tenure so far.

## Capturing, Crating and Crossing Two Thousand Kilometres

The operation began in earnest between March 19 and April 10, 2026, when Dr. Ghosh's team moved through Kaziranga's Central and Eastern ranges to capture sub-adult wild water buffaloes. The field work required veterinary expertise from Dr. Bhaskar Choudhury and Dr. Saurabh Buragohain, with logistics coordinated by Deputy Director Arun Vignesh CS. Wild water buffaloes are the largest bovine species in South Asia — moving them safely across more than 2,000 kilometres of Indian territory demanded months of health monitoring, crating trials and interagency coordination between the Assam and Madhya Pradesh forest departments.

On April 28, 2026, the translocation was formally launched as a joint programme, marking the first time wild water buffaloes had moved from Assam to Madhya Pradesh in decades. The animals entered quarantine at a one-hectare holding enclosure in Kanha's Supkhar range and were monitored closely through the monsoon months. By October 12, the herd was cleared for release, with eight buffaloes stepping into a **439-hectare dedicated habitat** inside Kanha.

The wild water buffalo (*Bubalus arnee*) once ranged widely across the Indian subcontinent. Hunting and habitat loss eliminated the Madhya Pradesh population over the course of the twentieth century, leaving the species alive in significant numbers only in Kaziranga's seasonally flooded grasslands. Dr. Ghosh's project bridges a hundred years of absence: animals whose genetics reach back to those floodplain herds now graze in central India again.

Madhya Pradesh has set a target of **50 wild buffaloes** within three years. If the Kanha herd establishes itself — adapts to the habitat, breeds, and grows — it will represent one of the most complete wildlife restoration efforts in the state since Project Tiger returned the big cat to viable numbers. For Dr. Sonali Ghosh, it is one chapter in a career that began by bringing fresh leadership to a historic park and is now reshaping which species call central India home.
