The school in Dewas where Jay Meena grew up sat next to a soft tennis court. That proximity — not family tradition — is what drew him in. He had wanted to be a cricketer. But the boys on the adjacent court were doing something curious with soft rubber balls and rackets, and Meena walked over to watch. That detour, two decades later, has brought him to Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, for the 2026 Asian Games, where the soft tennis competition begins September 18 at Higashiyama Park Tennis Center.

Soft tennis is played with a rubber ball rather than the pressurised felt ball used in lawn tennis, and the sport's entire competitive infrastructure sits in East Asia — primarily Taiwan, Japan, and South Korea. India has participated in international soft tennis events for years without expecting to finish near the top. Jay Meena has been quietly changing what India can expect.

## From Schoolyard Curiosity to World Stage

By 14, Meena was representing India in international tournaments. He appeared at the 2018 Jakarta-Palembang Asian Games, gaining his first experience of the continental multi-sport platform he now returns to with a considerably stronger record.

In September 2024, at the World Soft Tennis Championships in Anseong, South Korea, Meena and his mixed doubles partner Aadhya Tiwari won a **bronze medal** — India's first-ever podium finish at a World Soft Tennis Championship. The pair defeated the Philippines in the Round of 16 and China in the quarterfinals. For a sport India has historically entered rather than expected to win, a World Championship medal was a landmark.

The 2025 domestic season extended his record further. Meena won the national championship in men's singles, becoming India's top-ranked player. He then won bronze in men's singles at the **2025 Asian Soft Tennis Championship** — making him the first Indian male to earn a podium at the Asian level in that category.

At Aichi-Nagoya, he enters three events: men's singles, men's team, and mixed doubles with Aadhya Tiwari. The competition runs through September 23. India has never won an Asian Games soft tennis medal, and Meena enters as the country's most credentialled player for the campaign.

Soft tennis seldom appears in Indian sports headlines. The athletes who play it typically do so alongside day jobs, without large federations behind them and without the media attention that follows cricket, wrestling, or even the better-known Olympic racket sports. Meena has built his record largely outside that visibility.

The sport he stumbled into on a schoolyard in Dewas — a mid-sized city in western Madhya Pradesh, better known for its role as a trading hub than as a cradle of racket sports — has taken him to two Asian Games, a World Championship medal, a national title, and now the best realistic shot India has had at a soft tennis podium at the Asian Games. The trajectory from curious schoolboy to Asian Games medal contender is not typical. In soft tennis in India, almost nothing is.
