On **September 25** at the shooting range in Aichi-Nagoya, Aishwary Pratap Singh Tomar and his two teammates — Niraj Kumar and Rudrankksh Patil — posted a combined score of **1762-94x** in the men's 50m rifle 3 positions team final. China won gold at **1766-95x**, four points clear. India took the silver medal.

The following day, in the individual event, Tomar scored **327.6** in the final to place fifth. Both results sit in the same column of his career — within reach of the top, narrowly short of it. At 25, he is building a record that already spans two continents and two Olympic Games.

Aishwary Pratap Singh Tomar was born on **February 3, 2001**, in Ratanpur village in the Khargone district of southern Madhya Pradesh. His father Veer Bahadur is a farmer and landowner; the family had no direct connection to competitive shooting. His entry into the sport came through his cousin **Navdeep Singh Tomar**, who introduced him to rifle marksmanship and encouraged him to try seriously.

At 14, Tomar moved to Bhopal — **320 kilometres from Khargone** — to join the **Madhya Pradesh Shooting Academy**. The decision meant leaving home for a residential training environment where he competed for position from the first day. He adapted quickly.

## Junior World Record, Then Two Olympics

In 2019, at the ISSF Junior World Cup in Suhl, Germany, Tomar won gold in the 50m rifle 3 positions event and set a **junior world record final score of 459.3**. He was 18. The result announced an athlete who was not merely developing — he was already competitive at the top of his age group.

The performance earned him selection for the **2020 Tokyo Olympics**, making him one of the youngest Indian shooters to debut at the Games at that time. He returned to the Olympics at **Paris 2024**, placing eleventh in the qualification round.

In 2023, the Government of India recognised his achievements with the **Arjuna Award** for outstanding performance over four years. His overall tally — more than **13 gold, 10 silver and 6 bronze medals** at international events — is one of the most comprehensive medal records among Indian shooters of his generation.

The Asian Games 2026 team silver adds a continental podium to that collection. Tomar had previously won at the Asian Championships and collected medals at multiple ISSF World Cups, but an Asian Games medal had stayed out of reach.

For Madhya Pradesh, Tomar's career is a direct demonstration of what the shooting academy pathway produces when an athlete follows it through. Khargone to Bhopal at 14, a junior world record at 18, two Olympics by 23, an Arjuna Award at 22, an Asian Games silver at 25. Each step was built on the infrastructure the state made available at the start.

The 50-metre range in Nagoya is behind him. The Paris experience and the Asian Games silver place him among India's most reliable performers in a discipline where the difference between silver and gold is four points. That gap is what he will spend the next cycle closing.
