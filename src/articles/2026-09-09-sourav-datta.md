Inside IISER Bhopal's Department of Biological Sciences, the boundary between a research meeting and a teaching session is deliberately thin. This is not an accident of scheduling. It is how Prof. Sourav Datta has structured his academic practice.

On 5 September 2026, Teachers' Day, President Droupadi Murmu presented Datta with the National Award to Teachers — Higher Education Institutions (HEI) at Vigyan Bhawan in New Delhi. The HEI sub-category of the Ministry of Education's national award specifically recognises faculty at universities and institutes who have transformed higher education through outstanding teaching and academic leadership. He was one of six educators from Madhya Pradesh honoured at the ceremony that day.

When IISER Bhopal announced his selection, the institution's message highlighted two things side by side: his classroom and his laboratory. They are, for Datta, part of the same undertaking.

## Light, Plants, and the Logic of Adaptation

Datta's research focuses on plant molecular biology and developmental biology. His central scientific question is **photomorphogenesis** — the process by which plants detect light signals and use them to regulate their development. How a seed decides when to break through the soil. How a seedling calibrates its growth to the quality and intensity of available light. How plants sense changing light environments and adjust their biology accordingly.

His recent publications in 2026 include work on the molecular regulation of the seed-to-seedling transition under dynamic light conditions (*Plant Physiology*, February 2026) and on light-driven biochemical changes in pepper fruit (*New Phytologist*, June 2026). His research has been cited by more than **3,500 scientists** worldwide across plant science, making his Bhopal-based laboratory a reference point in the field.

This work carries direct relevance to agriculture. As growing seasons become less predictable and light environments for crops shift, understanding the molecular switches that govern plant responses to light is foundational science for building more resilient crop varieties — the kind MP's farmers will need.

## Teaching Researchers, Not Just Students

IISER Bhopal's founding mandate is to integrate world-class science education with original research — to produce graduates who do science, not merely study it. Datta has pursued this from his first years at the institute: asking students to question experimental designs, to propose alternative methods, and to understand that every published result reflects someone's verified work, not a given truth.

He has received the IISER Bhopal Distinguished Teacher Award **twice** — a recognition that reflects both peer and student assessment. Earlier, in 2014, the Department of Biotechnology, Government of India, presented him the Innovative Young Biotechnologist Award (IYBA), one of the country's most competitive recognitions for early-career biological scientists.

## What the Recognition Means for MP

Madhya Pradesh has invested significantly in higher education and research infrastructure over the past decade. IISER Bhopal, a national institute of science education and research established in 2008, sits at the centre of that ambition. Having a faculty member receive the national teaching award in the HEI category is formal documentation that the teaching within its walls meets the highest standard the Ministry of Education can attest.

For a state whose next-generation economic ambitions — in agriculture, manufacturing, and technology — depend on a pipeline of well-trained scientists, the signal is practical, not ceremonial. The scientists who will answer those challenges are being shaped in classrooms like Datta's, by a teacher who does not separate research thinking from teaching thinking because, in his view, they are the same thing.
