On 28 September 2026, the Madhya Pradesh government will present the National Lata Mangeshkar Award to film composer Shantanu Moitra at the Lata Mangeshkar Auditorium in Indore. The award — which carries a cash prize of ₹5 lakh and a citation — is the state's highest honour for a lifetime contribution to Indian music, presented each year on the anniversary of the birth of the singer whose name it bears. This year's two-day programme on 27–28 September will conclude with a live performance by the singer-composer duo Sachet and Parampara.

Moitra built his reputation across two decades of Bollywood scores that stood apart for their melodic economy — tunes that sounded inevitable once you heard them, and stayed long after the film. His score for Parineeta (2005) drew from Rabindra Sangeet traditions and introduced him to a wide audience; Lage Raho Munnabhai (2006) and 3 Idiots (2009) followed, each of them number-one albums in their year of release. The 3 Idiots soundtrack, with songs like "Behti Hawa Sa Tha Woh" and "Zoobi Doobi", became one of the decade's defining film scores in Hindi cinema.

His range went beyond commercial Bollywood. In 2014, Moitra won the **National Film Award for Best Background Score** for Na Bangaru Talli, a Telugu film about child trafficking — recognition from the national jury for work done quietly, without the visibility of a mainstream Hindi release.

## From 3 Idiots to 12th Fail

The arc from 3 Idiots to 12th Fail (2023) is not an obvious one, but it is consistent: Moitra gravitates toward stories about aspiration and the odds stacked against it. 12th Fail, Vidhu Vinod Chopra's film about an IPS officer from a village who clears the civil services exam against every structural disadvantage, earned wide critical acclaim and became one of 2023's most discussed Hindi films. Moitra's score, which leaned into folk-inflected simplicity, matched the film's tone precisely.

The National Lata Mangeshkar Award, administered by the Madhya Pradesh government's culture department, is one of the oldest state-level music honours in the country. Previous recipients have included classical musicians, playback singers and composers who built their careers largely outside the Hindi film mainstream. Moitra's selection brings in a composer whose work is rooted firmly in contemporary cinema while drawing on classical and folk traditions.

The ceremony location carries its own significance. Indore was Lata Mangeshkar's birthplace, and the auditorium named in her honour is the city's primary venue for classical and film music events. Presenting the award there, on a stage where her recordings have been played for decades, connects the honour directly to its origin.

For Madhya Pradesh, the award is also a statement about the state's place in Indian cultural life — a recognition that music made in Bombay studios often has its roots, its listeners, and its deepest resonances in the towns and cities of central India.
