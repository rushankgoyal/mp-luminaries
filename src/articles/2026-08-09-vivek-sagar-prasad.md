When India's men's hockey team takes the field against Wales in Amstelveen on 15 August, one of the midfielders wearing the blue jersey will be from Itarsi — a town on the railway line between Bhopal and Nagpur. Vivek Sagar Prasad, 26, was named in the **20-member Indian squad** for the FIH Hockey Men's World Cup 2026, which runs from August 15 to 30 across venues in Belgium and the Netherlands.

The selection came in late July. Vivek anchors the midfield alongside Manpreet Singh and Hardik Singh — the trio the coaching staff has entrusted with controlling the tempo and distributing the ball in India's build-up play. The role suits him. Over seven years in the senior national setup, Vivek has been described by observers as a midfielder who combines quick feet with defensive discipline: he reads the press line, recovers possession, and moves the ball in tight spaces faster than most of his peers.

The World Cup squad follows two Olympic campaigns. Vivek was part of the Indian team that won bronze at **Tokyo 2020** — India's first Olympic hockey medal in 41 years — and played again four years later at **Paris 2024**, where India defended that bronze position. After Paris, the Madhya Pradesh government awarded him **₹1 crore**, recognising the achievement as a milestone for the state's hockey tradition.

## From a Village Near Itarsi to Two Olympic Podiums

Vivek grew up in Shivnagar Chandon village, near Itarsi in Hoshangabad district. His father, a schoolteacher, was initially reluctant to see hockey become his son's career. It was his elder brother Vidya Sagar who encouraged him when a local coach began offering training sessions around 2010–11. Vivek took to the sport quickly.

A few years later, at a tournament in Maharashtra, he met Ashok Kumar — the Indian forward who was part of the 1975 World Cup–winning team and who runs a hockey academy in Bhopal. Vivek was offered a place there and relocated to the state capital while still a teenager, training under a direct link to one of Indian hockey's defining moments.

His junior career was interrupted in 2016 when a collarbone injury during practice forced him out for months and looked set to cost him a place at the Junior Hockey World Cup. He recovered, and in January 2018 he debuted for India at the senior level at the age of **17 years, 10 months, and 22 days** — the second-youngest player to do so in the sport's history.

The next year brought the **FIH Rising Star of the Year 2019** award — the first Indian player to receive it. In 2021 he was named the **FIH Young Player of the Year**. A gold medal at the **Asian Games 2022** in Hangzhou and the Asian Champions Trophy title in 2024 followed.

India's 2026 World Cup campaign carries a 51-year wait. Their only World Cup title came in Kuala Lumpur in 1975 — the same tournament Ashok Kumar, the coach who first spotted Vivek, helped win. Pool D fixtures against England, Pakistan, and Wales are set for Amstelveen before the knockout rounds. For Vivek Sagar Prasad, the tournament is the one major title missing from an already-full cabinet.
