On 7 September 2026, Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav presented Jabalpur the **Clean Air Appreciation Award** at Indira Paryavaran Bhawan in New Delhi. The occasion was Swachh Vayu Diwas, the ceremony for the 5th Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan — the Ministry of Environment's annual ranking of **130 cities** on how rigorously they act on their National Clean Air Programme action plans. Jabalpur finished **third** among India's million-plus cities, earning **195 points**. The IAS officer who built the programme that produced that result is Preeti Yadav, Commissioner of Jabalpur Municipal Corporation.

Yadav joined the Madhya Pradesh cadre after clearing the UPSC civil services examination with an All-India Rank of 145. Her background is in mechanical engineering — a training that shapes how she approaches urban problems. Where many administrators read summaries, she reads operational specifics: which machine is running at which ward, which contractor has missed the target, which monitoring station is showing a spike. She was the first woman collector of Agar-Malwa before moving to Jabalpur, where she is known for inspecting water-treatment plants and waste-to-energy facilities on weekends.

## What Jabalpur built

Jabalpur sits in the Narmada belt with an industrial base in defence manufacturing, quarrying, and construction materials. Particulate pollution has historically been its primary air-quality challenge. Under Yadav's watch, the Municipal Corporation ran a programme across multiple fronts simultaneously.

Road-cleaning machines replaced manual broom sweeping on arterial roads, cutting the dust clouds that form behind morning traffic. More than **200 active construction and demolition sites** were covered with netting and subjected to mandatory daily water-sprinkling, targeting the fine-dust dispersal that typically spikes between October and March. Green-belt planting expanded along major corridors, adding vegetated barriers between traffic exhaust and residential neighbourhoods. A community-awareness campaign on construction dust, crop-residue burning, and the city's AQI monitoring network reached localities that the data had flagged as persistent hotspots.

The results appear in Jabalpur's NCAP monitoring data and in the Sarvekshan scores. The city has now appeared in the national top five in consecutive years.

Madhya Pradesh is the only state with three simultaneous national clean-air honours from the 2026 survey. Indore finished second, Jabalpur third, and Bhopal's Ward No. 60 earned the first-ever micro-ward recognition under the NCAP framework — a triple outcome no other state achieved this cycle.

## Why it matters

India's NCAP set a target of **40 per cent reduction** in particulate matter for covered cities by 2026. Most cities under the programme are still working towards it. A third-place national finish means Jabalpur is not just completing paperwork — its measured air quality is improving in a city with active quarrying, defence-sector industry, and dense traffic corridors, where 1.4 million people breathe the air every day.

For Preeti Yadav, September 7 is a reference point. She has said Jabalpur's monitoring infrastructure needs to grow as fast as its city footprint. The engineering instinct persists: measure it precisely, then manage it. The survey says Jabalpur is already one of the places in India getting that right.
