India's canoe slalom team for the 2026 Asian Games, opening in Aichi-Nagoya on September 19, includes Pradhyumna Singh Rathod — an Indore-trained paddler who graduated from Emerald Heights International School in 2023. His selection makes him the youngest Indian canoe slalom athlete to compete at the Asian Games since India first fielded a team in the discipline in 2014.

Canoe slalom arrived late to Indian competitive athletics. The sport — in which athletes navigate a kayak or canoe through a course of hanging gates on white-water rapids, against the clock and with two-second penalties for each gate touched — appeared on the Olympic programme decades before India had a serious domestic presence in it. The country's first Asian Games canoe slalom participation came in 2014. In the twelve years since, only a handful of Indian paddlers have qualified. Rathod is now among them, and at the youngest age in that history.

## From National Games Gold to the World Cup

The results that earned Rathod his selection came in quick succession. At the **38th National Games** held in Uttarakhand in early 2025, he won **gold in canoe slalom** — a performance that established him as the country's strongest domestic paddler in the discipline. Within months, he made his senior international debut at the **ICF Canoe Slalom World Cup in Augsburg, Germany**, one of the most competitive venues on the world circuit. ICF confirmed he had reached the highest finish ever recorded by an Indian paddler in that event.

He had also competed at the **2023 ICF Junior and U23 Canoe Slalom World Championships**, arriving at the senior circuit with international experience already behind him.

For the 2026 Asian Games, Rathod is entered in **two events: men's kayak single (K1) and kayak cross** — the latter a format that involves four athletes on the course simultaneously, with tactical positioning as central as speed.

India has targeted Aichi-Nagoya for a record national medal haul. In canoe slalom, where the country has no Asian Games medals yet, Rathod's task is to compete at the level that earned him the youngest-ever selection tag. The National Games gold and the Augsburg World Cup finish suggest the pace is there.

Rathod grew up in Indore, trained through the MP development system, and went through Emerald Heights International School's sports programme before making the national roster. The Asian Games start in fifteen days. Madhya Pradesh's canoe slalom representative is going to Japan.
