Each year on the eve of Independence Day, the Ministry of Home Affairs releases a list of police officers selected for the President's Police Medal for Distinguished Service — the highest peacetime honour the Government of India confers on a serving police officer. Most names on it belong to supervisory ranks. On 14 August 2026, Constable Raj Lochan Prasad Dubey of Madhya Pradesh Police was placed in that company.

Of the four Madhya Pradesh officers selected this year for the President's Medal for Distinguished Service, Dubey is the only one holding a constable's rank. The other three recipients from the state are Deputy Inspector General Sunil Kumar Pandey, Superintendent of Police Dharmvir Singh, and Inspector Anil Kumar Bhadauria. In a system where rank and recognition usually travel together, a constable standing alongside a DIG and an SP on the honours list makes a visible point about the nature of the service being acknowledged.

## What the Medal Means

The President's Police Medal for Distinguished Service is not given for a single act of bravery — that is a different award. This medal recognises sustained, exceptional performance across a career: consistency, discipline, and a record of public service that, taken together, rises above the ordinary work of the force. For constables, who handle beat duty, bandobast, first-response calls, and the daily weight of ground-level policing, the award speaks to what decades of reliable, careful work look like when finally brought into the light.

Madhya Pradesh Police as a whole received **twenty medals** in this year's Independence Day announcement — four President's Medals for Distinguished Service and sixteen Medals for Meritorious Service. Director General of Police Kailash Makwana noted that the recognition reflects the dedication, discipline, and exemplary service of the recipients to the force and the people of Madhya Pradesh.

On 15 August, Chief Minister Dr Mohan Yadav attended the Medal Investiture Ceremony at Lal Parade Ground, Bhopal, honouring **forty-seven** police, home guard, and civil defence personnel in all, including recipients of the Sarvottam Jeevan Raksha Padak. It was, as these ceremonies always are, the moment when quiet service becomes public record.

## Why It Matters

India's 1.6 million-strong police force rests, in practical terms, on its constables. They are the first face the public sees, the first to arrive at a scene, and the last to leave after a long deployment. Their careers rarely generate headlines. Raj Lochan Prasad Dubey's selection for the President's Police Medal for Distinguished Service on India's 80th Independence Day is one of those rarer occasions when a career spent in the background is acknowledged at the country's highest level — a recognition that will stay on the record long after the parade grounds empty.
