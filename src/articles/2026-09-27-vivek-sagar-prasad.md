During India's Pool A match against South Korea at the Asian Games 2026 in Japan on September 24, Vivek Sagar Prasad stepped onto the pitch for his 200th senior international appearance. He is 26 years old, from Shivnagar Chandon village near Itarsi in Madhya Pradesh's Hoshangabad district — and in that match, he joined the small group of Indian hockey players to have reached that number.

Hockey India marked the moment on the field itself. Chief coach Craig Fulton presented Vivek with a commemorative jersey in front of his teammates. The acknowledgement came mid-tournament, not at a retirement ceremony, because Vivek is still very much in the middle of representing India: he has played through Pool A at the Asian Games 2026, and India has moved through that stage unbeaten, heading into the knockout rounds.

## Two Olympic Bronzes, an Asian Games Gold, and 200 Matches

Vivek made his senior debut in 2018 at 18 years old, and the 200 caps since then have been built at tournaments that shape careers — not at the margins.

He was part of the squad that won India's first Olympic hockey medal in 41 years at the Tokyo 2020 Games: a bronze that ended a painful drought in the sport that had once defined Indian athletics at the Olympics. He was in Paris 2024 as well, same result, same podium, the same composed presence in midfield. Two Olympic bronzes before turning 25.

Between those Games, Vivek was in the Indian team that won the 2022 Asian Games in Hangzhou — the competition held in 2023, and India's first Asian Games hockey gold in **57 years**. He also holds a silver from the 2022 Commonwealth Games in Birmingham and another from the 2018 Champions Trophy in Breda. Across eight years of senior internationals, all before turning 27, the record reads: two Olympic bronzes, an Asian Games gold, a Commonwealth silver, a Champions Trophy silver.

The **200-cap** figure is the sum of all of it — the marquee medals, but also the Pro League cycles, the FIH ranking events, and the qualification matches that fill every playing year. It is a number built on showing up, on being selected, on earning the starting place again.

India's semifinal at the Asian Games 2026 comes next. A gold medal here, with Vivek anchoring midfield, would make this the most decorated run in Indian hockey in living memory. For the sport in Madhya Pradesh, and for players watching from Hoshangabad district, his 200th cap is a marker on a path that has become visible: a player from a village near Itarsi, 200 times in blue, still running.
