With 24 shots over a women's 10m air rifle SH1 final in Aichi-Nagoya on Monday, India placed two athletes on the same podium at an Asian Para Games shooting event for the first time. Mona Agarwal won gold with a Games record **250.0 points**. Avani Lekhara took silver with **249.6** — a margin of 0.4 points separating them. South Korea's Lee Yunri finished third.

Lekhara had been the defending champion in this event, having won gold at the **2022 Hangzhou Asian Para Games**. Monday's silver in Nagoya was one place lower, but the result comes with a consequence that matters for the longer game: it secured India's **second quota place** for the Los Angeles 2028 Paralympics in the women's 10m air rifle SH1 event.

## A Career Built on Defending the Hardest Thing to Win

Lekhara was born in Jaipur, Rajasthan, in 2001. At ten years old, she was injured in a car accident that left her with paraplegia. She began shooting a few years later, reportedly inspired by reading about Abhinav Bindra, and her progress moved fast. By 2021, at nineteen years old, she was competing at the Tokyo Paralympics and winning gold — becoming the **first Indian woman to win a Paralympic gold medal** and setting a Paralympic record of 249.6 in the process.

She defended that title at the **Paris 2024 Paralympics** in front of a European crowd that had come to watch someone fail. She did not. The gold in Paris made her the most decorated Indian female Paralympic athlete in history.

In between, and since, the honours have come steadily: the **Padma Shri in 2022**, gold at the 2025 Para Shooting World Cup in Al Ain, and now a silver in Nagoya. She has not finished outside the top two at a major international final in five years.

The 0.4-point margin that separated her from gold on Monday is small enough to reverse on another day, and this is precisely the context in which Monday's silver should be read. Lekhara is twenty-four years old, still within the arc of her competitive prime, and already in possession of a record that most athletes never approach. The Nagoya result — a silver that seals a 2028 quota and extends India's most consistent individual medal record in para-shooting — is one more step in a career built on defending the hardest thing there is to win.

India's para-sports ecosystem has grown substantially in the decade since Lekhara first picked up a rifle. Her results are among the clearest measures of how far that growth has gone. For the Los Angeles cycle, she arrives early, quota secured, and very much in form.
