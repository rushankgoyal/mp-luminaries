On the eve of India's 80th Independence Day, the Ministry of Home Affairs released its list of President's Medal awardees. Among the names on that list — drawn from police forces across every state and union territory — was **DIG Sunil Kumar Pandey** of the Madhya Pradesh Police, recognised with the **President's Medal for Distinguished Service**.

Pandey, who joined the Indian Police Service in **2009** and is currently posted at the **MP Police Headquarters, Bhopal**, was one of four Madhya Pradesh officers to receive the distinguished-service tier of the award this year. Alongside them, **16 more** MP police personnel received the President's Medal for Meritorious Service — a total of **20 medals from a single state force** in one Independence Day cycle.

## What the Medal Recognises

The President's Medal for Distinguished Service is not awarded for a single act. Unlike gallantry awards, which honour a specific moment of courage, this medal recognises sustained excellence — years of professional conduct, institutional contribution, and consistent performance across postings and roles.

The process is competitive. Nominations travel through state police leadership, through the Ministry of Home Affairs, and are evaluated against nominations from every state and central police organisation in India. Of the **1,057 personnel** honoured nationwide this Independence Day 2026, the selection of four from Madhya Pradesh for distinguished service represents a meaningful mark of quality.

For an officer of the 2009 batch, a DIG posting at headquarters typically means work that is less visible than a field range — co-ordinating operations between ranges, overseeing training pipelines, managing the administrative infrastructure that lets district and sub-divisional police function without disruption. It is unglamorous, essential work. Officers who make this list have usually done it well for long enough that the selection committees notice.

## Madhya Pradesh's Strong Showing

The scale of MP's medal count this year — **four distinguished-service and sixteen meritorious-service medals** — is not a coincidence. Over the past decade, the MP Police has invested in training infrastructure, expanded its data-driven crime-mapping systems, and put resources into police station-level reforms that are measured in response times and charge-sheet completion rates. Those systemic improvements show up, eventually, in the quality of the officers the force produces and retains.

Twenty medals from one state force in one year is a number that commands attention inside police circles. It tells the story of a force building towards something — and of individual officers, like Sunil Kumar Pandey, who have contributed to that trajectory quietly, year after year, posting after posting.

In Bhopal on the morning of August 15, the tricolour went up and the names came out. One of them was his.
