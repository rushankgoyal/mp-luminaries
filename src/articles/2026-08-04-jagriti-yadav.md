Gwalior, 3 August 2026. The Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy's Sub-Junior women faced a familiar opponent in the final — Pritam Siwach Hockey Academy from Sonepat — but an uncomfortable scoreline. With forty-four minutes gone, the Sonepat side had restored their lead to 2-1 after MP's earlier equaliser. Fifteen minutes to play, one goal down, the championship very much in the balance.

In the 55th minute, Jagriti Yadav scored.

The goal levelled the match at **2-2** and, when no further score followed, sent the final to a penalty shootout. The Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy won that shootout **3-1**, and with it the Sub-Junior Women's title at the **4th Hockey India Women Academy Championship 2026**. On the same day, their junior women's side had already claimed the Junior title in a straight **3-2** win over the same opponents. It was, by any measure, a complete day for the academy.

The nine-day tournament, held in Gwalior from 26 July to 3 August, brought together **15 academies** from across the country in the Sub-Junior category. MP played through the group stage and knockout rounds with enough consistency to reach the final, but it was the closing moments of that final that will be remembered — and the player who brought her team level when the championship appeared to be slipping away.

## From Varanasi to Gwalior

Jagriti Yadav is not from Madhya Pradesh. She grew up in Varanasi, in eastern Uttar Pradesh, and came to the game without the advantages that proximity to a top academy provides. Her mother supported the family through tailoring work, delivering stitched garments door-to-door, and the financial constraints of that upbringing meant that hockey was as much a choice made despite circumstances as because of them.

In August 2024, she was selected for the **Madhya Pradesh State Women Hockey Academy of Excellence** in Gwalior. The selection was formal recognition of potential that had shown itself through state-level competition; the challenge from that point was to perform on a bigger stage, against academies with deeper resources and longer histories.

She had already shown she could. At the **16th Hockey India Sub Junior Women National Championship** held in Ranchi in April 2026, she scored for the Madhya Pradesh state side in a national-level field where every team had earned their place. The championship tournament in Gwalior was the next level up.

The final was a harder test. Trailing with the clock running down, the pressure to score is particular — the kind that collapses young players who have not yet been in situations where a result depends on a single touch. She played through it, found the goal, and gave her team what they needed to stay in the match.

## Why the Goal Mattered

MP Hockey Academy has been building its women's programme steadily, producing players who go on to represent the state in national tournaments. A double-championship result — winning both the Sub-Junior and Junior categories at the same national academy championship, in the same day, against the same opponent — marks a step beyond steady progress. It is an outcome that validates the training environment in Gwalior and the athletes it draws.

For Jagriti Yadav, the goal in the 55th minute of a championship final was the most visible single moment of a path that began with hockey in Varanasi and an opportunity taken at the Gwalior academy. The sweep on August 3 belongs to the full squad. That equaliser, however, belonged to her.
