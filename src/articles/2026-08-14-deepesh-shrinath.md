Walk into any EV charging station in India today and you face a puzzle: the charger for a two-wheeler may not serve an electric auto, and neither works for a car. Incompatible standards between vehicle types and charging speeds have quietly slowed India's transition to electric transport — not from lack of ambition, but from a very practical hardware mismatch.

Indore-based entrepreneur Deepesh Shrinath has spent five years building an answer. This month, his company RoadGrid India received **₹13 crore** in financial assistance from the Technology Development Board (TDB), the statutory body under India's Department of Science and Technology that backs technologies ready to move from prototype to factory floor. The funds are earmarked for a dedicated manufacturing facility in Indore that will produce RoadGrid's patented Universal EV Charger at scale.

Shrinath, who brings 15 years of experience from UPS systems, power infrastructure and energy roles — including stints at global firms Emerson and Eaton — co-founded RoadGrid in Indore in 2020 alongside Shashank Narayan (CTO) and Chandraprakash Akotkar (COO). The company holds two patents on its EV charging architecture and has built chargers that work across two-wheelers, three-wheelers and four-wheelers, covering both slow AC charging for overnight parking and fast DC charging for fleet use and highways.

## ₹27.5-Crore Plant, 2,000 Chargers a Year

The TDB grant — **₹13 crore** of a total **₹27.5-crore** project — will build a manufacturing and assembly facility capable of producing up to 2,000 universal chargers each year, with commercial production targeted to begin by March 2027. The grant follows a **₹12-crore Pre-Series A** round raised in January 2026, led by Inflection Point Ventures with participation from Venture Catalysts and FAAD Network.

RoadGrid already operates 100-plus charger installations across Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Karnataka, serving enterprise clients that include Indian Oil Corporation, Amazon, BSES and NPCL, and managing over 200 active charging points from its Indore base. The team has grown to more than 40 people.

The TDB partnership carries weight beyond the rupee figure. India's Technology Development Board evaluates both the maturity of the technology and the credibility of the manufacturing plan before committing government funds. RoadGrid passed that evaluation — an independent stamp of confidence in a system that, until now, the market alone was validating.

## Why This Matters for Madhya Pradesh

India's government has set a target of 100 million electric vehicles on the road — a goal that only becomes realistic when drivers can reliably charge whatever vehicle they own, wherever they stop. RoadGrid's universal approach directly addresses that: one charger, any vehicle class.

Madhya Pradesh has staked its clean-energy future on exactly this kind of deep-tech manufacturing. Indore — MP's commercial and industrial capital, the city that has topped Swachh Survekshan seven years running — is already a live RoadGrid market, with chargers in daily use across the city. If the new facility ramps as planned, a significant share of India's next generation of interoperable chargers will carry a Made-in-Indore label.

For a state that has grown its registered startup count from under 2,000 to more than 6,500 in five years, Shrinath's TDB win signals something important: that MP's startup ecosystem is producing not just service and app businesses, but companies solving hard infrastructure problems with patented technology.
