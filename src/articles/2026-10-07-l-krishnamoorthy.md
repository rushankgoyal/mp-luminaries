Wildlife Conservation Week runs from October 2 to 8, and this year it closed with a number that tells an unusually positive story: **12,981 vultures** counted across Madhya Pradesh in the 2025 census — up from 8,397 in 2019, a **54 percent increase in six years**. The Vulture Population Assessment-2026 report, released during the week, confirmed MP as the state with the **highest vulture population in India**, hosting **seven of India's nine vulture species**. The officer most directly associated with the forest management practices behind that recovery is **L. Krishnamoorthy**, the 1997-batch IFS officer who currently serves as Additional Principal Chief Conservator of Forests (Wildlife), Madhya Pradesh.

The Sanctuary Nature Foundation recognised his contribution with its **Wildlife Service Award** — one of India's most respected conservation honours — for his career-long work rebuilding habitat in central India's forests.

## A Satpura Record That Became a Template

The most documented chapter of Krishnamoorthy's career is his tenure as Field Director of **Satpura Tiger Reserve** in Hoshangabad. When he took charge, Satpura's tiger count stood at 41. By 2023, it had climbed to **61 tigers**, a growth rate that placed the reserve among the strongest-performing in the country. In the 2022 Management Effectiveness Evaluation, Satpura was ranked **second** among all tiger reserves in India — a peer-reviewed assessment that covers governance, ecology, and long-term planning.

The method behind those numbers was deliberate. Krishnamoorthy's team worked through the difficult work of **relocating nine villages** from core forest areas — a process that requires legal clarity, community consultation, and the actual construction of alternative settlements. Clearing the core zone of human habitation is not a technocratic fix; it is a decade-long negotiation with communities whose livelihoods depend on land that the forest needs to function. That Satpura completed nine such relocations and held its wildlife trajectory through the period reflects the operational discipline his leadership maintained.

The vulture recovery follows the same principle. Vultures depend on undisturbed carrion access, minimal poisoning of carcasses, and intact open woodland — conditions that come directly from the management quality of the surrounding forest landscape. MP's lead over other states in vulture population is a product of the same approach that produced its tiger numbers: habitat protection enforced consistently over time.

Krishnamoorthy's current role gives him oversight of wildlife management across the entire state — a portfolio that includes the cheetah reintroduction at Kuno National Park, the expanding network of tiger reserves, and the conservation programs that produced the vulture report released this week. The Sanctuary Nature Foundation's award named his career work; the 2025 vulture census made the argument in numbers.
