At the 2025 Asian Paracanoe Championship in Thailand, Pooja Ojha won gold in the VL-1 200m and gold in the KL-1 200m. The double title came from an athlete born in 1990 in Bhind, Madhya Pradesh, with 80 percent lower-body paralysis from polio — and no access to adaptive sports infrastructure growing up.

She works as a Postal Assistant and is based in Mumbai, which is where she trains. She has competed at the Paris Paralympics. None of these were obvious destinations for a young woman from Bhind with a significant physical disability and no family background in competitive sport. When she found para-canoeing as an adult, she began building a career others had decades of head-start on. The results arrived quickly.

In Bhind, in the Chambal region of northern Madhya Pradesh, there is little by way of para-sports infrastructure. What took Ojha from there to the Asian championship podium was the national para-sports pathway — training facilities run through the Sports Authority of India, consistent international exposure, and the kind of upper-body strength that para-canoeists develop over years of specific conditioning. She had to build all of it from late.

## The Record

In 2022, at the **ICF Para Canoe World Championships in Halifax, Canada**, Ojha won India's **first ever international medal in para-canoeing**. A country of 1.4 billion people had not previously placed an athlete on a para-canoeing world championship podium. She changed that.

In May 2024, she won **silver at the Para Canoe World Championships in Hungary** in the VL-1 200m event. Later that year, she represented India at the **2024 Paris Paralympics**, competing in the KL-1 women's 200m kayak event. She reached the semi-finals before falling short of the final — placing her among the fastest para-canoeists in the world in that classification.

VL-1 is the category for para-canoeists with lower-limb impairment who compete in a va'a outrigger canoe, paddling one-sided; KL-1 is the kayak equivalent for similar classifications. The two events demand different boat handling, different paddle mechanics, different race strategies. Winning at continental level in both, in the same championship, is an unusual result.

Para-canoeing is one of India's quieter sporting disciplines internationally — it does not have the visibility of athletics or wrestling. Ojha's journey through it — first medal, world championship silver, Paralympics semi-final, Asian double gold — belongs in the record even so. She is 35 years old. The **2028 Los Angeles Paralympics** are two years ahead.
