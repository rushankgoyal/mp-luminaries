On 22 September 2026, President Droupadi Murmu presented the Rajat Kamal — India's National Film Award for Best Actor — to Kartik Aaryan at a ceremony in Kevadia, Gujarat. The 35-year-old actor from Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, received the honour for his performance in *Chandu Champion*, directed by Kabir Khan. He shared the award with Malayalam actor Mammootty, who won for *Bramayugam*.

Standing on the stage, Aaryan was visibly moved.

> "This is the dream of a small-town boy. I am from Gwalior. We watch these ceremonies on television and hope that one day we might be there. Today that day is here."

*Chandu Champion*, released in June 2024, tells the true story of **Murlikant Petkar** — an Indian Army havildar who was shot multiple times during the 1965 India-Pakistan War, lost the use of his legs, and then refused to treat that loss as the end. Petkar trained in swimming, took up para-sports, and became India's first Paralympic gold medalist when he swam the **50-metre freestyle in 37.33 seconds** at the 1972 Heidelberg Paralympics — a world record at the time.

Aaryan dedicated the award to India's para-athletes. "This is not just my award," he said. "It belongs to every person who fights every day without a crowd watching."

## A Performance Built Over 14 Months

To prepare for the role, Aaryan reduced his body-fat percentage from **39% to 7%** over fourteen months. He trained in swimming, boxing, wrestling and javelin — disciplines Petkar had mastered across the different phases of his career — and worked directly with Petkar, now in his late seventies, to reconstruct the interior life of a man who refused to stop competing.

The 72nd National Film Awards, which honoured films released in 2024, were announced in July 2026 and the ceremony was held on September 22. The jury recognised Aaryan's ability to hold, across more than two hours of screen time, the physical reality of an injured body and the undiminished will inside it. The performance did not soften Petkar's story; it made the obstacles visible so that every small advance registered.

Kabir Khan, who has built a filmography around stories of Indian sporting achievement — *83*, *Bajrangi Bhaijaan*, *Chak De! India* among the scripts he has produced or directed — said the casting decision came from Aaryan's willingness to commit fully. Fourteen months of preparation was not a number arrived at in negotiations; it was the number the role required.

## Gwalior to Kevadia

Kartik Aaryan was born in Gwalior on 22 November 1990. His parents, both doctors, sent him to Mumbai to study pharmacy — a qualification that would guarantee a living if the film industry did not work out. He attended every audition he could find while working through the course, and his first break came in 2011 with *Pyaar Ka Punchnama*. The National Film Award is his first.

For Madhya Pradesh, the moment connects a city better known for Lashkar, Scindia palaces and the classical music of Hindustani tradition to the peak of Indian cinema. The boy from Gwalior who trained in a swimming pool for over a year — to play a soldier who trained in a swimming pool to change his life — now holds cinema's highest honour.
