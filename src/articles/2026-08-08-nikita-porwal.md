Tomorrow, when representatives of more than 130 nations begin arriving in Hanoi for the 75th Miss World pageant, the woman wearing the Indian sash will be from Ujjain — Nikita Porwal, who in October 2024 became the first contestant from Madhya Pradesh to win the Femina Miss India World title.

Porwal grew up alongside the Shipra river in one of India's oldest pilgrimage cities. Long before she walked onto any pageant stage, she was performing on the local theatre circuit. By the time she left for her undergraduate studies at Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda, she had appeared in more than **60 stage productions** and written a **250-page play** — *Krishna Leela*, a script she spent months developing from the mythological stories heard in Ujjain's temples and ghats.

She began anchoring on television at 18, running both careers in parallel. It is the stage work, she has said, that trained her to communicate with precision — to hold a character, to read an audience, to be present without being performative. A role in a feature film that has screened at international festivals followed.

## A First for Madhya Pradesh

In October 2024, Porwal competed in the 60th Femina Miss India at Famous Studios in Mumbai. The pageant assesses candidates over weeks of mentoring, tasks, and panel interviews. Porwal stood out for a biography unlike most finalists: not a single performing art, but a body of stage work, a produced script, and a broadcast career. She also spoke with clarity and specificity on animal welfare, the cause she has chosen as her platform.

When her name was called as Femina Miss India World 2024, it ended a blank that had persisted across six decades of competition: no woman from Madhya Pradesh had held the title before.

The 75th Miss World, which Porwal will now compete in, brings more than **130 nations** to Vietnam. Events span Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Hai Phong, and Nha Trang through the grand finale on **September 5**. It is one of the largest editions the pageant has held.

Madhya Pradesh has deep performing-arts traditions — the Khajuraho Dance Festival, Ujjain's own theatre circuit, Bhopal's cultural institutions — but the state has rarely placed its artists on the global stages where those traditions might find the widest audiences. Nikita Porwal, a product of that theatre circuit, arrives in Vietnam carrying both a crown and a body of work that earned it.
