Every year on Independence Day, the Ministry of Home Affairs announces the President's Police Medals — the nation's formal recognition of distinguished and meritorious service by officers across India's police forces. On August 1, the ministry released the 2026 list, and among the 21 names from Madhya Pradesh was ADG Chanchal Shekhar, selected for the President's Police Medal for Meritorious Service. The recognition marks the culmination of a career spent doing something uncommon in policing: systematically rebuilding how a state police force gathers, moves, and acts on information.

Shekhar, a 1995-batch IPS officer currently serving as Additional Director General of Police (Special Armed Forces), spent a significant part of his career heading the State Crime Records Bureau (SCRB) at Bhopal. The SCRB is the intelligence backbone of any state police — the unit that holds criminal records, manages fingerprint data, and coordinates information-sharing between districts. Under Shekhar, it became something more: a base for building practical technology that reached down to the ground level of the force.

## The Apps That Changed Beat Policing

The most visible result of that work is the E Rakshak App, a mobile tool designed specifically for constables on beat patrol. Before E Rakshak, a beat officer's shift ended with paper notes that slowly made their way back to the station; information moved across districts unreliably. E Rakshak changed the loop: a constable can now scan a face or check a name against a central criminal database in real time, log patrol activity digitally, and flag concerns before the shift ends. The app addresses the specific point where policing most commonly leaks — the first human encounter on the street.

The FICCI Smart Policing Award 2024, one of India's top prizes for policing innovation, went to MP Police for E Rakshak — a recognition not just of the technology but of the institutional will to build something that actually improved daily duty for ground-level officers.

Alongside E Rakshak, Shekhar's team developed BAMS (Behaviour Analytics and Monitoring System) for tracking patterns in crime data; E Vivechna App to bring investigation workflows into a digital format; and Digi Summons, which replaced paper court notices with electronic notifications. Taken together, these systems did not merely add a digital coat to existing processes — they addressed the specific friction points that made old processes slow and unreliable.

The cumulative impact of this work earned MP Police the Digital India Award, presented by the President of India — the government's highest recognition for digital public service innovation. The award acknowledged what the FICCI prize had signalled: that MP Police's technology drive was substantive, not cosmetic.

This year, Shekhar was also chosen unanimously as President of the Madhya Pradesh IPS Association, a role that reflects the respect his peers hold for both his technical record and his leadership within the service.

For Madhya Pradesh's police, the President's Medal on Independence Day 2026 recognises that the most lasting changes in a force happen not always at the crime scene but at the system level — in the patient work of building tools that make every constable more capable, every piece of evidence more accessible, and every shift more useful to the state it serves.
