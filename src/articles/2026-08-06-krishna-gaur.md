At a review meeting at the Mantralaya in Bhopal on 5 August, Krishna Gaur gave her department a firm deadline: **15 August**. All 107 government hostels managed by the Backward Classes and Minority Welfare Department must have their kitchens complete and mess facilities running by Independence Day. The students — boys and girls from backward classes and minority communities across Madhya Pradesh — will have nutritious meals available when they need them.

Commissioner Satyendra Singh reported the department's progress at the meeting: kitchens in **86** of the 107 hostels were already complete, with the remaining facilities in their final stages of construction. The work was expected to finish within the week. The August 15 deadline was not aspirational — it was specific, tied to the date, and tied to the people waiting for the facilities to open.

## A First in Bhopal

Krishna Gaur came to this ministerial role through decades in Madhya Pradesh's civic and political life. She served as Mayor of Bhopal — one of the state's most demanding administrative roles — before entering the legislative assembly. In 2018, she won the Govindpura constituency and became the first woman ever elected as a Member of the Legislative Assembly from Bhopal, a historic milestone in a city that had never before sent a woman to the state assembly. She won the seat again in 2023 by a margin of over 46,000 votes.

In December 2023, she was appointed Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Backward Classes and Minority Welfare and Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic Tribes Welfare in Chief Minister Mohan Yadav's cabinet.

The portfolio covers some of Madhya Pradesh's most under-served communities. The 107 hostels represent physical infrastructure built specifically to support students from backward classes and minority communities in staying near their schools and colleges — removing one of the most concrete barriers to completing an education. A hostel without a working kitchen is a hostel where students must arrange meals outside, adding cost and uncertainty to a situation the hostel was built to stabilise. Gaur's priority was to fix that.

## The Shaurya Sankalp Training Scheme

The hostel infrastructure push sits alongside a separate skills programme that Gaur launched earlier this year: the **Shaurya Sankalp Training Yojana**. The scheme is designed for youth from backward class and minority communities, combining vocational and skills training with structured mentorship intended to build both practical employability and a sense of civic purpose.

Gaur described the programme as a way of providing employment pathways while building discipline and a sense of responsibility toward the country. The chairman of the MP Backward Classes Commission joined the launch in Bhopal.

The two initiatives — hostel kitchens and skills training — are different in scale but connected in aim. A government hostel means a student can stay near a school. A well-equipped kitchen means that student eats properly. A skills programme means that same student has a pathway to work afterward. The chain of welfare delivery, from housing to food to employment, is what the ministry is trying to build.

For the students in those 107 hostels, the August 15 target is entirely practical: it means a kitchen is ready, a meal is served, and the academic year can begin without that particular problem unsolved. The deadline falls on Independence Day — a coincidence that is also, in this context, an appropriate one.
