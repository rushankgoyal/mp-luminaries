On August 25, 2026, Jyotiraditya Scindia walked into the Centre for Development of Telematics' 43rd Foundation Day and delivered an assessment that would have been unthinkable a decade ago: India, he said, has gone from being behind the world on 4G to keeping pace in 5G — and it now has the people, the patents, and the purpose to **lead in 6G**.

The occasion was not a political rally. C-DOT is India's state-run telecom research body, the organisation that has spent four decades quietly building network technologies that power calls and data across the country's toughest terrain. Scindia, as Union Minister for Communications, chairs the institution that oversees it. His message to the 1,565-strong team — **more than half of them under 40** — was that the era of India as a technology importer in telecom is ending, and that C-DOT's job is to ensure it ends on India's own terms.

## The numbers behind the confidence

India's position in the 6G race is no longer aspirational. The country is now among the **top six globally in 6G patent filings**, a metric that the International Telecommunication Union uses to gauge how seriously a nation is investing in next-generation standards. C-DOT's recent international recognition — including an IEEE-SA Corporate Award in 2025 and recognition at Mobile World Congress 2026 for its AI-driven fraud-detection solution — shows that Indian telecom R&D is being taken seriously beyond South Asia.

Scindia's ministry has backed that trajectory with resources. The **Technology Development and Investment Promotion Scheme** (TDIP), revised in April 2026, carries a **₹203-crore outlay for 2026–31** specifically to accelerate indigenous development of 5G Advanced and 6G technologies. The goal is not just to build — it is to commercialise and export. C-DOT technologies must move from the lab to the handset, and from the handset to global markets, Scindia told the Foundation Day audience.

The practical expression of that ambition is on display in a few weeks. From **October 7 to 10, 2026**, Scindia will host the **10th India Mobile Congress (IMC)** at Yashobhoomi in New Delhi — Asia's largest digital technology forum. The event will bring together more than **1.5 lakh attendees**, **300-plus exhibitors and partners from over 100 countries**, and **1,500-plus technology use cases** spanning 5G, 6G, AI, quantum computing, satellite communications and semiconductors. The theme Scindia chose — **"Scale without Boundaries"** — is a deliberate statement of intent: India's telecom innovations are not meant to stop at the border.

## Scindia and Madhya Pradesh

The minister carries the Scindia name — the family that has governed Gwalior in Madhya Pradesh for three centuries. His great-grandmother, Vijaya Raje Scindia, was the "Rajmata" who shaped modern Gwalior's politics; his father Madhavrao Scindia was one of the most effective aviation ministers India has had, transforming domestic air connectivity in the 1990s. Jyotiraditya has inherited both the name and the intensity that comes with it.

As India positions itself to shape the standards that will govern how five billion people connect over the next twenty years, the minister from Gwalior is making sure that the research centre in Delhi — and the engineers half his age who work there — understand exactly how large the opportunity is. For Madhya Pradesh, a state whose sons have shaped Indian aviation, rail policy, and now digital infrastructure, the 6G race is very much a home fixture.
