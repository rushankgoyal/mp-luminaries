On August 25, at Krishi Bhawan in New Delhi, Union Agriculture Minister **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** launched **Mission Samriddh Gaon** — a scheme designed to build villages that are poverty-free and full of livelihoods, rather than communities people feel compelled to leave.

The mission targets three problems at once: persistent rural poverty, inadequate basic amenities, and the distress migration that empties villages when there is no work. Chouhan's answer is not a single programme but a framework that welds together multiple existing government schemes — health, education, infrastructure, agriculture support — and makes departments, panchayati raj institutions, civil society groups, and private-sector partners work as a coordinated team rather than parallel silos.

"The aim is to create villages that are poverty-free and full of employment," Chouhan said at the launch, describing the mission as a way to give rural households a genuine reason to stay and build lives close to home.

## Starting in Madhya Pradesh, with a Personal Connection

The scheme's first pilot projects will begin in **Budni block in Sehore district** and **Khategaon block in Dewas district** — both in Madhya Pradesh. Chouhan was born in Jait village in Sehore and built his political career representing Budni. Choosing that constituency as a pilot is, among other things, a statement of accountability: the minister is putting his own home ground forward as a proving ground.

The pilot design matters because Mission Samriddh Gaon is explicitly built to scale. The plan is to develop replicable models in the two MP blocks, study what works, and then replicate the successful approaches in other states. That sequence — test, refine, replicate — is a departure from schemes that announce national coverage upfront and then struggle with uneven implementation.

The convergence approach at the heart of the mission addresses a long-standing complaint about rural development: that programmes arrive in villages as disconnected initiatives, each with its own paperwork, each managed by a different department, and none adding up to the whole a household needs. Mission Samriddh Gaon asks departments to co-ordinate around outcomes in a specific village rather than around delivery of their own scheme's targets.

For Madhya Pradesh, where the government has already invested heavily in rural infrastructure and agricultural support over the past decade, the mission provides a national framework within which that existing work can find a more coherent shape. Farmers in Budni and Khategaon will be among the first to see what that looks like in practice — a grid of services that moves together, backed by a minister who knows what the fields there look like at harvest.

If the pilots demonstrate what Chouhan is promising, the replication phase will extend the model well beyond MP's borders. But the proof will be in whether fewer families in those two blocks pack their bags for the city next season.
