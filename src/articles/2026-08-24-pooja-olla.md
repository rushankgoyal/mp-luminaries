Pooja Olla crossed the finish line at Bhubaneswar's Kalinga Stadium on August 22, 2026, clocking **4:17.24** to take gold in the women's 1500m at the World Athletics Continental Tour Silver — India. She controlled the race from the front, absorbing the pace in the middle laps before stretching clear in the final straight. For the SAI Centre of Excellence athlete based in Bhopal, the victory was further proof that India's middle-distance track scene is entering a new era.

The win came on a stage that also featured Rohit Yadav's javelin title and Murali Sreeshankar's long-jump gold, making August 22 a bright evening for Indian athletics. Pooja's gold was notable not just for the result but for how she earned it — patient, tactically sound, and finishing with the kind of authority that separates contenders from champions.

## From Hockey to the Track to the International Stage

Pooja Olla was born in June 2002 in Fatehabad, Haryana, and her first sport was hockey. At fourteen she joined the Sports Authority of India centre in Badal, Punjab, to develop as a hockey player. It was a coach there, Preetipal Kaur, who noticed something different in the way Pooja covered ground during warm-up runs and persuaded her to try middle-distance events instead.

The transition paid off quickly. She became the first Indian female athlete to qualify for both the 800m and 1500m at the same major championship — a rare double that signals both raw speed and superior endurance. Her performances at the **2025 Asian Athletics Championships** confirmed what her coaches already knew: a silver in the 1500m (**4:10.83**) and a bronze in the 800m (**2:01.89**, a personal best at the time) placed her among Asia's best in both events. Earlier in 2025 she swept both distances at the Taiwan Open and collected gold medals at the National Senior Inter-State Athletics Championships.

Now stationed at the SAI Centre of Excellence in Bhopal, Pooja has become part of the city's emerging athletics ecosystem. She also serves as a ticket collector at the Bilaspur Railway Division — a reminder that many of India's finest track athletes combine elite training with government employment, navigating both without letting either slip.

## Why This Matters for MP Athletics

Bhopal has long been a centre for field sports and shooting, but middle-distance running has not always been its calling card. Pooja Olla's growing presence in the city, and victories like the one in Bhubaneswar, are reshaping that picture. Her gold at a World Athletics-ranked Continental Tour event carries international ranking points, nudging her closer to future Asian Games and World Athletics Championships selection standards.

For young runners in Madhya Pradesh, watching a woman from Haryana make Bhopal her base and then win on an international stage is its own kind of inspiration — evidence that the SAI infrastructure in the state can anchor a serious career. The gold medal in Bhubaneswar is one more reason to watch Pooja Olla closely as the international season builds toward 2027.
