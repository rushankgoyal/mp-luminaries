Dev Kumar Meena stood at the approach runway in Aichi-Nagoya on September 29 and did what he has been doing all year: he cleared a bar he had never cleared before. His vault of **5.50 metres** in the men's pole vault final at the 2026 Asian Games was a new Indian national record, moving Indian pole vaulting into territory it has never reached.

He was 21. He was competing in his first Asian Games final. He finished seventh in a 12-man event. The number that mattered — **5.50m** — was his alone to keep.

Meena grew up in Dewas, a mid-sized city in western Madhya Pradesh. He came to the sport from an unusual direction: he was a 400-metre runner before his coach Ghanshyam Yadav saw something in his long-limbed build and suggested he try the vault. He shifted events and enrolled at the **Madhya Pradesh Athletics Academy in Bhopal**, the state residential programme that has become one of India's most effective athletics production lines. He later worked with international coach Ángel García to refine his technique.

The transition paid off faster than anyone expected. By 2023, he had cleared **5.16 metres** — enough to win gold at the National Games. He won bronze at the **2024 Asian Under-20 Championships in Dubai** with **5.10 metres**, qualifying him for senior circuits the following year.

## A Record That Keeps Moving

The story of Indian pole vaulting across 2025 and 2026 is the story of two athletes from the same academy trading a record back and forth. Meena and his MP Academy training partner **Kuldeep Kumar** have passed the national mark between them across club meets and federation championships. Meena cleared **5.32 metres** at the 2025 National Games to set the record. Kuldeep bettered it with **5.41 metres** in Bhubaneswar. Meena responded with **5.46 metres** at the same venue months later. At the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow, Meena cleared **5.30 metres** to finish fourth — close to the podium, but not on it.

September 29 in Aichi-Nagoya was the clearest confirmation yet of where his ceiling sits: it is still rising. He cleared 5.50m on his second attempt, then competed through four more rounds as Asian Games finalists from China, Bahrain and Japan raised the bar beyond 5.65 metres. He did not challenge for a medal — the bronze required **5.60m** — but he left the stadium with a number no Indian pole vaulter has ever posted.

For Madhya Pradesh, the result underlines what the Athletics Academy's investment in technical field events has produced. Pole vault requires specialist equipment, trained coaching in a physically precise discipline, and a long runway of junior competition. The state system has provided all three.

Dev Kumar Meena began 2026 holding a national record of 5.46 metres. He ended September 29 holding one of 5.50 metres. The next number is his to set.
