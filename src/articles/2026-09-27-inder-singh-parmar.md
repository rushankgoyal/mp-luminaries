On September 23, National Ayurveda Day, the courtyard of Pandit Khushilal Sharma Government Autonomous Ayurveda College in Bhopal was the site of the largest single-day AYUSH commitment Madhya Pradesh has announced in recent years. Cabinet Minister for AYUSH Inder Singh Parmar presided over ceremonies marking the inauguration and foundation-stone laying of **25 AYUSH hospitals, dispensaries and college buildings** across the state, built at a combined cost of **₹185.50 crore**.

It was a day the sector had been waiting on. Madhya Pradesh has a substantial base of AYUSH practitioners — doctors trained in Ayurveda, Yoga, Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homeopathy — but the physical infrastructure to serve patients and train the next generation has lagged behind the practitioner numbers. The 25 facilities announced on the 23rd, spread across multiple districts, are designed to close that gap in communities where modern hospitals are often far away.

## Nine New Ayurveda Colleges and the Stipend Question

Alongside the infrastructure inaugurations, the state revealed plans to establish **nine new Ayurveda colleges** in Madhya Pradesh. The expansion of the college network, once operational, will significantly increase the pipeline of qualified Ayurveda graduates — doctors who will eventually staff the new hospitals and dispensaries now being built.

Minister Parmar also used the National Ayurveda Day platform to raise the issue of AYUSH interns' pay directly before Chief Minister Mohan Yadav. Interns in Ayurveda programmes across MP currently receive a monthly stipend of ₹9,383. MBBS interns performing comparable clinical hours receive ₹21,500. Parmar called publicly for that gap to be closed. Chief Minister Yadav responded by indicating that Minister Parmar would be directed to take the matter forward — a signal that the stipend revision has moved from a standing demand to a policy conversation at the top of the state government.

Parmar is a three-time MLA from Shujalpur in Shajapur district, serving his third consecutive term. He has held the AYUSH portfolio under CM Mohan Yadav's government since December 2023, alongside Higher Education and Technical Education. His tenure at AYUSH has centred on building institutional capacity — making traditional medicine a system with its own modern infrastructure, not merely a supplement to allopathic care.

The investment announced on Ayurveda Day is the most concrete evidence yet of that direction. **₹185.50 crore** in new facilities, nine colleges in the pipeline, and a stipend conversation that has moved from petition to public advocacy by the minister himself — these are outcomes with long-range consequences for practitioners and patients alike.

For Madhya Pradesh, a state where traditional medicine is often a first choice for communities in semi-urban and rural districts, the expansion of AYUSH infrastructure is not a symbolic gesture. The minister's advocacy on the national stage on September 23 made that case plainly — and the Chief Minister's response suggests Parmar's push is being heard.
