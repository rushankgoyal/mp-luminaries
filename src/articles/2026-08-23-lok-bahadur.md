On August 21, in Pune, Hockey Madhya Pradesh's junior women's team beat Manipur **3–1** in the quarter-final of the 16th Hockey India Junior Women National Championship 2026 and earned a place in the last four. Two players — Kajal, with two goals, and Tanvi, with one — did the scoring. The result placed MP among the four strongest junior women's hockey programmes in the country.

It is the kind of result that looks routine when it keeps happening. Looked at over time, it is not routine at all.

## Twenty-Nine Medals in Sixteen Years

Since Hockey Madhya Pradesh began building a structured national championship programme, the state has accumulated **29 medals** across Hockey India national championships — **23 of them in women's hockey, six in men's**. Within the women's programme, the junior team has won **6 medals (3 gold, 3 silver)**; the sub-junior women have added **10 medals**.

For a state that has never been on the traditional map of Indian hockey — which runs through Punjab, Odisha, Jharkhand, and the northeast — this record represents something that was built rather than inherited. Madhya Pradesh does not have the same depth of hockey infrastructure that these states have accumulated over generations. What it has built instead is a system: operations based in Jabalpur, with academies in Bhopal and Gwalior, drawing on district-level identification across a geographically large state.

**Lok Bahadur**, General Secretary of Hockey Madhya Pradesh, has described the model as deliberately distributed. "Hockey Madhya Pradesh's growth is a result of a truly collective effort across the state," he said after one of the programme's earlier national results. "With our operations led from Jabalpur, we have built a system that works closely with districts across Madhya Pradesh, where talent is first identified and nurtured before progressing into our academies in Bhopal and Gwalior." The association is led by President **Nitin Dhimole**; Bahadur handles the operational architecture.

## What the Semi-Final Means

The 16th Hockey India Junior Women National Championship — running August 12 to 23 in Pune — assembled the strongest under-20 women's hockey states in Division A. Reaching the semi-finals means MP finished in the top four of the field after navigating the group stage and the knockout round.

The semi-final itself, on August 21, was a closer contest: MP faced Jharkhand, a state with an established women's hockey pipeline that has produced national-level players at the senior level. The result went Jharkhand's way. The final, contested today between Jharkhand and Haryana, will determine the national title — but MP's bronze-medal finish is already confirmed.

Against states that have produced senior India internationals from their junior pipelines, finishing third nationally is the kind of benchmark that hockey federations — and national selectors — track.

For the players who scored in Pune, and for the coaches and district officials whose work produced them, the result is the evidence. For Lok Bahadur's programme, it is the continuation of a record that has been building for sixteen years.
