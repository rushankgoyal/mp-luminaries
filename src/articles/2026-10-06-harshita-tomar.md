The women's 49erFX series at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games concluded on **October 2** with India appearing to have finished fourth. Harshita Tomar and her sailing partner Shital Verma had collected **24 net points** across the series — not enough to overhaul Japan (**11 points**) or Hong Kong China (**15 points**), and apparently not enough to beat China (**23 points**). Then the race committee's rulings came through: China's pair was disqualified from three races, their corrected penalty score lifting them past India's total. Japan kept gold. Hong Kong kept silver. India moved to bronze.

Tomar is twenty-four and from Hoshangabad — officially renamed Narmadapuram — in Madhya Pradesh. She grew up far from any coastline, and her path into high-performance sailing ran through the **Madhya Pradesh State Water Sports Academy** at the Barasia reservoir outside Bhopal, operated by the state's Directorate of Sports, Youth Welfare and Multipurpose. It is not a facility that produces sailors who drift. It produces sailors who win.

Her first Asian Games medal arrived in **Jakarta in 2018**, when Tomar was sixteen years old. Competing in the women's Laser 4.7, she won bronze — one of the youngest members of India's Asian Games squad that year. The medal brought recognition in Madhya Pradesh sporting circles, but she was already training toward a longer arc.

By 2026, that arc had carried her into a different and faster class: the **49erFX skiff**, a demanding twin-wire high-performance dinghy requiring precise coordination between helm and crew. The class rewards sailors who read wind shifts quickly and execute cleanly when it matters. Across the series in Aichi-Nagoya, Tomar and Verma showed they could do exactly that.

## Two Asian Games, Two Bronze Medals

The 49erFX racing at Aichi-Nagoya ran over multiple days, with Tomar and Verma building their points position through each successive race. Their final net score of **24 points** placed them fourth on the water — a competitive result at the continental championships, but not the podium. The upward revision came after race officials confirmed that China's team had committed rule infringements carrying disqualification in three races. With their corrected score, they dropped below India's tally, and Tomar and Verma rose to third.

The result makes Tomar the holder of two Asian Games medals across two different sailing classes, earned eight years apart with two different partners. That span — adapting to a faster boat, switching class, competing at the Asian Games level twice — is uncommon for an Indian sailor.

In the same session at Aichi-Nagoya, India also won silver in the men's 49er through Manu Francis and Prince Noble, making it a strong day on the water for India's sailing programme overall.

For Madhya Pradesh, a landlocked state with no coast, Tomar's two bronzes are proof that inland infrastructure, sustained coaching, and long-term commitment to an athlete can produce continental medals. The water sports academy at the Barasia reservoir does not announce itself loudly. Its athletes let the results speak.
