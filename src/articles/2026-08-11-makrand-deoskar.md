On 15 August 2026, when India marks Independence Day, Makrand Deoskar will receive the President's Police Medal for Distinguished Service — the highest national honour in Indian policing. The 1997-batch IPS officer from the Madhya Pradesh cadre has been named among four state officers to receive the PSM as part of the Independence Day 2026 announcement covering 21 Madhya Pradesh law-enforcement personnel in total.

Deoskar was born in Bhilai on 27 November 1972, grew up in Bhopal, and joined the Indian Police Service with a career that would take him through some of Madhya Pradesh's most demanding field postings. His educational background includes a B.Tech degree, a postgraduate degree in History, and a Diploma in Cyber Law — a combination that reflects both analytical training and a grasp of the digital dimensions of modern policing. Over more than two decades as a field officer, he served as Superintendent of Police in Barwani, Hoshangabad, Chhindwara, Jabalpur, and Indore before moving to senior leadership positions in Gwalior, Bhopal, and Indore.

## From the State's First Commissioner to the Country's Border

In December 2021, Madhya Pradesh restructured policing in Bhopal by converting to a commissionerate system — meaning the state capital, for the first time, would have a Police Commissioner rather than a Superintendent or DIG at the top of local law enforcement. Makrand Deoskar stepped into that role and became Bhopal's first-ever Police Commissioner. The appointment placed the city's policing under a direct, unified command structure designed for large urban areas, a shift the state had debated for years.

He later moved to Indore, MP's commercial hub, as its Commissioner of Police. In Indore, Deoskar prioritised anti-drug operations and traffic safety. In one widely noticed initiative, he rode a bicycle to his office on #NoCarDay, a public act that set the tone for his approach to urban traffic from the top of the chain of command.

In January 2024, the central government deputed Deoskar to the Border Security Force as an Inspector General — a five-year posting at the national level of India's paramilitary infrastructure. He currently holds that role, posted from the Madhya Pradesh state cadre to the country's primary land-border security organisation.

The President's Distinguished Service Medal on Independence Day 2026 recognises not a single posting but a career: a Madhya Pradesh officer who came up through the districts, made history in Bhopal, shaped policing in Indore, and now contributes to national border security. Of the **21 medals** being announced for MP law-enforcement personnel, only four are the PSM — the rest are the Medal for Meritorious Service. Deoskar is one of those four.

For Madhya Pradesh, a state whose police force often does its work far from national headlines, the cluster of Independence Day medals is a reminder that good policing has always happened here. Deoskar's recognition, in particular, spans a career that left visible marks on two of the state's major cities.
