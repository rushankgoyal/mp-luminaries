India did not just win hockey gold at the 2026 Asian Games in Japan. They defended it — a thing Indian hockey had never done before at this competition — and they did it with a scoreline that left no room for doubt.

On October 3 at Gifu Prefectural Green Stadium in Kakamigahara, captain Harmanpreet Singh's side beat Malaysia **5-1** to claim the country's fifth Asian Games hockey gold and, with it, a direct berth to the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics. It was the kind of performance that distinguishes a team that came to defend a title from one that merely hoped to.

Harmanpreet opened the scoring in the 11th minute with a drag-flick — the shot that has become his trademark across international hockey — and returned in the 57th to complete a two-goal afternoon. In between, Jugraj Singh (23'), Hardik Singh (42'), and Shilanand Lakra (54') added to the tally. Malaysia's Shello Silverius scored in the 14th minute for the lone consolation in what was otherwise India's complete control of the match.

## The Road Through Lahore's Rival

The final capped a tournament in which India navigated the one fixture that still draws millions to hockey: Pakistan.

Three days earlier, on October 1, India beat Pakistan **4-3** in the semifinal — a match that stayed tight deep into the second half before India held their nerve to advance. Harmanpreet's captaincy through that fixture set the mental tone for what followed in the final: composure, not just skill.

The 2026 gold is India's **fifth Asian Games hockey gold** and their first successful title defence, retaining what the team had won in 2022. The victory also produced an individual milestone: Manpreet Singh became the first Indian hockey player to win three Asian Games gold medals across his career — a record that sits alongside the team achievement in Kakamigahara.

## What the Olympic Berth Means

The stakes at the Asian Games were higher than a medal. A gold here carries an automatic qualification slot for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics — bypassing the gruelling continental qualifier cycle. For a team that finished with a bronze at the Paris Olympics in 2024, the direct path to LA is not just convenient; it is time to build.

India's men's and women's hockey teams both reached the 2026 Asian Games finals, a coordination of achievement that is rare for any generation of the sport. The women's result is its own story; this one belongs to a squad that arrived in Japan with something to prove and a captain who answered the brief in the most direct way available: two goals, five scored, one conceded, title defended.

For Indian hockey, the question after Paris was whether the momentum would hold. In Kakamigahara, Harmanpreet Singh answered it.
