Two gold medals. One Asian record. One piece of history no Indian woman had written before. In back-to-back days at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi, trap shooter Neeru Dhanda from Shivpuri, Madhya Pradesh, produced the performance of her career.

On the first day, she stood at the Aichi Prefectural General Shooting Gallery for the women's trap individual final and hit **28 of 30** clay targets — a new Asian record, two points clear of China's Sun Yunong. The score bettered the previous Asian record and made Neeru the **first Indian woman to win an Asian Games gold medal in any shotgun discipline** — trap, double trap, or skeet. The next day, she paired with Kynan Chenai to win the trap mixed team gold at a record score.

The achievement is the peak of a year in which Neeru had already established herself as the best women's trap shooter in Asia. In July she won India's first-ever women's trap gold at an ISSF World Cup in Lonato, Italy — her first World Cup title. At the Asian Shooting Championship in 2025, she had also taken gold. She arrived at Aichi as the clear favourite; she left it as a two-time Games champion.

## A Long Road from Shivpuri

Neeru's family moved from Jind in Haryana to Shivpuri, Madhya Pradesh, more than a decade ago. Growing up, she watched her cousin Lakshay Sheoran win a silver medal at the 2018 Asian Games in trap shooting and quietly set the same target for herself. She joined the Indian Army's Corps of Military Police, rose to the rank of Naib Subedar in the Army Marksmanship Unit, and trained with support from the Madhya Pradesh State Shooting Academy, the Sports Authority of India under the Target Olympic Podium Scheme (TOPS), and Olympic Gold Quest (OGQ).

The road had obstacles. Reports from Aichi noted she overcame a recent hospitalisation to compete at the Games — a detail that makes the Asian record score all the more striking.

## What Two Golds Mean

Indian shotgun shooting — trap, skeet and double trap — has produced strong men's results at major events over the years. Until now, no Indian woman had matched that at an Asian Games. Neeru's individual gold and the Asian record she set in the process put women's trap shooting on equal footing.

For Madhya Pradesh, the result is a direct return on the State Shooting Academy's investment. Neeru Dhanda is 26 years old, at the midpoint of her competitive prime, with Paris 2028 ahead. The odds of seeing an Indian woman stand on an Olympic shooting podium have just improved considerably.
