On 23 September 2026, the International Day of Sign Languages, Kanishka Sharma from Bhopal was recognised as one of four extraordinary achievers from the city who have turned hearing and speech impairment into something other than a limitation. She is 18 years old. She has competed at the **2025 Deaflympics in Tokyo**. And she is the **first Indian girl ever to represent India in taekwondo at the Asia Pacific Deaf Games**.

Kanishka was born deaf and speech-impaired. In 2021, her father Kapil — a photographer who had run his own studio — made a decision that would define the next phase of her life. He shut the studio and dedicated himself fully to supporting Kanishka's training. The family's commitment to her sport was not casual enthusiasm; it was a career change. That level of backing, combined with the right coaching environment, is what the next few years would require.

She trains at the **Madhya Pradesh Martial Arts Academy at TT Nagar Stadium in Bhopal**, under chief coach **Jagjit Singh** and trainer **Arjun Rawat**. The academy competes at national level, and Kanishka moved through the circuit steadily — district, state, national — winning medals and building the experience base that earns a place in an international squad.

## A First for Indian Deaf Taekwondo

The moment that placed Kanishka in a specific piece of Indian sporting history came in 2024, when she was selected to represent India at the **10th Asia Pacific Deaf Games in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia**. She was the **first Indian girl to compete in taekwondo at the Asia Pacific Deaf Games**, a distinction no athlete had held before her. The Asia Pacific Deaf Games brings together deaf athletes from across the region in a multi-sport format. Walking onto the mat in Kuala Lumpur in the Indian kit, Kanishka became a reference point for other young deaf girls in Madhya Pradesh and beyond who aspire to compete internationally.

From Kuala Lumpur, she continued her climb. She earned selection to the **India squad for the 25th Summer Deaflympics in Tokyo, Japan**, held in November 2025. The Deaflympics is the world's highest stage for deaf athletes — the equivalent of the Olympic Games, organised by the International Committee of Sports for the Deaf. Kanishka competed in the women's category, finishing fifth. A fifth-place finish at the world's premier deaf sports event is not a medal, but it is confirmation that her standard is genuinely international.

In recognition of her journey, she was named an **International Deaf Taekwondo Player at the 7th Women Excellence Award 2025** held at Rabindranath Tagore University in Madhya Pradesh.

For Bhopal's martial arts community, Kanishka's story carries a specific lesson: the path to the Deaflympics runs through consistent daily training at academies like TT Nagar, through fathers who make sacrifices, and through coaches who understand what it takes to prepare an athlete for international competition. She has done all of this before the age of nineteen. What she does next is still being written.
