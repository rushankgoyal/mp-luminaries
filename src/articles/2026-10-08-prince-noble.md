On **October 1**, at the sailing venue off Aichi-Nagoya, Prince Kurisinkal Noble and his cousin Manu Francis crossed the finish line with enough points to stand on India's first-ever podium in the men's **49er skiff** at an Asian Games. Their silver medal — **23 net points** across the series, behind China's gold on 16 and ahead of Hong Kong's bronze on 24 — arrived without drama or disputed rulings. They earned it race by race: a win in Race 3, second place in Races 1, 5, and 9.

The class they competed in rewards sailors who move fast and think faster. The 49er is a high-performance twin-wire skiff — the helm and crew both fly on trapeze wires extended out over the water, using their combined body weight to keep the boat flat as it accelerates past other skiffs. It is a physical, technical discipline that India had never before medalled in at the continental games.

## Cousins, Colleagues, Soldiers

Prince Noble and Manu Francis grew up in Ernakulam, Kerala, and are first cousins. They both joined the Indian Army and sail through the Army's Yachting Node — the institutional structure that provides competitive sailors within the armed forces with the time, boats, and coaching to pursue national-level racing. Their joint campaign at the Asian Games reflects years of coordination, both on and off the water.

Their time at the **National Sailing School in Bhopal** — part of the Madhya Pradesh Water Sports Academy infrastructure — gave them access to inland training waters and coaching that helped shape their technical base. The Free Press Journal's coverage of MP's eight-medal Asian Games haul listed them among the athletes connected to the state's water sports programme.

Before Aichi-Nagoya, they had already built a regional reputation: a bronze at the **Asian Sailing Championships in Abu Dhabi in 2022** and another at **Pattaya in 2023**. The silver at the Asian Games is the step up that turns a promising regional pair into continental medallists in one of sailing's most demanding classes.

Prince Noble dedicated the medal to his father, Noble KG, who died in the **2018 Kerala floods**. The dedication made a quiet round through Indian sports reporting — a medal won in Japan, offered to someone who never saw the result.

## What the Medal Means

India's sailing programme has produced Asian Games medals in other classes before — Harshita Tomar and Shital Verma won bronze in the women's 49erFX in the same session at Aichi-Nagoya. But the men's 49er silver is a first, and it comes in a class that feeds directly into Olympic competition. The 49er is a Paris 2028 Olympic discipline; a pair that finishes second at the Asian Games has made a credible case for selection.

For the Indian Army's sailing programme and for Bhopal's water sports infrastructure, the medal is evidence that investment in landlocked training venues and institutional backing for military athletes produces results on open water. Prince Noble and Manu Francis raced in Japan. They trained, in part, in Madhya Pradesh.
