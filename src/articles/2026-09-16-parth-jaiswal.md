On September 15, 2026, the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation held the 12th edition of its District Collectors' Peyjal Samvad — a national forum where collectors share what is actually working under the Jal Jeevan Mission. Shahdol, a district in Madhya Pradesh's eastern Vindhyan belt, was among only six districts selected to present their best practices. Standing in for Shahdol was its newly appointed collector, Parth Jaiswal, a 2015-batch IAS officer from the Madhya Pradesh cadre who had taken charge just weeks before.

The Jal Jeevan Mission set out to give every rural household a functional tap connection, then extended its ambitions into JJM 2.0 — focused not just on infrastructure delivery but on ensuring that systems work, generate revenue, and are maintained locally. Shahdol's presentation was a demonstration of how a district moves from construction to operation.

## What Shahdol Showed the Nation

Three things defined Shahdol's showcase. The first was a **digital platform for overhead tank cleaning and maintenance** — a tool that prevents the water quality failures and system breakdowns that often undermine rural supply schemes once the bricks-and-mortar phase is over. The second was a set of systems for **Gram Panchayat-led operation and maintenance**, putting accountability for local infrastructure in the hands of the communities that use it.

The third was a clear trajectory with public deadlines. Civil and electrical works across the district's rural water supply schemes are largely complete, with remaining works expected to be commissioned by **November 2026**. Jaiswal set two firm finish lines for the national audience: **full completion and functionality of all schemes by December 2026**, and the declaration of Shahdol as a fully JJM-functional district on **January 26, 2027** — Republic Day.

In a programme where many districts have struggled to bridge the gap between connections installed and water actually flowing reliably, choosing Republic Day as the public deadline is a specific act of accountability. There is no ambiguity about whether it has been met.

Shahdol sits in a region that has historically seen slower infrastructure rollout. The district covers parts of the Vindhyas and is home to a large Scheduled Tribe population. For communities that have depended on wells, handpumps, and seasonal streams, functional household taps are not a convenience — they change how much time women and girls spend on water collection, what diarrhoea rates look like in the next monsoon season, and whether a child can reach school without a detour to a distant source.

The Peyjal Samvad series exists precisely to accelerate that convergence by spreading solutions that have already proven out. Shahdol's digital maintenance platform and panchayat ownership models are now on the national register of JJM approaches worth replicating. Jaiswal, fresh in the role, used his district's first national appearance to stake a specific claim: that Shahdol will be functionally complete when the tricolour rises on Republic Day.
