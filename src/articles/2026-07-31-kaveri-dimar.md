At the 2022 Asian Canoe Sprint Championships in Rayong, Thailand, a team of four Indian women — **Kaveri Dimar**, Namita Chandel, Anjali Bashishth, and Shivani Verma — won bronze in the Women's C-4 200m event. It placed India on the Asian championship medal table in a team boat discipline. It also placed a young woman from a fisherman's family in Sehore district, Madhya Pradesh, at the centre of that result.

Kaveri Dimar is from Mandi village in Sehore district, the fifth of nine children in a family that earned its living from the rivers of the Narmada basin. The household was close to the water and, for much of her childhood, close to financial difficulty. There was nothing predetermined about the path from that village to a medal in Rayong.

The connection was made in 2016 by **Joseph Baxla**, the Sports Officer in Khandwa district, who encountered Kaveri competing as a swimmer and saw something worth developing. He supported her admission to the **Madhya Pradesh Water Sports Academy** in 2017. The academy switched her discipline: from open swimming to canoe sprint, the flatwater competitive form that runs on technique, paddle rate, and explosive acceleration over short distances.

## From Academy to Asian Competition

Between 2020 and 2022, Kaveri Dimar won **15 gold medals, 2 silver medals, and 1 bronze** across India's National Canoe Championships. The consistency of that domestic record — across solo, pair, and team events — is what moves athletes from national rankings to national squads. More than 50 medals at national and international level across that period placed her among India's top-ranked women canoeists in the C-class events.

The Rayong bronze was her first major international appearance. A C-4 200m event is a four-person canoe sprint over flatwater — a discipline in which China, South Korea, and Uzbekistan have dominated Asian competition for years. India's bronze was significant because it placed a full team, not just a single athlete, on the podium in a boat discipline.

She has since secured a position in the **Indian Navy** through the sports quota — the programme that provides high-performance athletes with career stability and continued access to training. Her brother Sudeep Dhimar has also competed at the national level in canoe sprint; in a family from a river basin, competitive paddling has become something close to an inheritance.

For Madhya Pradesh's water sports programme, Dimar's path represents the model doing what it was designed to do: identify talent in a district most people cannot place on a map, develop it through the state academy system, and deliver an athlete to the Asian podium. That delivery took six years — from a sports officer's observation in Khandwa to a medal in Rayong. The state's canoe programme is still running the same model.
