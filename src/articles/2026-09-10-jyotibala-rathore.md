On 5 September 2026 — Teachers' Day — President Droupadi Murmu presented the National Award to Teachers to Jyotibala Rathore in New Delhi. Rathore, a Training Officer at the National Skill Training Institute (Women) in Indore, was one of six Madhya Pradesh educators honoured this year among 82 recipients chosen from across the country. The award recognises not just teaching but what teaching produces — and in Rathore's case, that means women who can compete nationally and find work in the garment industry.

Her career has unfolded in two stages. Before she entered a classroom, she spent six years working in the garment and fashion sector as a dress designer and trainer. She brought that floor experience into NSTI Women Indore — a central government institute under the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship — and has been teaching there for well over a decade since. The combination of a Diploma in Garment Technology and years of actual industry work gives her instruction a grounding that purely academic teaching cannot replicate.

## What Her Students Do After They Leave

NSTI Women Indore offers trade courses in dress-making, garment construction and related skills — craft disciplines where the gap between classroom exercise and workplace standard can be wide. Rathore has worked to close it. Her trainees compete at national and state-level skill competitions, and they have won. Beyond competition results, she has consistently worked to place her graduates into employment — maintaining industry connections and guiding women from the end of their course into actual jobs in the garment sector.

The two outcomes together — competition wins and employment placement — speak to a course structure shaped by what the industry expects, not just what a curriculum prescribes. Rathore's qualifications on paper are modest: class XII and a garment technology diploma. What two decades of instruction adds to those credentials is harder to list formally but immediately visible in student outcomes.

The National Teacher Award is given to 82 teachers annually across India; it is not a participation recognition but a selective one. In 2026, MP's six recipients included school teachers, a professor at a research institute, and Rathore — the one teacher on the list whose subject matter is a vocational trade rather than an academic discipline. That inclusion signals something about how skill development is being understood at the national level: not as a lesser category of education, but as work that deserves the same recognition as classroom teaching.

India's women's workforce participation in the garment and textile sector runs into millions of jobs. Madhya Pradesh has its own share of that industry, and the pipeline into it runs through training institutes like NSTI Women Indore. Teachers there are not producing graduates for a test; they are preparing workers for a trade. For more than a decade, Jyotibala Rathore has been doing that — and on Teachers' Day 2026, the President of India acknowledged it.
