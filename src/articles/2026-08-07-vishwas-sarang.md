On 1 August, Vishwas Sarang announced that two athletes from Madhya Pradesh who had just returned from the Commonwealth Games 2026 in Glasgow with silver medals would each receive **₹25 lakh** in prize money and a government job under the Madhya Pradesh Sports Policy. Yamini Mourya, a judoka from Sagar who finished second in the women's 57 kg event, and Ajay Babu, a weightlifter from Barwani, had placed MP athletes on an international podium. Sarang said their success had brought pride to Madhya Pradesh and the country, and that he expected it to motivate the next generation of young sportspersons in the state.

The announcement was the application of a standing policy — one that Sarang has been consistent about since he took charge as Cabinet Minister for Sports and Youth Welfare in December 2023. When an athlete from Madhya Pradesh wins a medal that qualifies under the state's sports policy, the response is not discretionary. The prize money is paid and the job offer follows.

## Building the System Behind the Medals

What Sarang has been constructing as minister is an architecture, not a set of gestures. The clearest example is his decision to reserve dedicated seats for para athletes in all **11 state sports academies**, which offer free training alongside residential facilities. Para athletes had previously been outside the mainstream academy pipeline. Reserving those seats meant athletes with disabilities could access the same systematic training that MP's able-bodied competitors use to reach international competition.

He also backed the National Rifle Association of India's talent hunt initiative, which aims to introduce **7.5 lakh students** across the country to shooting — a long-term grassroots investment with the 2036 Olympics as the target horizon. MP's shooting academies are part of the feeder structure for that programme. At the state level, Sarang launched the Summer Camp Aroh-2026 and the Khelo MP Youth Games as platforms for young athletes who have not yet entered the academy system.

In July 2026, he met with **22 Madhya Pradesh athletes** who had qualified for the Asian Games 2026 in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, scheduled for September and October. Those 22 athletes cover disciplines spanning athletics, shooting, canoeing, kayaking, judo, and equestrian sports — the widest range of qualifying athletes any edition of the Asian Games has sent from this state.

Sarang came to this portfolio through a long career in Bhopal's civic and political life. He has represented the Narela constituency in Bhopal's Legislative Assembly since 2008 — the first MLA ever elected from that seat — and served as BJP's Madhya Pradesh state president from 2007 to 2010, during which he drafted the state's first youth policy. He is a civil engineer by training; that background is visible in an approach that emphasises infrastructure and pipelines over individual acts of recognition.

For Yamini Mourya and Ajay Babu, the ₹25 lakh and the jobs are meaningful in themselves. But the more important detail is why they exist: because a policy framework was built before anyone won a medal, so that when medals arrived, the support was already written into the rules. That is the pattern Sarang is extending — not individual rewards for individual wins, but a standing structure that tells every young athlete in Madhya Pradesh that when international success comes, the state will be ready to receive it.
