At the Senior Commonwealth Fencing Championships in Lagos, Nigeria, last week, India closed out four days of competition with 35 medals — 13 gold, 8 silver, and 14 bronze — and the overall championship title, the first time India had topped the standings at this level. Among the athletes who contributed to that count was **Khushi Dabhade**, a fencer from the Banganga neighbourhood of Bhopal, who left Lagos with a team gold and an individual silver.

The gold came in the Women's Épée Team event. Fencing's team épée format is a relay: a sequence of individual bouts, each capped at specific cumulative scores, until one team reaches 45 touches or the time is spent. India's women's team won that relay. Khushi then entered the individual Women's Épée draw, fought through to the final, and faced Taniksha Khatri of the Indian Navy. She lost 7–15. The silver was the second medal of the championship.

## From Tatya Tope Stadium

Khushi Dabhade grew up in Banganga — one of the lower-income localities in Bhopal — in a family that measured progress in small, consistent steps. Her father **Babarao Dabhade** works as an OT attendant at a hospital; her mother **Vidya Dabhade** is a staff nurse at a private clinic. There was no family tradition in fencing, no existing connection to elite sport. What there was, eventually, was the **Madhya Pradesh Sports Fencing Academy** at Tatya Tope Stadium.

The academy is the state's primary facility for developing competitive fencers. Khushi trained there under the guidance of **Bhupendra Singh**, the head coach, who has prepared several athletes for national representation. The transition from a Banganga household to a national team bib involves years of training in a sport that few people in MP follow or understand — a technical discipline that runs on blade control, distance management, and the speed of a single touch.

The championship ran from August 9 to 14, 2026, at Rugby School Lagos in Eko Atlantic. India's 35 medals covered both under-23 and senior divisions. The country's fencing programme has been building depth through state academies like the one in Bhopal; the overall title is evidence of what that depth looks like at the competitive level.

For Madhya Pradesh, an athlete from Banganga winning gold at a Commonwealth-level event — alongside a silver in the individual final — is the kind of result the state's sports infrastructure exists to produce. Khushi Dabhade is 2026's clearest demonstration that it is working.
