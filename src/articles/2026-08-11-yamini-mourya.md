Yamini Mourya walked onto the competition mat in Glasgow on 31 July 2026 with the gold medal already saved as her phone wallpaper. The 28-year-old judoka from Sagar, Madhya Pradesh had trained for this moment for years, and in the women's -57 kg final against England's Acelya Toprak, she showed exactly why she belongs at the top of the sport. The contest went to Golden Score — sudden-death overtime — where a third Shido penalty ended her gold-medal bid. She left Glasgow with silver. She left with India's attention.

For a district not widely known for producing international athletes, Yamini's silver at the 2026 Commonwealth Games marks a turning point. Born on 26 March 1998 in Sagar, she began her judo career through state-level competitions and steadily built a record that earned her a place at the Inspire Institute of Sport in Karnataka, one of India's elite high-performance training centres. She competes in the women's -57 kg category and has represented India at the World Championships, Asian Games, and Asian Championships, building a competition record across the IJF World Tour circuit.

## A Decade of Mat Work, Crowned in Glasgow

Yamini's resume before Glasgow was already substantial. She won gold at the 2022 National Games, announced herself internationally at the 2025 Hong Kong Asian Open with another gold, and earlier in 2026 took the Dakar African Open gold. A bronze at the 2023 FISU World University Games showed her consistency across formats. Still, the Commonwealth Games — with its scale and television reach — gave Yamini a stage that earlier competitions had not.

She reached the Glasgow final by defeating rivals in the earlier bouts, setting up the showdown with England's Toprak. Golden Score judo is unforgiving: every grip, every movement, every passive moment carries the risk of a penalty. Mourya received her third Shido, handing the gold to Toprak — but the silver was the outcome of a near-perfect day on the mat.

> "I had gold on my phone wallpaper, but one mistake changed everything," she said after the final — a line that captures both her ambition and the fine margins that separate gold and silver at the top level of judo.

The Madhya Pradesh government recognised her effort quickly. Chief Minister Mohan Yadav congratulated Yamini and announced a cash reward of **₹25 lakh** along with a government job, in line with the state's sports policy for international medallists.

Yamini is one of four Indian judokas who medalled at Glasgow 2026, a historic haul for Indian judo at a single Commonwealth Games. Two of those medals — won by Asmita Dey and Harsh Singh — were gold. Yamini's silver carries its own distinction: she went closest to the top step and came away knowing the difference was one penalty call in overtime.

For Sagar — a city of temples and a university town in central Madhya Pradesh — Yamini Mourya's silver is a signal. Judo programmes in smaller Indian districts rarely produce Commonwealth finalists. That one just did, and that it came from Sagar sends a message worth hearing all the way back home.
