On the morning of Independence Day 2026, as the national flag unfurled at state functions across Madhya Pradesh, Rakesh Gupta's name appeared on a very short list. Only four officers from the state's entire police force earned the President's Distinguished Service Medal (PDSM) this year — India's highest honour awarded by the President for exceptional, career-defining contribution to police service. For Gupta, a 1999-batch IPS officer of the MP cadre, it was recognition for nearly three decades of service that moved, deliberately, between the visible and the unglamorous.

## A Career That Followed the Work

Gupta's record is built on variety of a purposeful kind. He served as Commissioner of Police in Indore — MP's commercial capital and its most complex policing environment — taking charge in February 2024 during a period of administrative transition. Later that year, in October 2024, he moved to one of the most senior advisory roles available to a serving officer: Officer on Special Duty to Chief Minister Mohan Yadav. The OSD posting, far from a step back, placed him at the intersection of policy, security planning, and state-level governance decisions.

Before those assignments, Gupta led the Directorate of Sports and Youth Welfare. The posting might seem peripheral for a career police officer, but it sat at the centre of a state-wide push to identify and support young athletes from rural MP — an effort whose results are now visible in national and international competitions. The administrative groundwork laid by the Directorate during those years contributed to the infrastructure that has since supported MP athletes competing at the Commonwealth and Asian Games level.

Most recently Gupta has held the rank of Additional Director General of Police at PHQ Bhopal, where he oversees coordination across multiple police districts.

## What the Medal Represents

The President's Distinguished Service Medal is awarded twice a year — on Republic Day and Independence Day — and the total number of recipients from any state in any cycle is small. For Independence Day 2026, MP received four PDSMs and seventeen Meritorious Service Medals, out of a national pool shared across every state, union territory, and central police organisation. In a state with thousands of serving police personnel, four PDSM recipients represents a strict selection against a demanding standard.

The PDSM is not awarded for a single act or a single case. It recognises the aggregate: a pattern of decisions, investigations, administrative outputs, and institutional conduct across a full career. Gupta's movement between frontline command, government advisory work, and department administration over 27 years reflects exactly that breadth.

For Madhya Pradesh's police force — which collectively earned 21 medals of various ranks in this Independence Day cycle — Gupta's recognition is a signal that the state's mid-to-senior leadership cadre is producing officers whose careers hold up to national scrutiny. That matters both inside the force, where it sets expectations for younger officers watching, and outside it, where institutional confidence in police leadership shapes how communities and government bodies engage with law enforcement.
