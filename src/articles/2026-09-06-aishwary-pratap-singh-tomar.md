In Ratanpur village, Khargone district, the nearest shooting range is not close. Aishwary Pratap Singh Tomar grew up far from the infrastructure that tends to produce elite rifle shooters, in a district better known for cotton farming than precision sport. Yet by the time he was 25, he had stood at the top of the world in his discipline — not metaphorically, but on the scoreboard.

In February 2026, at the Asian Shooting Championships held at the Dr Karni Singh Shooting Range in New Delhi, Tomar fired a score of **362.0** in the men's 50m rifle three positions final. The score was a **world record**. He won gold, and India completed a podium sweep in the event, with Niraj Kumar taking silver and Akhil Sheoran bronze.

## From Khargone to the Top of the Scoreboard

The 50m rifle three positions event is arguably the most demanding discipline in rifle shooting. A shooter fires 45 rounds from three distinct positions — kneeling, prone and standing — each demanding different muscle groups, breathing control and mental stillness. The smallest tremor in the standing position, where the rifle has no sling support, can move a shot by a full point. Tomar's world record of 362.0 means he lost fewer than ten points across the entire final — an exceptional margin of control.

This was not his first time competing at the top of Asian shooting. Tomar represented India at the **Tokyo 2020 Olympics** and returned for the **Paris 2024 Olympics**, making him a two-time Olympian before turning 25. At the 2022 Asian Games in Hangzhou, he was part of the Indian men's 10m air rifle team that won **gold** — a result that placed him among India's most experienced shooters heading into the 2026 cycle.

He has been training at the Madhya Pradesh State Shooting Academy in Bhopal, a facility that has supplied the national team for decades. This month, the National Rifle Association of India organised a final **preparatory camp in Bhopal from 5 to 14 September** for the Asian Games rifle and pistol squad, with mental conditioning and technical refinement the focus of the two-week programme. Tomar is one of three men entered in the 50m rifle three positions event, alongside Niraj Kumar and Rudrankksh Balasaheb Patil.

> "I have to take India to the top," he said ahead of the Games. "The expectations motivate me. They always have."

The rifle shooting events at the Asian Games 2026 begin on **17 September** at the Aichi Prefectural General Shooting Range in Japan, two days before the official opening ceremony. Tomar will compete against shooters from South Korea, China and Japan, each of them capable of world-class scores.

What he carries to the range is a world record set nine months ago and a career built in Khargone, far from the shooting academies that usually define the path to international competition. That distance, and what he did despite it, is the Madhya Pradesh story he now takes to Japan.
