On the morning of August 13, 2026, President Droupadi Murmu took her seat at Vigyan Bhavan in New Delhi and began conferring the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowships and Awards for 2024 and 2025. More than a hundred performing artists came forward — musicians, dancers, theater practitioners, puppeteers, folk and tribal performers — to receive India's highest national recognition in the performing arts. Among them was **Rajiv Verma**, an actor from Hoshangabad (now Narmadapuram district) in Madhya Pradesh, who received the Akademi Puraskar for Acting.

The Sangeet Natak Akademi Puraskar, awarded annually across disciplines in music, dance, and drama, goes to artists who have made a sustained contribution to their field. In the Acting category, it recognises work in theater — the performance tradition that the Akademi was established to preserve and develop. Verma's recognition placed him in the company of artists honoured alongside him across India's entire breadth of performing arts.

## A Long Road Through the Stage

Rajiv Verma was born on June 28, 1949, in Hoshangabad — a town on the Narmada in what is now Narmadapuram district. He studied architecture at Maulana Azad National Institute of Technology and later earned a master's degree in Urban Design from Delhi's School of Planning and Architecture. The path from design education to the theater stage was his own — he moved toward acting and made his career in a medium where the work is the credential.

He has been based in Bhopal for much of that career, a city that has served as Madhya Pradesh's primary center of theater activity. Bhopal's performing arts ecosystem — built in part around institutions established in the 1980s and sustained since — has produced a generation of theater practitioners who moved between the stage, Hindi-language cinema, and television. Verma worked across those registers, accumulating the kind of record that earns national recognition not with a single performance but over decades.

The Sangeet Natak Akademi's recognition of Verma, conferred in 2026 for the 2024 cycle, catches up with a career that has long operated outside the national spotlight. That is not unusual in theater: the discipline rarely delivers the visibility of cinema, and artists who spend careers on stage — developing craft, mentoring younger performers, sustaining institutions — often receive formal recognition late. In the Akademi's framing, the award is precisely for this: the cumulative contribution, not the individual moment.

For Madhya Pradesh, Verma's award is a reminder that the state's performing arts tradition, rooted in the Narmada basin and anchored in Bhopal, continues to produce artists of national stature. His is one of the quieter careers in that tradition — and, now, one of the recognised ones.
