On July 20, 2026, the Lokmanya Tilak Smarak Trust in Pune announced that National Security Adviser Ajit Doval would receive the **Lokmanya Tilak National Award 2026**. The award will be presented by Union Home Minister Amit Shah on August 1 — the 106th death anniversary of Bal Gangadhar Tilak — at a ceremony in Pune. The citation recognises Doval's contribution to India's national security, counter-terrorism architecture, and border security over more than five decades of public service.

The Lokmanya Tilak National Award carries weight that goes beyond a ceremony. Previous recipients have included Prime Minister Narendra Modi, former Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, Sharad Pawar, and Anna Hazare. Each selection is made by the Trust to mark someone who has served the nation at a level Tilak himself would have recognised — where personal conviction shapes public outcomes.

## From the Field to the NSA's Office

Ajit Kumar Doval is a 1968-batch IPS officer of the Kerala cadre. He was born in the Pauri Garhwal region of what is now Uttarakhand and built a career in intelligence that few in Indian history can match for length or intensity.

For most of his active career, his work was not publicly known — which is, of course, the point. He spent years in counter-terrorism operations and covert intelligence work in environments where discovery would have meant death. In **1988**, he was part of the operation that helped end the long occupation of the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar, known as Operation Black Thunder. He later headed the **Intelligence Bureau (IB)**, India's domestic intelligence agency, before retiring from service.

He came out of retirement when Prime Minister Narendra Modi appointed him National Security Adviser in **2014**. He has since been reappointed twice, making him India's **longest-serving NSA**. In that role he has handled some of the most consequential strategic decisions the country has faced — the Doklam standoff with China in 2017, the Balakot air strike in 2019, and the coordination of India's response to the 2025 Pahalgam attack that preceded **Operation Sindoor**.

The Lokmanya Tilak Award recognises the full arc: the field intelligence officer, the IB director, and the NSA who has sat beside three Prime Ministers through moments when the country's security was directly at stake. The prize itself — **₹1 lakh**, a memento, and a citation — is modest. What it marks is not. For a person whose most significant work was done in the dark, the award on August 1 will be one of the rare moments when the nation formally and publicly says: this service mattered.
