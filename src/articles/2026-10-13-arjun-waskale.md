On October 9, at Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in New Delhi, Arjun Waskale ran the final hundred metres of the men's 1500m with two rivals pressing just behind. When the race ended, the gap between first and third was 0.10 seconds — Waskale in **3:43.04**, Shivam Sharma in 3:43.12, Rahul Baloda in 3:43.14. It was one of the tightest finishes at the 65th National Open Athletics Championships, and the gold medal went to Waskale, a runner from Thikri village under Kasrawad Janpad in Khargone district, Madhya Pradesh.

The 23-year-old trains at the Madhya Pradesh Sports Academy in Bhopal, a state programme that has shaped a number of the middle-distance runners who have emerged from western MP in the last decade. Waskale's father is a farmer in Khargone district. The journey from a farming village in the Nimar region to a national open title is rarely straightforward; for Waskale, the MP Academy made it possible.

He came to national attention early. In 2022, still competing in the under-20 category, he ran the men's 1500m at the National Federation Cup Junior U20 meet in Nadiad, Gujarat, and clocked **3:46.31** — a new meet record and the qualifying standard for the World Athletics U20 Championships in Cali, Colombia. He was 18, running for the first time at a major national selection meet, and had broken the record set by a runner who would later become one of India's top-ranked senior 1500m competitors.

## From Khelo India to New Delhi

At the Khelo India Youth Games 2022 in Panchkula, Haryana, Waskale won gold in the 3000m — his first significant multi-sport title at the national level. By October 2023, his 1500m personal best had improved to **3:41.69**, recorded at Bambolim, Goa, placing him among the faster Indian runners in the event. He was included in India's 1500m entry for the 2025 South Asian Athletics Championships, competing at the international level for the first time alongside other national squad members.

The 2026 National Open was his first national open title in the senior men's field. The race demanded patience through the middle laps and a sustained effort over the final straight. Sharma was close enough at the bell that a 0.10-second margin across 1,500 metres reflects the race's quality: in a slightly different closing 200 metres, any of the three finishers could have crossed first.

The 65th National Open ran from October 8 to 11 at Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in New Delhi. Madhya Pradesh athletes won four medals in total — two gold and two bronze — making the state one of the steadier individual performers at the meet. Waskale's gold on the second day was one of those four.

For Khargone district — and for the small village of Thikri where he grew up — his national open title is the clearest signal yet of what rural athletes from this part of Madhya Pradesh can reach when a structured programme is available to them. He arrived at the MP Academy as a teenager with speed and potential. He has left this particular championship a national open gold medallist.
