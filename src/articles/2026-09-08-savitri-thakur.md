On the morning of 8 September 2026, Savitri Thakur stood at Tanda Road station and watched a 12-coach MEMU train pull out of the platform. The moment she had worked towards for twelve years had arrived. The train — the first ever to reach the tribal heartland of Dhar district — connected communities in Kukshi, Jobat, and Alirajpur to the national rail network, eight decades after independence.

Thakur was born in Kalikiray village, Dhar district, and spent years working with Adivasi communities before entering politics. When she won the Dhar Lok Sabha seat in 2014, she joined the Consultative Committee for the Ministry of Railways with one priority: rail connectivity for a district the country's rail map had bypassed for generations.

The area she represents is among Madhya Pradesh's most geographically isolated tribal belts. Dhar, Alirajpur, Kukshi, and Jobat sit in hilly terrain that roads reach with difficulty. Traders, students, and patients have long faced expensive, time-consuming bus journeys to reach larger cities in MP or Gujarat. The Chhota Udepur-Dhar railway project — originally envisioned to eventually span **157 kilometres** — was the structural answer, but it moved through surveys, clearances, and budget cycles over many years.

## The 28 kilometres that changed everything

The **₹334-crore** Jobat-Tanda Road section, covering 28 kilometres of new track, is the first completed leg of the larger project. Prime Minister Narendra Modi virtually inaugurated the line from Vadodara on September 8 as part of a broader **₹35,000-crore** railway package. Thakur flagged off the daily 12-coach MEMU service at Tanda Road station, the new terminal that puts Dhar and Alirajpur's tribal towns on the national railway map.

The new train runs between Tanda Road and Vadodara Pratap Nagar, passing through Alirajpur, Kukshi, and Chhota Udaipur. For farm families taking produce to Gujarat markets, for students seeking colleges in larger cities, and for patients needing specialist care, the journey that once required hours by road now has a direct rail option.

Thakur credited the outcome to Prime Minister Modi's focus on infrastructure in tribal regions and to the engineering teams who laid track through difficult hill terrain.

> "This is not just a railway line. This is the first train connecting our people to opportunity."

## Why it matters to Dhar

Rail connectivity changes what is economically possible in a tribal district. Market access for agricultural produce improves when farmers can reach buyers in distant cities affordably. College enrolment in remote areas tends to rise when transit becomes available. For Alirajpur — one of Madhya Pradesh's poorest districts by economic indicators — the Tanda Road-Vadodara link is the kind of foundational infrastructure that precedes everything else: industry, tourism, educational migration, and the movement of goods.

For Savitri Thakur, September 8 is the first milestone, not the last. The Chhota Udepur-Dhar project has considerably more track still to build. But for the villages around Kukshi and Jobat that watched their first train this morning, twelve years of a constituency MP's persistence has become something they can ride.
