Two days after the 2026 Asian Games closed in Aichi-Nagoya, Madhya Pradesh Sports and Youth Affairs Minister Vishwas Sarang was in Bhopal to receive athletes home. On October 6, field hockey gold medallist Vivek Sagar Prasad and soft tennis bronze medallist Jay Meena arrived at the ministry, where Sarang met each of them personally — a formality that, under his tenure, has consistently come with a specific commitment rather than a handshake and a photograph.

Prasad had just completed his second consecutive Asian Games with a gold medal, anchoring India's midfield through a tournament run that ended with the national anthem playing in Aichi-Nagoya. Meena's bronze in soft tennis represented Madhya Pradesh's first podium in the discipline at the continent's premier multi-sport event. Both athletes credited their state government's sustained backing for keeping them in competitive programmes through the long preparation cycle.

Meena, speaking to reporters after the meeting, named his parents, mentors and the state government's consistent support as the reasons he reached the podium. Prasad said he was pleased to receive a warm reception following a second consecutive Games medal.

## A Year of Delivered Promises

The October 6 welcome was one point in a year-long pattern. When wrestler Yamini Maurya and boxer Ajay Babu returned from the 2026 Commonwealth Games with silver medals, Sarang announced **₹25 lakh each and government jobs** for both athletes — and followed through. The state has maintained a standing policy of rewarding international performances at major championships, and what distinguishes Sarang's tenure is the absence of the delays that sometimes separate an announcement from an appointment letter.

Madhya Pradesh sent **32 athletes** across disciplines to the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya. Their collective showing produced **eight medals for India** across shooting, sailing and soft tennis — a performance the Free Press Journal described as the strongest representation the state has put together for the continental event. Athletes from the state have also featured in India's gold-winning team campaigns in hockey and cricket at various tournaments this year.

Behind those results is a network that Sarang has been expanding: Khelo India centres, state sports academies and district-level talent identification programmes that have pushed Olympic and Asian Games disciplines beyond the traditional strengths of cricket. Shooting, wrestling, archery and hockey now have structured pathways from district competitions to national selection — a pipeline that did not exist in anything like its present form five years ago.

For the athletes who walked through the ministry doors on October 6, the welcome was more than ceremonial. Sarang's track record — consistent on rewards, consistent on follow-through — makes the meeting a reliable step in an athlete's return from an international event, not a one-off occasion. That reliability, more than any single announcement, is what Madhya Pradesh's sporting community has come to count on.
