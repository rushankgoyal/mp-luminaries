The Brilliant Convention Centre in Indore filled this morning with the names of players, coaches, and officials who have shaped cricket in Madhya Pradesh — and among the awards given, the one that carries the longest memory went to **Prashant Mehta**.

The **Madhavrao Scindia Lifetime Achievement Award**, named for the former BCCI president and Union Minister whose family has long been associated with MP cricket, was presented to Mehta at the **MPCA Annual Awards 2026** ceremony today. The award is reserved for administrators, coaches, selectors, and commentators who have given sustained service to the game — people whose contributions rarely show up in a scorecard but without whom the infrastructure of cricket does not function.

Mehta received a **silver plaque and a prize of ₹5 lakh**. He has served as a member of the MPCA managing committee and is the former President of the **Central Domestic Cricket Association (CDCA)**, where he worked to develop the game at the club and district levels — the tier below the state team where most careers are shaped or broken before they even reach Ranji Trophy selection.

## A Season of Recognition for MP Cricket

The ceremony distributed **₹2.94 crore in total prize money** to players and officials across categories — a figure that reflects how seriously the MPCA has invested in rewarding performance and service in recent seasons. Chief guests were **Javagal Srinath**, the ICC Match Referee and former India pace bowler, and **Union Minister Jyotiraditya Scindia**, in whose family's name the lifetime achievement award is given.

Scindia described the MPCA as a "temple of cricket," a phrase that lands differently now than it might have a few years ago. Madhya Pradesh's **Ranji Trophy title in 2022** — the state's first — changed what the rest of the country expected from this team. In the seasons since, MP has consistently produced players who reach the IPL and national selection conversation: Rajat Patidar, Avesh Khan, Kumar Kartikeya. That pipeline exists because of the administrative work done at every level, from club grounds to district associations to the state board.

Prashant Mehta's decades at the CDCA are part of that foundation. District cricket associations are often unglamorous work — organising tournaments with limited budgets, maintaining grounds, running selection panels that will disappoint more players than they please. The fact that MP cricketers now arrive at Ranji level better prepared owes something to the structures those administrators maintained during the years when MP's national profile was far smaller.

The lifetime achievement award recognises exactly that kind of long service — not the moment of triumph but the years of unglamorous upkeep that make triumph possible.
