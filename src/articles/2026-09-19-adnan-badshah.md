The 20th Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, opened on September 19, 2026. India has sent **502 athletes** across 35 sports — and among them is Adnan Mohammed Badshah, known in competitive esports by his tag "Badshahh," who has traveled to Japan as the captain of India's Pokémon UNITE squad. He is from Dewas, a city of about two million people in Madhya Pradesh, roughly 35 kilometres from Indore. He is the only athlete from the state in this year's Indian esports contingent.

The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports approved India's 15-member esports delegation in late August 2026. Badshah leads a six-player squad that will compete in Pokémon UNITE, a team-based multiplayer strategy game that pits two five-member teams against each other in real-time matches. The remaining squad members come from Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and Gujarat — Badshah, holding the captain's armband, is the anchor from central India.

## Esports Arrives as a Full Medal Sport

Esports was introduced as a demonstration event at the 2022 Hangzhou Asian Games, giving the format a significant international platform. At Aichi-Nagoya 2026, it returns as a full medal discipline. Eleven titles are contested, including Pokémon UNITE, League of Legends, Gran Turismo 7, eFootball, and Puyo Puyo Champions, with the esports competitions running from September 23 to October 2.

India's 15-member contingent spans multiple titles, making this the country's largest organised presence at an international esports event. The government's decision to formally recognise and fund the delegation — through the same ministry that supports Olympic sports — signals a shift in how India views competitive gaming.

For Badshah, competing at the Asian Games is the culmination of years spent building a competitive career in Pokémon UNITE. He has established himself as one of India's leading players in the title, and his selection as captain reflects both his skill and his standing within the national esports community.

The recognition carries particular significance for Madhya Pradesh. The state's sporting achievements are more commonly celebrated in athletics, hockey, archery and traditional disciplines. Badshah's selection is a reminder that talent in newer competitive arenas is emerging from the same cities and towns that have long produced athletes in other fields.

As India's team takes the arena in Aichi-Nagoya later this month, Adnan Badshah will play not just for a medal but for the visibility of a sport that is still working to earn its place in the public imagination. Having a Dewas-born captain leading the charge is a small but clear signal that India's esports future is being built from the ground up, across the country.
