The Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy's junior women came to the final of the **4th Hockey India Women Academy Championship 2026** in Gwalior having won every match that mattered. The last one was the hardest. In a final against Pritam Siwach Hockey Academy from Sonepat — the same opponents that the academy's sub-junior team faced in a simultaneous final — they needed a 54th-minute goal from their captain to seal the result.

Kanak Pal scored it. Final score: **MP Hockey Academy 3, Pritam Siwach Hockey Academy 2**. Junior Women's title won. And when the sub-junior women claimed their own title in a shootout hours earlier, Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy had completed a **double championship sweep** — winning both the Sub-Junior and Junior categories on the same day at the same national tournament, against the same opponent in both finals.

The nine-day tournament, which brought twelve academies from across India to Gwalior in the Junior Women's category, concluded on **3 August 2026**. The championship final was the last fixture on the schedule.

## A Tournament-Long Performance

Kanak Pal's contribution to the Junior Women's championship stretched well beyond the 54th minute of the final. In the pool stage, she was the driving force of the academy's attack. She scored **two goals** in a **14-0** win over Mumbai Schools Sports Association and found the net in other pool matches, accumulating the kind of consistent output that builds a team's confidence across a long tournament week.

As captain, her role was not only to score. Academy hockey at the junior level demands that a captain hold the team's shape across nine days, against academies that have specifically prepared for each opponent, in back-to-back fixtures that wear down depth. The MP junior women's run included wins over academies from multiple states before reaching the final, and Kanak Pal was the player whose name kept appearing on the scoresheet and whose leadership shaped how the squad carried itself from match to match.

When the final arrived, the task was straightforward in conception and difficult in practice: stay in front long enough to close out Pritam Siwach, who had already proven they could compete at this level by reaching the same final. Kanak Pal scored the **54th-minute goal** that made the score **3-2** and held it there. The Sonepat side had no answer in the remaining minutes.

## What the Double Means for the Academy

The Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy's women's programme operates under the Department of Sports and Youth Welfare, Government of Madhya Pradesh, with residential training and competitive tournament access in Gwalior. Its consistent production of national-level players reflects years of investment in the programme's structure.

The sub-junior team's win and the junior team's win are separate achievements from separate squads. But both came at the same tournament, on the same day, against the same opponent in both finals — a coincidence that amplifies the significance of the result. For the academy's coaches and staff, the double championship on August 3 is evidence that the programme is producing not just individual players but complete, competitive squads at multiple age levels simultaneously.

For Kanak Pal, it validates a captain's season: consistent in pool play, decisive in the final, and the player who crossed the finishing line. The trophy is the team's; the 54th-minute goal is hers.
