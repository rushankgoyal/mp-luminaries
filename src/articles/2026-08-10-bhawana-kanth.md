On 6 August 2026, at the Tactics and Air Combat Development Establishment in Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, Squadron Leader Bhawana Kanth completed the Fighter Combat Leader course — and became the first woman in the Indian Air Force's history to do so. The FCL course is the IAF's most rigorous and exclusive professional programme for fighter pilots. Fewer than one per cent of the service's pilots are selected for it.

The moment is the latest in a string of milestones that Bhawana Kanth has been building since 2016, when she was commissioned as part of India's first-ever batch of women fighter pilots alongside Avani Chaturvedi and Mohana Singh. She grew up in Begusarai, Bihar, the daughter of an Indian Oil Corporation engineer, and graduated in Medical Electronics Engineering from BMS College of Engineering in Bengaluru before joining the IAF.

In March 2018 she flew her first solo on the MiG-21 Bison. In May 2019 she became the first Indian woman cleared for daytime fighter combat missions. In January 2021 she made history again as the first woman fighter pilot to fly in the Republic Day parade. Each milestone seemed remarkable on its own. The FCL graduation in Gwalior puts them all in a new light — it shows a pilot who has not just entered the fighter stream but climbed to its most demanding peak.

## What the Fighter Combat Leader Course Requires

TACDE — the Tactics and Air Combat Development Establishment — is the IAF's centre for advanced warfighting instruction, often compared to the United States Navy's TOPGUN programme. Pilots who arrive there are already experienced fighter aircrew. The FCL course then puts them through **20 weeks** of concentrated training in advanced air combat strategies, mission planning, air-to-air and air-to-ground warfare, and the command skills needed to lead other fighter pilots in high-intensity operations.

The course does not simply make a better pilot; it produces a Combat Leader — someone qualified to design tactics and train the next generation of aircrew. The selection bar is set deliberately high: roughly **1 in 100** IAF fighter pilots ever attends. Graduates leave equipped to pass what they have learned through the entire fighter fleet.

Bhawana Kanth is the first woman to clear all of it.

It has now been ten years since India opened the fighter pilot stream to women. In that decade, three women joined a force that did not previously have space for them. They logged hours, proved their competence, and steadily earned wider responsibilities. The FCL course, with its near-impossible selection rate and its central place in the IAF's professional hierarchy, was always going to be the clearest test of whether the door that opened in 2016 led somewhere real.

On 6 August 2026, Bhawana Kanth showed that it does. She graduates from TACDE not as a symbol of what might be possible for women in combat aviation, but as a qualified Fighter Combat Leader in the Indian Air Force — one of a small, elite group in the entire service.
