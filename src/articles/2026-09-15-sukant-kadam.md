Twelve years ago, Sukant Kadam came to para badminton the way people often come to something that changes their life: through a door that grief opened. He had spent his childhood as a cricketer in Kautholi, Sangli, Maharashtra. A knee injury at age ten led to multiple surgeries. When the procedures were done, his left leg could no longer bend. Cricket, the sport he had grown up loving, was finished.

In 2012, watching the London Paralympics on television, he saw high-jumper Girisha Nagarajegowda leap to a silver medal for India. The moment was enough. If Girisha could stand on a Paralympic podium, so could he. Sukant picked up a badminton racket.

On 13 September 2026, at the Japan Para Badminton International in Tokyo, he collected his **100th career medal**.

## A Tally Built Over 12 Years

Sukant competes in the SL4 category — athletes with lower-limb impairment who stand while playing — and has risen to world number two in Men's Singles. He won his first BWF Para Badminton International medal in 2016 and has been a fixture at major tournaments ever since, accumulating silvers, bronzes, and golds across BWF Super Series, international-level, and continental competitions.

At the Japan tournament, he reached the Men's Singles SL4 final, where France's Lucas Mazur outplayed him convincingly in the first game, 21–08. Sukant regrouped and pushed harder in the second, but Mazur held his composure to close it out 21–16.

In Men's Doubles SL3-SU5, Sukant partnered Pramod Bhagat — a Paralympic gold medallist and one of the best para badminton players India has ever produced. The Indian pair faced Indonesia's Hikmat Ramdani and Fredy Setiawan in the final and lost 21–05, 21–15.

Two silvers in a single day is a strong performance. But the number at the end of it — medal 100 — is what Sukant will carry home.

> "Reaching 100 career medals is a very special moment for me. When I started playing para badminton 12 years ago, I never imagined that I would reach this milestone."
> — Sukant Kadam, September 2026

## Next: the Asian Games

With the Asian Games on the horizon, Sukant's goal is clear. Para badminton at that level is among the most competitive circuits he will face: China, Japan, and Indonesia are powers in the SL4 category, and a gold medal there would be the kind of result that finishes the arc he started as a teenage cricketer who had just run out of options.

For Indian para sport more broadly, Sukant's milestone matters as a proof of what consistent commitment produces. Not every milestone comes in a gold-medal final. Some come in the two silvers won on the same day, after a hard decade of showing up at tournaments in Japan, Europe, and across Asia — and being the person, every time, who gets to the final.

A hundred medals later, Sukant is still setting sights forward.
