Jay Meena was once the boy who collected balls on the soft tennis court at Pioneer Public School in Dewas, Madhya Pradesh. He watched the players, picked up stray balls, and eventually borrowed a racket. On September 23, 2026, he used one to win India's **first-ever soft tennis medal at an Asian Games**.

The bronze, in men's singles at the Aichi-Nagoya Games in Japan, came after Meena beat the Philippines' Sherwin Nuguit **4-3** in the quarterfinal — recovering from **3-5 down** in the deciding game to win it **7-5**. In the semifinal he faced Japan's Takuya Kurosaka and lost 4-2. In soft tennis, both losing semifinalists receive a bronze medal. Meena had battled a hip problem through the tournament but pushed through.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi congratulated him on the result, writing that it "opens a new and memorable chapter for soft tennis in India."

## From Dewas to the Asian Games Podium

Soft tennis — a version of the game played with a pressurised rubber ball — is popular across East and South-East Asia, with Japan and Korea producing the world's top players. For most of its history in India, the sport has had minimal visibility. A state-level player from Madhya Pradesh winning an Asian Games medal was not, until now, something that had happened.

Meena grew up wanting to be a cricketer. That changed at Pioneer Public School, where a soft tennis court happened to sit on the grounds. After watching and helping as a ball boy, he started training in earnest. By age **14** he was already competing internationally in Japan, the sport's home country.

The results followed steadily. At the **2022 National Games** in Gujarat, he won the men's singles gold. The same year, he reached the quarterfinals at the Hangzhou Asian Games. In **2024**, he became the first Indian to win a World Soft Tennis Championships medal. In **2025**, he won the national championship and took a singles bronze at the Asian Soft Tennis Championships.

At Nagoya, his path to the podium ran through one of the most dramatic moments of the Games: a quarterfinal that he was close to losing before winning the final game **7-5** after being down **3-5**.

> "I want to dedicate this medal to my coaches, the Sports Authority of India, and everyone who supported me through the journey."
> — Jay Meena, after the Aichi-Nagoya bronze (ANI)

## A Medal That Changes What Seems Possible

Soft tennis in India runs on a thin margin. There are few full-time coaches, limited funding, and almost no professional league infrastructure. That a player from Dewas — a city of roughly 1.5 million in western Madhya Pradesh, not historically associated with racket sports — reached an Asian Games podium in this discipline says something about what self-directed persistence can produce.

Meena has said his next aim is Olympic inclusion for soft tennis. The sport is not currently on the Olympic programme, but its Asian Games status keeps the pathway open. For now, the **bronze medal** sits as proof that Dewas produced something the sport had not seen from India before.
