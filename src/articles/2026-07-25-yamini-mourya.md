Judo at the **2026 Commonwealth Games** begins on the tatami of Glasgow's SEC Centre on **31 July**, and among the names on India's start list is one that Madhya Pradesh fans will want to mark: **Yamini Mourya**, 28, from Sagar, enters the women's **-57 kg** draw after a 2025–26 season that put her form beyond reasonable doubt.

She has not always been easy to find in national headlines. Judo in India draws most of its media attention to weight categories with Olympic medal prospects or to athletes attached to the most prominent training centres. Mourya has built her career more quietly — grinding through the national circuit year after year, accumulating titles, then, in the last twelve months, collecting international results that could no longer be ignored.

## Three International Events, Two Golds

Mourya was born on **26 March 1998** in Sagar, a city set on the Sagar Lake in the elevated plateau of central Madhya Pradesh. She first appeared on continental scoreboards in **2015**, winning bronze at the Asian U18 Championships in Bangkok at age seventeen — a debut that signalled intent but did not come with immediate follow-through at the senior level.

What followed were years of consistent national work. She became a **multi-time senior national champion** in the -57 kg category, and at the **36th National Games in Gujarat in 2022**, she won the gold medal in the final, defeating Haryana's Savitri. In **2023**, she stepped onto a global stage: **bronze** at the FISU World University Games in Chengdu, China — her first senior world-stage result and the clearest sign yet that her reach extended beyond domestic circuits.

Then came the sequence that made her Commonwealth Games selection look inevitable. In 2025, she won **gold** at the Asian Open in Kowloon, Hong Kong, and **silver** at the Asian Open in Taipei. She carried that form into 2026, claiming **gold** at the African Open in Dakar, Senegal. Three separate international events across two continents, two golds in the most recent competitive cycle. The Axis Bank Judo Development Programme, run in partnership with the Inspire Institute of Sport (IIS), supported her preparation through this period, and her selection for the Glasgow squad followed directly from that form.

India's judo competition at CWG 2026 runs from 31 July to 2 August at the SEC Centre. Mourya will compete in the -57 kg draw as one of her country's more experienced athletes on the international circuit — backed not by reputation but by a result sheet that makes her presence at Glasgow wholly earned.

For Sagar, the distinction is an uncommon one. The city has a central university, a lake that defines its identity, and now a native daughter competing for India at a Commonwealth Games — at the peak of her career, in the best form she has ever shown.
