At Lok Bhavan in Dehradun on the morning of **19 October 2026**, Dr. Dhal Singh Bisen stood before Chief Justice Manoj Kumar Tiwari of the Uttarakhand High Court and took the oath of office as the state's ninth Governor. Uttarakhand Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami and other dignitaries were present as Bisen was administered the constitutional oath. When the ceremony ended, he said what he had been thinking since President Droupadi Murmu announced his appointment a week earlier: "Heartfelt gratitude to serve Devbhoomi."

For Madhya Pradesh, the moment marks the elevation of a man who spent three decades in the state's assembly halls, cabinet rooms and planning committees — a career built not in Delhi but in constituencies and offices across MP.

## From Seoni's forests to four assembly terms

Dr. Dhal Singh Bisen was born on **14 May 1952** in Pipariya village in Seoni district — a region of dense sal forests and tribal communities in east-central Madhya Pradesh. Trained in Ayurvedic medicine with an A.V.M.S. and D.H.B. from Bhopal, he worked as a medical practitioner and farmer before entering politics. His assembly constituency was **Barghat** in Seoni.

He was first elected to the Madhya Pradesh Legislative Assembly in **1990** and won the Barghat seat again in **1993**, **1998** and **2003** — four consecutive victories in a region that blends Gondi-speaking communities with small-town BJP support. After the 2003 assembly election, when the BJP formed the government, Bisen entered the cabinet and served as Minister for **School Education, Forest, Transport and Mines** — a portfolio set that covered the state's rural schoolrooms, its vast tree cover and its road network.

He continued to contribute to state planning as Chairman of the **Fourth MP State Finance Commission** from 2011 to 2013, the body that recommends how resources flow from the state to its local bodies. In 2019, he moved to Parliament, winning the **Balaghat Lok Sabha seat** on a BJP ticket, and served until the Lok Sabha dissolved ahead of the 2024 general election.

## What the appointment means

A Governor's role is constitutional rather than executive — convening the assembly, assenting to bills, forwarding grievances to the Centre — but the depth of experience a Governor brings to Raj Bhavan shapes how effectively that office functions. Uttarakhand, the young hill state formed in 2000, shares with Madhya Pradesh's forested districts an economy built on forests, rivers and tribal communities. Bisen's direct experience with forest policy and rural administration as a state minister brings practical understanding to those conversations.

At the swearing-in ceremony, Bisen pledged to uphold constitutional values, maintain the dignity of the Governor's office, and work to assist Uttarakhand's elected government. He replaces Lieutenant General Gurmit Singh (retired), who had served as the state's Governor since September 2021.

At 74, with a career that began in a village clinic in Seoni and moved through four assembly terms, two finance roles and a Lok Sabha seat, he arrives in Devbhoomi with one of the more grounded résumés any Raj Bhavan has received.
