India's canoe slalom team at the 2025 Asian Canoe Slalom Junior and U23 Championships in Rayong, Thailand, included three paddlers from the same training ground: Lower Lake, Bhopal. One of them was 16-year-old Pallavi Jagtap. She returned from Rayong with a U18 women's individual C1 silver medal, a team silver in the K-1 Junior event, and a team bronze in the C-1 U23 Women's category — a haul from a single tournament that told coaches a great deal about what she was capable of next.

She is now 17. Three months after Rayong, she was on the water again at the 2025 Asian Canoe Slalom Championship in Guizhou, China, this time as part of an Indian trio that included Shikha Chauhan and Reena Sen. The three of them — all products of the Madhya Pradesh State Water Sports Academy — won India a **team silver**, the first international team silver India had earned in canoe slalom. That result made national headlines in a sport rarely covered by the mainstream press, and the fact that all three came from the same Bhopal institution made it something rarer still: a story about a system working.

## What Lower Lake Produces

The Madhya Pradesh State Water Sports Academy sits alongside Lower Lake in Bhopal — an urban water body that, from the outside, has none of the drama of a whitewater course. Inside the academy, athletes spend years developing the technical precision that canoe slalom demands: reading water, anticipating gate sequences, and building the boat-handling feel that cannot be taught in a classroom.

Pallavi Jagtap came up through this system. The journey from a quiet training lake in the heart of MP to the medal tables of the 2025 Asian Junior Championships is a short geography and a long discipline. She negotiated both in the time it typically takes a young athlete to complete one qualifying cycle.

Her individual U18 silver in Thailand was particularly notable because C1 — canoe solo — is a boat in which the paddler faces the course alone with a single-bladed paddle, and every gate decision is hers to make in real time. Team events allow the group to compensate for individual hesitation; C1 does not. Pallavi Jagtap winning silver in that format, at 16, against the full field of Asian junior competition, was a different kind of result from a team medal.

In July 2026, Sports and Youth Welfare Minister Vishwas Sarang met with the 22 Madhya Pradesh athletes who have qualified for the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan. Pallavi Jagtap is among them — confirmed for the biggest multi-sport event in Asia, alongside canoe slalom athletes from the same Bhopal academy that first put her in a boat.

The Asian Games take place in September and October 2026. India has never won a canoe slalom medal at the Asian Games. The three young women from Bhopal's lake — Jagtap, Chauhan, and Sen — have been identified as India's best chance of changing that.

At 17, Pallavi Jagtap will arrive in Aichi-Nagoya having already competed at the continental level in two separate international championships and converted those appearances into medals. Whether she adds a third in Japan or not, the trajectory is clear: the Bhopal training lake, the long hours reading slow water, and the two Asian medals already in her résumé have built an athlete who belongs on the course.
