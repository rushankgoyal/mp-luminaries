On August 13, in Vigyan Bhawan's central hall, President Droupadi Murmu placed a Tamrapatra in the hands of an 86-year-old man who has spent most of his life trying to answer a single question: what does Kathak actually look like when you trace it all the way back to its textual roots? The answer — developed over seven decades of practice, research and teaching — earned Puru Dadheech the **Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship**, the **Akademi Ratna**, India's highest lifetime honour in the performing arts.

Dadheech was born on 17 July 1939 in **Ujjain**, in what was then the Central India Agency. He began his formal training at fifteen, initially under Pt. Durga Prasad and Pt. Sunder Prasad, before coming under the guidance of **Shambhu Maharaj** — one of the three brothers who defined the Lucknow gharana of Kathak in the twentieth century. That connection gave Dadheech a direct thread into the gharana's tradition of refined *abhinaya* and meticulous footwork, a tradition he spent the next seven decades transmitting, extending and documenting.

He is also one of very few practitioners to hold a **doctorate in Kathak Classical Dance**, with his research centred on the Natyashastra and Abhinaya Darpana — the foundational Sanskrit treatises that govern gesture, expression and movement in Indian classical performance. That academic commitment showed up not just in his teaching but in his output: **thirteen books** on dance and dramaturgy that are now part of the Kathak curriculum at several Indian universities.

## A Career That Planted Roots in MP

Dadheech did not simply pass through Madhya Pradesh. He founded the **Natwari Kathak Nritya Academy in Indore**, establishing one of the state's earliest institutional homes for serious Kathak training. He later served as professor at Bhatkhande Music Institute University in Lucknow and as dean at Khairagarh University in Chhattisgarh, but his pedagogical roots run through MP. He also developed the **Dadheech style**, a distinct strand within the Lucknow gharana that foregrounds the Shastraic framework without abandoning the emotional suppleness that distinguishes the style.

The Akademi Ratna is an honour with a hard ceiling. The Sangeet Natak Akademi restricts its active fellowship to forty living artists at any one time, which means the honour is conferred only when a vacancy appears. Dadheech was one of **seven artists elected by the General Council in March 2026** — and, at 86, arguably the senior-most among them. His earlier recognitions include the **Padma Shri**, the **Kalidas Samman** from the Government of Madhya Pradesh, the **Tagore National Fellowship** and a prior Sangeet Natak Akademi Award. Guided ten doctoral students to their degrees, and now heads the Sri Sri Centre for Advanced Research in Kathak at Sri Sri University, Cuttack.

The fellowship completes a record that is, by any measure, definitive. For Madhya Pradesh, a state whose classical music and dance heritage traces directly to the courts of Gwalior and Ujjain, Dadheech's recognition is a reminder that that heritage is still being actively tended — not only preserved.
