At Lord's Cricket Ground in July 2026, a 23-year-old right-arm pacer from Ghuwara, a small town in Sidhi district, Madhya Pradesh, bowled in only her second Test match and took five wickets for 35 runs. That performance does not sound, in isolation, like a moment for the history books — until you learn that no woman had ever taken a five-wicket haul in a Test at Lord's before. Kranti Gaud's name now sits on the Lord's honours board, a distinction that has eluded some of the greatest cricketers India has produced.

Cricket writers who watched the innings described Gaud bowling with certainty. She moved the ball in London's overcast conditions, held her length, built pressure and finished with figures that left England's batting searching for answers. India bowled England out for 170. The match was India's, and the morning belonged to Gaud.

## From Ghuwara to the Lord's Pavilion

Ghuwara is a small town in Sidhi district — a part of Madhya Pradesh better known for its forests and hills than for producing international cricketers. Gaud was born there on August 11, 2003, and came through the Madhya Pradesh women's cricket pathway, a route that is narrower and less resourced than its male counterpart but one that has, in recent seasons, delivered talent ready for the highest level.

She made her international debut in 2024 and has since become a fixture in India's pace attack. The India Women's tour of England in 2026 included both the ICC Women's T20 World Cup and a one-off Test; Gaud was part of the squad for both. After the T20 tournament concluded, India faced England in the Test at Lord's — one of cricket's most storied grounds, where the pavilion's honours board records the names of players who have taken five wickets in an innings. No woman's name had appeared on it before July 2026. Gaud changed that.

She also plays for UP Warriorz in the Women's Premier League, one of the professional structures built in recent years to give Indian women cricketers the sustained competition they need to develop. Her path from Sidhi district to the Lord's honours board — in fewer than two years of international cricket — is exceptional by any measure.

For Madhya Pradesh, a state whose cricket story has been told through Ranji Trophy triumphs and a steady production of domestic talent, Kranti Gaud extends that story in a direction it has rarely gone: into women's cricket, into international Test matches, and into the kind of history that is not broken every season. Her performance at Lord's belongs to a small list of genuinely unprecedented things — and it came from one of MP's most remote corners.
