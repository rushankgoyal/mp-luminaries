The village of Silphodkheda has fewer roads than fields and sits in the agricultural sprawl of Dewas district, about 75 kilometres from Bhopal. Dev Kumar Meena grew up there, in a farming household, with a clear picture of what an athletic career from Madhya Pradesh could look like: his cousin Jay Meena had represented India in soft tennis at the Asian Games, and that single example was enough to make the idea feel real.

By the time Dev was 15, that idea had found an unlikely vehicle — a fibreglass pole. He had been spotted in November 2020 by the Madhya Pradesh government's talent-search programme, initially enrolled for sprint training in Bhopal. But coach Ghanshyam Yadav at the MP Athletics Academy watched him move and had a different thought: the boy's build, his explosive coordination, was made for the vault. Dev tried it. He never went back to the track.

## Five Times He Has Lifted the Bar

What followed is a record-breaking run that is unusual in Indian athletics for its consistency. Dev has improved the national pole vault record **five times** across two seasons.

In 2025 he set two national marks. At the Federation Cup in Ranchi on **25 May 2026**, competing alongside training partner Kuldeep Kumar, both men simultaneously cleared **5.45 metres** — a new joint national record. Six weeks later, at the **65th National Inter-State Senior Athletics Championships** in Bhubaneswar on **24 June**, Dev cleared **5.46 metres**, going one centimetre further and claiming the outright record.

That clearance also booked his place in the Indian squad for the **Asian Games 2026**. The Athletics Federation of India confirmed the 73-member athletics contingent on **23 August**, with Dev and Kuldeep Kumar named for the men's pole vault. The Games begin in **Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, on 19 September**.

In between, Dev made his Commonwealth Games debut in Glasgow in August, competing against athletes who have trained in European facilities for years. He finished fourth in the final after clearing **5.30 metres** — just outside the medals, but a result that confirmed he belongs in the conversation at the highest level.

He is 21 years old, pursuing a Bachelor of Arts degree at Khalsa College in Amritsar, and has worked with international coach Ángel García to refine his technique. A decade ago, Madhya Pradesh rarely produced field-event athletes who competed at this level. Dev is part of a small group changing that.

The Asian Games pole vault record stands at 5.90 metres. Dev is not expected to win. But a young man from Silphodkheda who started vaulting at 15 and has broken the national record five times has already demonstrated that expectation is not always the best guide to what he will do next.
