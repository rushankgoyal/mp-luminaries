The convention floor at Laghu Udyog Bharati's Udyam Samagam-2026 in Indore on October 4 was packed with small-business owners, cluster operators and manufacturers who had come to hear what the state government planned to do about the cost of space. For many of them, the price of setting up inside a formal industrial area — land preparation, utilities, access roads — had been the single biggest barrier to formalising their operations. Madhya Pradesh MSME Minister Chetanya Kashyap had a specific answer.

The government would offer a grant of **₹800 per square foot** to entrepreneurs developing new private industrial areas for small-scale industries. In addition, a **subsidy of up to 50 per cent** had been approved for the establishment of new small-scale units. Both schemes, Kashyap announced, would be processed through a simplified online system with a turnaround of approximately two months — a deliberate signal that approvals would not pile up in departmental queues.

For a minister who has been building MSME infrastructure since taking office in December 2023, the Indore announcement was one instrument in a much larger programme.

## The Architecture of a Roadmap

Madhya Pradesh currently has more than **25 lakh registered MSME units**, placing it among the leading states in the country. Kashyap's three-year roadmap targets **₹5,000 crore in incentives**, approval for **30 new private clusters**, and the establishment of **22 Common Facility Centres** — shared infrastructure giving smaller manufacturers access to machinery, testing equipment and certification facilities they could not afford to own individually. To back those commitments, the state's MSME budget has been **doubled from ₹1,100 crore to ₹2,100 crore**.

The longer horizon is more ambitious still. Kashyap has set a target of **one crore registered MSME units** in Madhya Pradesh by 2047 — growing from 25 lakh today. The startup ecosystem is expected to develop in parallel, with 6,000 registered startups targeted to double to 12,000, backed by 100 new incubation centres being established across districts.

In an interview with The Organiser in February 2026, Kashyap described the state's direction plainly: "Agriculture strengthened our foundation; industry will define our future." He was speaking about an economy where farming has historically driven growth but where industrial investment is now arriving at a pace that requires structured channels, not improvised responses.

The ₹800-per-square-foot grant announced at Udyam Samagam-2026 addresses a constraint that has kept capable entrepreneurs outside the formal economy. Private industrial clusters, when well-located and properly serviced, generate employment more durably than large anchor factories — and they scale in ways that individual units cannot. In a state building toward one crore MSME units, that is infrastructure, not incentive. Kashyap's particular contribution has been to put that distinction into policy, budget it, and show up in Indore to explain it directly to the people it is designed to help.
