On September 29 at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, India's women's trap team — Neeru Dhanda, Aashima Ahlawat, and Manisha Keer — shot a combined **328 points** to win silver, finishing behind China (346) and ahead of Kuwait (325). For Aashima, a 22-year-old from Bhopal, it was a first Asian Games medal. The margin between silver and bronze was three points. The margin between silver and gold was eighteen.

Aashima has been inside the Indian national shooting setup for several years, supported by the Target Olympic Podium Scheme (TOPS) — the Sports Authority of India's programme for athletes with a credible path to a Games podium. At the 2025 Asian Shooting Championships in Shymkent, Kazakhstan, she won a team gold alongside her Indian teammates and added an individual bronze with a score of 29 in the final. Those results positioned her clearly in Asia's upper tier of women's trap shooters before the Games began.

At Aichi-Nagoya, she did not advance to the individual final, finishing sixteenth in qualification. But the team event is a different calculation: it rewards consistency across all three shooters, and Aashima's 106 points anchored the squad's middle bracket between Neeru Dhanda's leading 118 and Manisha Keer's 104. Without that contribution, the three-point gap over Kuwait disappears.

## What 328 Points Required

Women's trap at the Asian Games asks each shooter to hit clay pigeons launched at unpredictable angles from a bunker. Over the team qualification round, consistency matters more than a single peak performance, because one shooter's low score drags the total. India's 328 was built on all three athletes holding close to their respective levels — not one exceptional and two mediocre, but three reliable.

The Free Press Journal's coverage of the Games noted that Madhya Pradesh athletes contributed significantly to India's eight-medal haul from Aichi-Nagoya. Aashima's silver is part of that count. Neeru Dhanda, who led the squad with 118, trains out of Shivpuri. Manisha Keer also has MP connections. The overlap is not coincidental: the state's shooting infrastructure and the TOPS system together have made this kind of team possible.

After the medal, Aashima told reporters that the gold had been within reach. "The margins weren't that big," she said. A gap of eighteen points across an entire team round is, in fact, within a shooter's range to close. That awareness — alongside a silver medal at 22 — is as good a foundation as any for the cycle that follows.
