The seventh edition of MP Travel Mart opened in Bhopal on October 7, 2026, and the number that set the tone for the four-day event was not a projection — it was the actual count of tourist visits Madhya Pradesh recorded in 2025: **21.03 crore**, nearly double the 11.19 crore of 2023. The officer who put that figure before the assembled buyers, sellers, and officials from **34 countries** was **Dr. Ilayaraja T.**, the IAS officer who serves simultaneously as Secretary of the Tourism Department, Managing Director of the MP State Tourism Development Corporation, and Secretary to the Chief Minister.

More than **600 delegates** registered for the event, with **10,000 business meetings** scheduled across the four days — the kind of volume that makes MP Travel Mart the single largest tourism procurement platform the state has built. Investment proposals on the table total **₹1,100 crore**, spread across hotels, wildlife lodges, wellness retreats, and eco-tourism circuits.

## From Letters of Award to Live Projects

The event is not only a showcase — it is where deals move from proposal to commitment. In this edition, **Letters of Award worth ₹252 crore** were issued for **14 tourism projects**: hotel developments, a golf resort in Raisen, and a wellness retreat in Khandwa, among others. The issuance of LoAs at the Mart itself is a design feature, ensuring that operators who make the trip to Bhopal leave with something actionable rather than a brochure.

Dr. Ilayaraja T. is 2009-batch IAS, MP cadre. He took charge of his current tourism portfolio in May 2026, arriving from a stint as District Collector of Indore — India's cleanest city by the Swachh Survekshan rankings — where he worked on waste management and urban energy reform in one of the state's most complex administrative environments. In the months since, he has led the department through two major national platforms: the FAITH Conclave in New Delhi in July, and now MP Travel Mart at home in Bhopal.

The pitch he is making to international tour operators is grounded in geography. Madhya Pradesh sits at the centre of India, within reasonable reach of both the northern cultural circuit and the southern coast, and its travel inventory is unusually broad: tiger reserves, Mughal-era forts, UNESCO heritage sites, Narmada river ghats, and a growing network of homestays in tribal areas. The strategic framing MP Tourism uses — **Heart of Incredible India** — positions the state not as an alternative circuit but as the connective tissue between the destinations that global visitors already know.

The scale of growth the department is now managing — from 11 crore to 21 crore visits in three years — means the infrastructure question is no longer theoretical. MP Travel Mart 2026 is where the answer is being built, one LoA at a time.
