On 4 August 2026, at the RCVP Noronha Academy of Administration and Management in Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh's Higher Education Department hosted a day-long national workshop on advancing curriculum and emerging knowledge areas. When the formal proceedings ended, the state walked away with **30 signed memorandums of understanding** with some of India's most prestigious technical institutions — Indian Institutes of Technology, National Institutes of Technology, and premier research universities.

Madhya Pradesh Higher Education and Technical Education Minister Inder Singh Parmar drove the effort. The workshop, titled "Emerging Knowledge Areas, Advanced Curriculum: Vision, Direction and Implementation," was designed not just as a ceremony but as a working session to map what Madhya Pradesh's colleges need to offer students who will enter an economy reshaped by artificial intelligence, robotics, and advanced manufacturing.

Parmar has represented Shujalpur in Shajapur district in the Madhya Pradesh Legislative Assembly three times under the Bharatiya Janata Party, and took charge of the Higher Education portfolio in December 2023. Before that he served as the State Minister for School Education and played a key role in pushing the National Education Policy 2020 into classrooms across the state. The 4 August agreements are his most visible effort yet at the higher-education level.

## What the 30 Agreements Cover

The MOUs commit **30 Madhya Pradesh government colleges and universities** to structured partnerships with national institutions that have expertise in technology, research and innovation. The Indian Institute of Technology (Banaras Hindu University), Varanasi, signed a five-year agreement that will include joint programmes, faculty development, and startup incubation — with a specific focus on AI, robotics, quantum technologies and renewable energy.

Across the broader package of agreements, the themes are consistent: bring emerging fields into MP's classrooms, connect state faculty with national research communities, and give students pathways from their local college into national-level laboratories and incubators.

The SWAYAM online course platform is already mandatory for students across MP's universities, building a digital learning layer into every degree programme. The 30 partnerships add live institutional relationships on top of that — mentorship, joint research, faculty exchanges — that no online platform can replicate.

Madhya Pradesh has steadily climbed the country's ranking for NEP 2020 implementation, a policy that explicitly asks states to connect higher education with research, skill development and the needs of a technology-driven economy. The 30 MoUs convert that ambition into formal agreements with specific institutions and specific timelines.

For a student sitting in a government engineering college in Sagar or Rewa today, the immediate result may not be visible. But the agreements they represent will shape what courses are available, what research projects they can join, and whether a faculty member at IIT ever visits their campus. Those are the changes that raise the floor of what a government college degree means — and in a state where the majority of students depend on public institutions, raising that floor matters enormously.
