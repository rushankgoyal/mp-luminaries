On the morning of September 1, Kushabhau Thakre Auditorium in Bhopal filled with more than a thousand young faces — students who had scored 75 per cent or above in their Class 12 board examinations on their first attempt. Chief Minister Dr. Mohan Yadav walked onto the stage, pressed a button, and in that single click transferred **₹304.98 crore** into the accounts of **1.21 lakh meritorious students** across Madhya Pradesh.

Each student received **₹25,000** — enough to buy a laptop of their choice. The amount lands directly in their bank account, with no middleman and no form queue. For a girl from a small-town government school or a boy from a tribal district who has never owned a personal computer, that ₹25,000 is a genuine turning point.

## What the scheme does

The Pratibhashali Vidyarthi Protsahan Yojana — the state's meritorious student incentive programme — has run for over a decade, but the September 1 disbursement stands out for its scale. This is among the largest single-day transfers under the programme, covering students from all 55 districts simultaneously through direct benefit transfer (DBT).

Eligibility is deliberate: only first-attempt passers who clear the 75 per cent threshold qualify. The condition rewards consistent effort rather than repeated attempts, and the direct cash transfer lets families buy a device that matches what the student actually needs — a Windows laptop for engineering entrance prep, a tablet for graphic design, whatever the path demands.

At the ceremony, Yadav also symbolically handed laptops to a group of students on stage and spent time interacting with them about their aspirations. Several students were from the first generation in their family to complete higher secondary education.

The state has in recent years tied its education welfare programmes increasingly to DBT, reducing leakage and speeding delivery. The September 1 event was one more data point in that direction: a single authorisation, a single morning, and more than a lakh families woke up to an SMS confirming the credit.

## Why this matters

Madhya Pradesh has made consistent strides in Class 12 pass rates over the last several years, but digital access has lagged behind academic ambition, particularly in rural and semi-urban pockets. A student who clears board exams with distinction but cannot afford a computer is shut out of the competitive-exam economy, online learning platforms, and remote work opportunities that increasingly define the next decade.

The ₹25,000 cheque does not solve every barrier, but it removes the most immediate one. For the 1.21 lakh students who received it on September 1, the laptop they buy with it is not just hardware — it is the price of entry into a world their parents did not have access to.

For Madhya Pradesh, a state that set a 75-per-cent-first-attempt bar rather than a simpler one, it is also a signal about what the government considers worth rewarding: not just passing, but excelling, the first time.
