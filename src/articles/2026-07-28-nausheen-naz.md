On 25 July 2026, India's women's sub-junior hockey team faced China in the final of the FIH Youth Hockey5s Asian Championship in Muscat, Oman. Down three goals heading into the second half, the team clawed back. In the 13th minute, a 15-year-old striker from Seoni, Madhya Pradesh found the back of the net — cutting the deficit and reminding every coach in the arena where this team had come from over the previous five days.

India finished the match 2–4, losing the final to China. But the silver medal carried its own weight: by winning it, India secured a berth in the **FIH Youth Hockey5s World Cup**, placing a generation of young players on the global stage. Nausheen Naz is among them.

She grew up in Seoni, a district in Madhya Pradesh better known for its forests and wildlife sanctuaries than for producing international hockey players. Her father moved between livelihoods — selling vegetables, driving trucks — to keep the household running. There was no budget for a hockey stick when a ten-year-old Nausheen first fell in love with the sport. She found a broken one, took it to a local blacksmith who helped repair it, and went back to the field.

Her elder sister Tahoor Naz had played hockey, which planted the idea. Community pressure tried to uproot it: neighbours told her father that a girl from their community should not be playing sports. Her mother's position was different, and it held.

> "People who once told me not to play, now come to congratulate my family."

The shift happened gradually. Nausheen joined the Gwalior Women's Hockey Academy, where her instincts as a striker drew notice from coaches. From Gwalior, she made it to the National Coaching Camp at SAI Bhopal, where she now trains under the guidance of former India captain **Rani Rampal** — a direct line from Seoni's repaired stick to one of the finest hockey minds in the country.

## The Number That Made Selectors Look Twice

At the **16th Hockey India Sub Junior Women's National Championship 2026**, Nausheen Naz finished as the tournament's leading scorer with **nine goals** — the highest tally in the country at that level that year. The figure placed her in front of selectors choosing the squad for the Youth Hockey5s Asian Championship in Muscat. She was called up for her first senior international appearance.

She arrived in Oman as one of the youngest players in India's forward line. The tournament opened with an 8–3 win over Kazakhstan in the pool stage. India then defeated Kazakhstan again 8–1 in the semi-final to reach the final against China. The gold was China's — their precision in the opening twelve minutes built a lead India could not fully close. But Nausheen's goal in the 13th minute gave the match a different shape, a precise reminder that Seoni had sent someone who belongs at this level.

For Madhya Pradesh's hockey ecosystem — the Bhopal and Gwalior academies that have produced **29 national championship medals across 16 years** — Nausheen Naz represents the pattern working as it should: a child from a small district, a repaired stick, a pathway built by the state, and now a silver medal at an international championship.

The date of the FIH Youth Hockey5s World Cup has not yet been confirmed. When it arrives, a district that most of India cannot place on a map will be watching one of its own compete at the top of the game.
