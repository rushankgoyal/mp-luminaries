On 23 September 2026, the International Day of Sign Languages, Gauranshi Sharma from Bhopal was named among four extraordinary achievers from Madhya Pradesh's capital whose lives have been shaped by deafness and whose careers have refused to be limited by it. At 19, she has won **six international medals** in deaf badminton, been appointed UNICEF India's first Youth Advocate, and earned a place in India's squad for the **Asia Pacific Deaf Badminton Championship** in Penang, Malaysia, which begins on October 3.

Gauranshi was born with speech and hearing disabilities. Growing up in Bhopal, she discovered badminton in school and found that the shuttle moved exactly the same whether or not she could hear the crowd. She progressed through district and state competitions, earning selection to the Madhya Pradesh State Badminton Academy in Bhopal. The training there was the foundation, but her game shifted to a higher level when she moved to the **Gopichand Badminton Academy in Gwalior**, where she now trains under Indonesian coach Agus Dwi Samtoso alongside some of India's best able-bodied juniors. The transition to that environment was a statement in itself: there is no separate track for athletes who happen to be deaf.

## A Medal Collection Built Across Three Continents

The clearest measure of what Gauranshi has built is the medal tally. At the **2021 Summer Deaflympics in Brazil**, she won **gold in women's singles badminton**. The Deaflympics — organised by the International Committee of Sports for the Deaf — is the pinnacle of deaf sport, carrying the same prestige for deaf athletes that the Olympics carries for everyone else. Gauranshi won that gold medal.

She returned the following year and added a **team gold at the 2022 Summer Deaflympics**, also in Brazil. In 2023, competing at the **World Youth Deaf Badminton Championship in Brazil**, she won gold again. And in 2024, at the **10th Asia-Pacific Deaf Games in Kuala Lumpur**, she took a silver medal in the mixed team badminton event. Across international competition, she counts six medals in all: **two gold, one silver, and three bronze**. Across national competitions, she has accumulated thirteen more medals.

In October 2023, UNICEF India recognised her achievement both on and off the court by appointing her as its **first-ever Youth Advocate**, making her a voice for children's rights and disability inclusion across the country. She has since participated in several UNICEF India campaigns, speaking about sport, accessibility, and what it means to be heard when you cannot hear.

For Madhya Pradesh, this combination of athletic achievement and public voice is rare. Bhopal produced a young woman who won Deaflympics gold, trains with national champions in Gwalior, carries UNICEF's flag on disability rights, and now prepares to compete at Asia Pacific level again in October. The Penang championship — the Asia Pacific Deaf Multi Sports Championship 2026, from October 3 to 10 — brings together participants from 17 countries. Gauranshi Sharma will be among those representing India in badminton.

In a state where girls from ordinary families often have to fight simply for the right to keep playing sport, her presence in that squad is not just a sporting result. It is a demonstration of what sustained support, good coaching, and personal determination look like when they work together over years.
