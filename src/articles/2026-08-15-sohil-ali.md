On 8 August, in the final of the 16th Hockey India Junior Men's National Championship 2026, Odisha led by a goal when Sohil Ali stepped up in the **37th minute** and pulled Madhya Pradesh level. It was the kind of moment that separates players who can deliver in ordinary games from those who can deliver in finals.

The match ultimately went to Hockey Association of Odisha, 2-1, with goals from Mandeep Kerketta and Deepak Pradhan. But what Madhya Pradesh built over eleven days in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu — and what Sohil Ali contributed to it throughout — is a record worth marking.

## A Tournament Built Goal by Goal

The championship, which ran from **28 July to 8 August** with thirty teams competing across three divisions, required steady performance across multiple rounds, not a single good day. Sohil Ali proved his value in the group and knockout stages before the final.

In a **4-0 win over Hockey Unit of Tamil Nadu**, he scored in the 27th minute. In a hard-fought **2-2 draw against Hockey Haryana**, he equalised in the 48th minute — a result that kept Madhya Pradesh's progress intact. Then came the final, where he again delivered on the biggest occasion of the tournament.

Three goals across a national championship, including one in the decisive match, is a contribution that coaches, selectors, and teammates all notice.

## Hockey Madhya Pradesh's Growing Record

Sohil Ali's silver is the latest addition to a state record that has been building steadily. Over sixteen years of senior junior and sub-junior national hockey championships, **Hockey Madhya Pradesh has accumulated 29 medals**, establishing the state as one of the country's reliable producers of hockey talent. Academies across the state feed players into district, state, and national structures — and those pipelines are working.

The 2026 Junior Men's championship silver adds to that collection. Reaching the final at this level, against competition from across the country, confirms that the investment in grassroots hockey in Madhya Pradesh is translating into results at the national stage.

## Why It Matters

A junior national championship final is where careers are made visible. Selectors, coaches, and national programme administrators watch these tournaments closely, looking for players who can perform when the margin for error disappears. Sohil Ali's consistency across the Coimbatore tournament — and his ability to score in the final itself — puts him in exactly that kind of notice. For a state whose hockey programme has spent years building toward national-level competition, players like Sohil Ali are the evidence that the programme is working.
