The call-up arrived on July 28, twelve years after Saransh Jain made his first-class debut for Madhya Pradesh. He was 33. The BCCI named the Indore all-rounder in India's Test squad for the two-match series against Sri Lanka — his first senior national call-up, and one of domestic cricket's more satisfying long-game stories.

Jain was born into cricket. His father Subodh Jain played as a right-arm off-spinner for Madhya Pradesh; Saransh bowls the same way, bats left-handed — a useful lower-order variation. He made his first-class debut in December 2014. The early seasons were intermittent — eight appearances across six years — until coach Chandrakant Pandit identified him as a genuine bowling all-rounder and gave him the consistent run that shaped his career.

## Twelve Seasons, 188 Wickets, One Call-Up

In **54 first-class matches**, Jain has scored **2,223 runs** at 31.75 and taken **188 wickets** at 27.30. Those are the numbers of a cricketer who changes games; they are not the numbers that usually wait twelve years for recognition.

The 2021–22 Ranji Trophy is the pivot of his story. Madhya Pradesh had competed for 88 years without winning the title. In the final that season, Jain was decisive: **13 wickets and a half-century**, dismantling the opposition's batting plan and steadying MP's innings when it needed anchoring. They won. It was the state's first-ever Ranji Trophy title.

He then spent subsequent seasons building a case that selection could not overlook. His most recent Ranji campaign produced **518 runs at 57.55 and 30 wickets in seven matches**. His India A tour of Sri Lanka was the final confirmation: an unbeaten 70 with the bat, followed by a **six-wicket haul** in the second four-day fixture — at the same venues where the Test series will now be played.

He replaces injured Washington Sundar in the squad. Galle and Colombo, where the first and second Tests begin on August 15 and August 23 respectively, are conditions that reward right-arm off-spin and a reliable lower-order bat. Jain has spent his career preparing for exactly these surfaces.

For Madhya Pradesh, a state that won its first national domestic title in 2022 and now regularly places players in India A conversations, Jain's call-up is the clearest signal yet that provincial cricket is no longer merely a feeder for distant ambitions — it is where Test careers are built.
