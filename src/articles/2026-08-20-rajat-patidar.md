On 23 August, six zonal sides will begin the 2026-27 Duleep Trophy at the BCCI Centre of Excellence in Bengaluru. Central Zone — the defending champions, and the current dominant force in the tournament's seven-decade history — will walk out with Rajat Patidar again in the captain's armband.

The BCCI's selection committee retained the 32-year-old India batter from Indore for the role this week, alongside naming Rinku Singh as his deputy. It is a straightforward endorsement of a season in which Patidar has been the most productive Central Zone batter with the red ball in hand.

## The Season Behind the Selection

In last year's edition, Patidar was the tournament's top run-getter with **382 runs**, a total he capped with a century in the final as Central Zone beat South Zone by six wickets in Bengaluru to lift a seventh Duleep Trophy — more than any other zonal team has managed in the competition's modern format. He is being asked to do it again.

The Central Zone squad reads like a working blueprint for that ambition: Saransh Jain, the offspinner from Madhya Pradesh who has quietly built a domestic wickets column that rivals any in the country; Aryan Juyal behind the stumps; Rinku Singh and Aryan Pandey among the batters; Zeeshan Ansari and Kumar Kartikeya sharing the spin load. Fifteen players in the main squad, six on standby, and a tournament format that runs single innings across the group stage before the two league leaders play a five-day final.

Patidar's rise is by now well-established. He led Royal Challengers Bengaluru to their first Indian Premier League title in 2025, ending an eighteen-year drought for one of the league's most-supported franchises. He made his ODI debut for India against South Africa in 2023 and his Test debut against England in 2024. He was, and remains, the batter whose 2022 Ranji Trophy century in the final helped Madhya Pradesh win its first Ranji title in the state's history — a match that turned an unfancied side into champions and made the coach-captain axis of Chandrakant Pandit and Aditya Shrivastava a template that MP cricket has kept building on.

## Why It Matters

Domestic cricket does not sell tickets the way the IPL does, and Duleep Trophy squads are announced in short releases rather than trailers. But red-ball form is where India assembles its Test line-up, and for Madhya Pradesh's cricketing ecosystem — the academies in Indore and Bhopal, the state association, the players who watch someone from Indore captain a zone and then a country — Patidar's captaincy is a signal.

The tournament begins in three days. The final is scheduled for 10 September. If Central Zone lift the trophy again, it will be the second time in Patidar's captaincy — and the second time in a row that a batter from Indore has been at the centre of the celebration.
