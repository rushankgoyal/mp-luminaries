On August 22, 2026, the Ministry of Education published the list of recipients for the National Teachers' Awards 2026. Among the **48 teachers** selected from across India was Shailendra Pratap Singh — a secondary teacher at Government Middle School Kanjwar in Sidhi district, Madhya Pradesh. He is one of only two teachers from the state to make the national list this year.

President Droupadi Murmu will confer the awards at Vigyan Bhawan in New Delhi on **September 5** — Teachers' Day.

Shailendra Pratap Singh was born in Kanjwar village itself, in 1978. He has spent his career where he grew up, teaching at the government middle school in the same village. He holds postgraduate qualifications in English, Geography, and History, and has built a reputation in the district for approaching teaching as something more than syllabus delivery — investing in environmental awareness among students and developing discipline practices centred on responsibility rather than punishment.

## What the Selection Means

The National Teacher Award is not given for seniority. It is given for teaching. The selection process runs through state-level screening committees that evaluate teachers on the quality of their classroom work, their contribution to the wider school and community, and the innovations they have brought to learning in their specific setting. A rural schoolteacher in a district like Sidhi competes on the same basis as teachers in state capitals and well-resourced cities — without the advantage of better infrastructure, larger peer networks, or easier access to professional development.

That the selection reached Kanjwar village is, in itself, a statement. The 48 names on the 2026 list were drawn from **27 states, seven Union Territories, and six central government organisations**. Madhya Pradesh's two selections — Shailendra Pratap Singh from Sidhi and Dr. Archana Shukla from Bhopal — represent a broad range of the state's educational geography: the capital city and the eastern edge of the Vindhya plateau, both present on the republic's highest teaching honour in the same year.

## The Wider Picture

Sidhi district is far from a prominent address in Indian public discourse. Located in the north-east of Madhya Pradesh, near the border with Uttar Pradesh, it does not often appear in conversations about educational innovation. The district has worked against significant infrastructure challenges in its government schools, and the teachers who remain in village postings year after year — choosing continuity over transfers to bigger towns — are often the quiet load-bearers of its children's outcomes.

For teachers in such postings, national recognition of this kind carries weight well beyond the individual. It signals to students in similar villages that their school, and the person standing at its blackboard, belongs to the same country that produces award-winning educators. That signal is harder to quantify than a test score, but those who have worked in rural schooling know what it does.

Shailendra Pratap Singh will travel to New Delhi in two weeks to receive the award from the President of India. The classroom in Kanjwar will be waiting when he returns.
