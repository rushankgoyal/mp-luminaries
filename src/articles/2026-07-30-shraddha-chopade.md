At the 2024 Paris Grand Slam, Shraddha Kadubal Chopade stepped onto the mat and beat Fabienne Kocher of Switzerland. That result, in full, is this: an Indian judoka defeated a Swiss competitor who had placed fifth at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games. It was the kind of win that shocks the draw sheet and prompts coaches from other countries to look up a name they had not paid attention to before. Chopade was a teenager. She won cleanly.

On 31 July 2026, she steps onto the Commonwealth Games mat in Glasgow for the first time, representing India in the women's -52 kg category. Her selection is the result of three years of competitive judo that has taken her from national-level promise to a judoka who competes and wins at Grand Slams and continental championships.

Chopade trains at the Sports Authority of India National Centre of Excellence in Bhopal — a high-performance training hub that has sent four of its athletes to the Glasgow Games. Alongside her, Asmita Dey competes in the -48 kg category, Avtar Singh in the men's -100 kg, and Yash Ghangas in the men's +100 kg. The Bhopal centre's presence across four weight categories is a measure of how seriously India's national judo programme has invested in the city. For Chopade, the centre has provided the coaching, sports science, nutrition and recovery infrastructure that separates athletes who plateau early from those who keep improving.

## A Year of Opens and Grand Slams

The 2025 competition season produced results that gave her selection a clear basis. At the Oceania Open in Gold Coast in November 2025, Chopade won the **gold medal** in the -52 kg category. Later that year she took **bronze at the African Open in Casablanca** — two open-circuit medals on two different continents in the same year. Earlier, at the 2024 Asian Junior Championships, she had finished in the top five; at the 2025 Senior Asian Championships, she reached the same level against older and more experienced competition.

In 2026, ahead of Glasgow, she competed at the Grand Slam in Tashkent, the Barysy Grand Slam in Almaty, and the Asian Senior Championships — the full circuit of major competitions that all serious medal contenders must navigate to stay in the world rankings and in the selectors' view. The work was done in public, on the mat, in a weight category where India does not have a tradition of medal-winning.

Her Paris Grand Slam upset in 2024 is still the most striking data point in her career. Fabienne Kocher had Olympic experience; Chopade had timing, technique and the willingness to attack. That combination produced the most high-profile win of her career so far. Whether it produces a podium finish in Glasgow depends on the draws she faces and how her conditioning holds across a multi-bout tournament.

## India's Judo Pipeline

India has never won a gold medal in judo at the Commonwealth Games. The country's best result was a silver medal at Birmingham 2022. The presence of four SAI Bhopal-trained athletes in Glasgow's judo draw does not guarantee that record changes in 2026, but it reflects how steadily the pipeline from training centres to international competition has deepened over the past five years.

For Chopade, the Commonwealth Games debut is a beginning, not a career ceiling. The judoka who beat a Tokyo Olympic finalist at nineteen, won Opens in two countries in a single season, and kept competing through the Grand Slam circuit into the Olympic year is not at the limit of what she can do. Glasgow will be the first time a global audience sees her in this event. It is unlikely to be the last.
