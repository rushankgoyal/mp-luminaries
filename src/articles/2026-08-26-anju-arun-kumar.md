When the first batch of electric buses pulled out of Bhopal's depot on July 25, 2026, it was the first time Madhya Pradesh's capital had an electric city bus at all. Five weeks later, 35 of those buses are running daily across the city, carrying more passengers each week than the week before. The IAS officer who made that happen is Anju Arun Kumar — Additional Commissioner at the Bhopal Municipal Corporation and the CEO of Bhopal City Link Limited (BCLL), the city's public transport company.

The launch was part of the central government's PM e-Bus Sewa Scheme, which provides subsidised electric buses to cities on the condition that local authorities build the operating infrastructure. The condition matters: many cities have received buses and parked them. Bhopal moved faster. Under Anju Arun Kumar, BCLL ran five days of free passenger trials from July 26, opened commercial service at ₹1.50 per kilometre on July 30, and kept expanding the fleet in batches through August.

## From Zero to 35 in a Month

The first operating batch was 20 buses on two core routes: Kolar Road to the Inter-State Bus Terminal (ISBT), and the Sukhi Sevania–Bhojpur corridor. Both are commuter-heavy stretches where reliable public transport makes a direct difference to working families who cannot afford daily auto fares.

On August 24, ten more buses were added — bringing the fleet to 30, then a further five shortly after to reach 35. Waiting times on existing routes dropped to 10–15 minutes. Three new routes were introduced, extending service to Bairagarh, Bawadiya Kalan, MP Nagar, and Obaidullaganj, connecting the capital's suburban ring with its commercial centre.

The ridership response has been emphatic. Each expansion has triggered a jump of approximately 40 percent in daily boardings within the first three days of the new buses hitting the road. That figure reflects genuine demand, not novelty: commuters using the electric buses are returning to them, not just trying them once.

> "The current fleet is part of a larger plan under which 100 e-buses are expected to operate across Bhopal, further expanding connectivity and reducing waiting time for passengers."  
> — Anju Arun Kumar, BCLL CEO

## What Comes Next

Anju Arun Kumar has secured central approval for five additional depots across the city to house the expanded fleet. Two charging depots are already operational at Sant Hirdaram Nagar (Bairagarh) and Kasturba Nagar. The remaining three depots will be needed as the fleet grows toward the 100-bus target.

The Bhopal Smart City Limited — where Anju Arun Kumar also served as CEO before her current posting — has co-ordinated some of the route planning and demand analysis that informed the e-bus corridors. That institutional overlap means the data behind these routes is not guesswork: it draws on smart city mobility studies already completed for Bhopal.

For a city of nearly 24 lakh people that has long struggled with fragmented public transport, 35 clean electric buses running on schedule represent a meaningful change in daily life. If the 100-bus target holds, Bhopal will have a city-wide network that competes with the auto-rickshaw and private vehicle on both convenience and cost — and does it without a gram of tailpipe emission.
