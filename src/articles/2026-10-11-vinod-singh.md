On the first day of the 65th National Open Athletics Championship at Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in New Delhi, Vinod Singh ran **30:37.13** to finish third in the men's 10,000 metres and take bronze. Shivaji Madappa won in **30:30.54**; Gaurav Mathur was second in **30:36.33**. The gap between first and third was just six seconds over twenty-five laps.

For Vinod Singh — 21 years old, from Madhya Pradesh — this was his first medal at a senior national open athletics championship. The open meet is the most competitive format in Indian athletics: it places junior-age athletes against services team runners and senior state competitors who have spent years at this level. A top-three finish at 21 is not common.

His junior record made the result less surprising in hindsight. At the 2024 National Junior Athletics Championships, Vinod won the 10,000 metres for Madhya Pradesh, clocking the fastest Indian U20 time of the year in the event. At the same meet he also won the 5,000 metres and the 3,000 metres — setting a championship meet record of **8:14.22** in the 3,000 metres. Three national junior titles in a single championship across three distance disciplines. The range pointed to fitness and tactical awareness well beyond the junior level.

## From continent to national open

The next marker came abroad. At the Asian Athletics Under-20 Championships, Vinod Singh won a silver medal — a result that placed him among the best junior distance runners on the continent, competing against athletes from programmes in Japan, China, Kenya, and the Gulf states that invest heavily in distance running.

Back in India, he crossed the senior threshold in March 2026, winning the men's 1500 metres at the National Indoor Athletics Championships in Bhubaneswar in **3:46.13**. The indoor title showed he could perform in the shorter-distance events as well, a sign of a runner with pace and not just endurance. Distance runners who hold speed over 1500 metres tend to have more tactics available to them in 10,000-metre races.

His personal best in the 10,000 metres is **29:42.61**, set in Sangrur in April 2025 — a time that puts him solidly in contention at national level. The 30:37.13 in New Delhi was below that mark, which is typical in early-autumn championships where the season's sharpest racing has already passed, but the placement — bronze in the senior national open — is the metric that matters here.

Vinod Singh is one of four athletes whose results contributed to Madhya Pradesh's tally of four medals at this year's championship. The state's athletics programme has been better known for its field events — the MP Academy's pole vault unit in particular — but a runner of Vinod's calibre entering the senior circuit points to a distance pipeline developing alongside it.

At 21, with two more years of junior-category eligibility available internationally, he is building a career that still has most of its competition ahead. The bronze in New Delhi is the first piece of evidence that he can compete in the senior field — not just the junior circuit — and hold his ground.
