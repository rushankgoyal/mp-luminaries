The scoreboard in the men's 79 kg weightlifting hall at Glasgow 2026 told a very close story. Valluri Ajaya Babu's **149 kg** snatch on 27 July was not just a personal best — it was a Commonwealth Games record, the heaviest snatch anyone has lifted in this weight category in the history of the Games. He walked into the clean and jerk phase leading the competition.

The gold went elsewhere by one kilogram. Malaysia's Muhammad Erry Hidayat responded with a **184 kg** clean and jerk, also a Games record, to finish on a 331 kg total against Ajaya Babu's 330 kg. One kilogram in a sport where competitors load a bar as heavy as they can risk at the highest level of the discipline.

Ajaya Babu received his silver and said what any athlete who has trained for years to be this close would say: "It is a mix of happiness and sadness, as I missed out on the gold. I feel that a mistake cost me the gold today. I will ensure I don't repeat this error in future competitions and will work even harder for upcoming games." He dedicated the medal to his coaches, his father, and India.

The result places him among a small group of Indian weightlifters who have won at both the Commonwealth Weightlifting Championships and the Commonwealth Games in the same twelve-month stretch.

## A Family Medal and a Madhya Pradesh Connection

Ajaya Babu began lifting at age ten in Kondavelagada village, Vizianagaram district, under the guidance of his father, **Valluri Srinivasa Rao** — who won bronze in the men's 69 kg category at the **2010 Commonwealth Games** in New Delhi. A son bettering a father's Commonwealth medal by one position is an unusual thing, and the Valluri family now has two generations of Commonwealth Games podium finishers.

He joined the Indian Army's **Mission Olympics Wing**, the programme that identifies and develops athletes with international potential. At the **2025 National Games**, competing for **Madhya Pradesh** in the men's 81 kg category, Ajaya Babu won gold with a total of 322 kg — a dominant performance that drew attention from the national coaching programme.

In August 2025, at the **Commonwealth Weightlifting Championships** in Ahmedabad, he entered the 79 kg category and won gold with a personal best of **335 kg** (152 kg snatch + 183 kg clean and jerk). Muhammad Erry Hidayat finished second at 333 kg. The rematch in Glasgow on 27 July reversed those positions by a single kilogram.

The margin between first and second in weightlifting can come down to a single failed attempt, a bar loaded one kilogram higher than the competition expects, or a clean and jerk that catches a leader who has already shown their hand in the snatch. Ajaya Babu set a Games record in the snatch and led through the first phase of the final. The arithmetic of what happened next — Hidayat's 184 kg lift against Ajaya Babu's 181 kg — is not an ending. It is a number both athletes will train against for the next four years.

For Indian weightlifting, the Glasgow silver is a sign of a competitive field deepening: a 21-year-old from the Army's Mission Olympics Wing, representing Madhya Pradesh on the national games circuit, is now within one kilogram of a Commonwealth Games gold medal.
