At the sixth Indian Open Under-23 Athletics Competition in Jaipur on Saturday, Nitika Anup Akare took her run-up, planted her pole, and cleared **4.15 metres** — the highest any woman has jumped at this competition in its history. The bar had stood at 4.00 metres, set by Tamil Nadu's Pavithra back in 2022. Nitika broke it by 15 centimetres, won the gold medal for Madhya Pradesh, and ended the day with a new personal best that places her firmly in India's upper tier of women's pole vaulters.

Nitika was born in December 2005 and has been on the national circuit long enough to show a clear upward line. In March 2026 she won bronze at an international meet in Taiwan with a clearance of 3.95 metres. In June, at the National Inter-State Senior Championships, she cleared **4.10 metres** — the Asian Games qualification standard — to become only the third Indian woman in the season to reach that mark. Each competition has added to the record. Saturday's 4.15-metre jump in Jaipur is the sharpest single step in that progression.

## From Qualification Standard to Meet Record

The gap between Nitika and the rest of the women's pole vault field in Jaipur was striking. Her Madhya Pradesh teammate Vanshika Ghanghas cleared **3.80 metres** to win silver, finishing 35 centimetres behind. That gap speaks both to Nitika's form and to the depth in Madhya Pradesh's women's programme — both gold and silver went to the same state, in a discipline where the state had little prior representation at the national level.

To put the numbers in context: the national senior women's pole vault record, held by Tamil Nadu's Baranica Elangovan, is above 4.50 metres. The Asian Games qualification mark is 4.10 metres. Nitika, at 20, has already cleared the Games standard and set a U23 competition record on the same morning. The trajectory over the next Olympic and Commonwealth Games cycles is straightforward to read.

The Madhya Pradesh Athletics Academy in Bhopal has for years been the pipeline for India's leading men's pole vaulters — Dev Kumar Meena and Kuldeep Kumar have both broken national records while training there. Nitika's gold is evidence that the women's side of the programme has reached a comparable level. In a sport that rewards consistent technical improvement over time, she is twenty years old, already at 4.15 metres, and still rising.
