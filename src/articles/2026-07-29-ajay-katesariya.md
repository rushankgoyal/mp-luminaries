Ajay Katesariya spent his years in Bhopal's General Administration Department doing the least glamorous work in government: drafting rules. Not speeches, not inaugurations — rules. The procedural frameworks that determine who gets promoted, when, and on what basis. But on July 25, Madhya Pradesh's government sent a clear signal that this kind of work matters: it appointed Katesariya Collector of Ratlam, his first posting as a district head, and specifically noted the appointment as a recognition of his contributions.

The central achievement that earned this recognition was the Promotion Rules-2025. Madhya Pradesh government employees across dozens of departments had been waiting for career advancement for nearly a decade. The old promotion framework had become stuck — a tangle of pending court orders, legal complications, and outdated seniority structures that successive administrations had struggled to unpick. Year after year, competent officers and government staff watched their career timelines freeze while the bureaucracy around them moved forward.

Katesariya, working as Additional Secretary in the General Administration Department, spent months on the problem. He worked through the legal and procedural constraints, addressed competing claims in the seniority system, and produced the Promotion Rules-2025 — a framework that cleared the deadlock and made promotions possible again across state government. The rules came into force in early 2026 and quickly began to move people forward: in revenue, in health, in forest services, across departments that had been stuck for years.

## The Quiet Work That Moved a Bureaucracy

What made the achievement notable was its specificity. An outsider can see when a scheme is launched or a bridge is built. The internal machinery that keeps an administration functioning — the promotion rules, the service regulations, the cadre frameworks — is invisible to most citizens but shapes the lives of lakhs of government employees, and through them, the quality of services delivered across the state. Getting that machinery working again, after nearly a decade of gridlock, was a meaningful act of governance.

Katesariya also worked on drafting the preliminary framework for Madhya Pradesh's proposed Uniform Civil Code — another legally dense task requiring the same careful, methodical handling that defined his secretariat years.

The Ratlam posting that followed is a significant next step. Ratlam is a key district in western Madhya Pradesh's Malwa region, with a strong agricultural base, growing industrial activity, and a large rural population that depends on district administration for land records, welfare delivery, and local infrastructure. The skills Katesariya developed in the secretariat — precision, patience with complex problems, the ability to find workable solutions inside tangled regulations — are exactly what the role demands.

For Madhya Pradesh's administrative cadre, the message behind the appointment is straightforward: the work of getting systems right is noticed, and it counts.
