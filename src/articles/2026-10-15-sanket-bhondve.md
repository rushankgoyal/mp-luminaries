On 5 October 2026, the Union Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs held its World Habitat Day ceremony in New Delhi under the theme "Adequate Housing for All." Among the honours announced, Madhya Pradesh was recognised as one of three best-performing states in India for completing houses under the Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban Beneficiary-Led Construction scheme. Sanket Bhondve, the state's Urban Administration & Development Commissioner, accepted the national award at the event.

The Beneficiary-Led Construction scheme is the most direct strand of the urban housing mission: the central government gives families living in urban areas a subsidy to construct or upgrade their own homes on land they already own. Completing houses under this vertical is an administrative task as much as a construction one — the state must identify eligible families, process applications, disburse grants in stages tied to construction milestones, and verify progress through geo-tagged photographs and site inspections. Doing this well, at scale, requires sustained coordination across urban local bodies, district collectors' offices, and the state mission directorate.

## The Numbers Behind the Award

Madhya Pradesh's recognition is grounded in its long record on PMAY-U. Under the original programme and its successor PMAY-U 2.0 combined, the state has sanctioned more than **10.39 lakh** houses and completed **9.08 lakh** of them — placing it consistently among the top performers nationally. In June 2026, the Union Housing Minister sanctioned an additional **42,000 houses** under PMAY-U 2.0 in the state, with a subsidy outlay of **₹1,050 crore**. At the World Habitat Day ceremony, Madhya Pradesh was placed third among all states for BLC completion, alongside Uttar Pradesh and Gujarat — both larger states by population.

Minister of State for Housing and Urban Affairs Tokhan Sahu presided over the event, at which the Ministry also launched a new PMAY-U 2.0 mobile application and web portal to make the scheme easier to access for beneficiaries.

The scale of what MP has built is easy to understate. Nine lakh completed homes means nine lakh urban families — mostly first-time homeowners — now have a permanent address they own. That asset changes what a family can do: it provides collateral, stability for children to stay in school, and a place from which to build further. Commissioner Bhondve's team oversaw the administrative scaffolding that turned a central-government scheme into walls and a roof for nearly a million households. The national award, won in competition with every state and union territory in India, confirms that the work was done well.
