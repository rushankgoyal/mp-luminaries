Ghuwara is a small town in Madhya Pradesh's Chhatarpur district. It is not a place that has produced many cricketers at the national level. On September 13, 2026, Kranti Gaud — who grew up playing tennis-ball cricket in those lanes — walked off a ground in Bangladesh having won the Women's T20 Asia Cup as part of the Indian squad that defeated Sri Lanka by 73 runs in the final. She was 23.

The match was India's eighth Asia Cup title, extending a record no other nation in women's cricket holds. The victory was comprehensive. India posted **183 for 5**, built on a 129-run opening stand between Shafali Verma (68 off 39) and Smriti Mandhana (59 off 39). Sri Lanka were bowled out for 111 — Deepti Sharma took 3 for 19 and Shree Charani 4 for 20. Gaud contributed at the top of the innings, removing opener Dulani to take the first wicket and conceding 8 runs in her spell.

## From Chhatarpur to the India Squad

Cricket found Gaud through proximity and then became a serious pursuit through structure. Rajiv Bilthre, secretary of the Chhatarpur District Cricket Association, identified her pace in local matches and began working with her on the technical foundation she needed to move up through the ranks. From district cricket she progressed to the Madhya Pradesh women's senior team, making her first-class debut in the 2023-24 season.

The India call-up came in May 2025. Her ODI debut on the England tour that followed was exceptional by any standard: she took **6 for 52**, becoming the youngest Indian woman — at 21 — to claim a five-wicket haul in ODIs. That performance made her name recognisable to Indian cricket supporters who had not yet followed the emergence of players from smaller state units.

By September 2026, she is a Women's Premier League player with UP Warriorz, a 2025 Women's Cricket World Cup winner — India's first World Cup win in cricket — and now an Asia Cup champion. The arc from tennis-ball cricket in a Chhatarpur neighbourhood to that set of titles has taken less than a decade of formal coaching.

What Gaud's trajectory shows, more specifically, is that Madhya Pradesh's women's cricket pipeline now reaches the national team on merit. The state's DCA infrastructure, coaches like Bilthre working at district level, and the WPL's ability to expose young players to high-intensity franchise cricket have all contributed. Gaud is the person in whom those pieces are most clearly visible at the moment — but she is not the last one they will produce.
