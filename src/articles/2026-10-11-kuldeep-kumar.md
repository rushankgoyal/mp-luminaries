On the opening day of the 65th National Open Athletics Championship at Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in New Delhi, Kuldeep Kumar cleared 5.30 metres to win the men's pole vault gold and break the championship's own meet record. The previous mark was 5.15 metres. Reegan G finished second at 5.25 metres, also above the old record. Between them, the two vaulters reset the ceiling for what this competition has seen.

For Kuldeep, the gold adds a senior national open title to a season already crowded with milestones. He trains at the Madhya Pradesh Sports Academy in Bhopal — a programme that has invested steadily in pole vault since the mid-2010s, importing coaching methods and equipment that the event demands: specific runway surfaces, vaulting poles matched to each athlete's speed, and coaches who have trained athletes abroad. The investment has been visible in results.

In May, at the inaugural National Indoor Open Pole Vault Competition in Bhubaneswar, Kuldeep cleared **5.41 metres** on his first attempt to set a new national indoor record. The jump announced a new level: at 5.41 metres, he became one of a very small group of Indian vaulters to have cleared above 5.40 metres indoors. Within weeks, at the National Federation Cup in Ranchi, he went further. He and training partner Dev Kumar Meena — also from the MP Academy — pushed each other through a series of heights and both cleared **5.45 metres**, jointly setting a new national outdoor record. The back-to-back records in a single season from the same academy said something about the programme's depth.

## Madhya Pradesh's pole vault corridor

Earlier in the year, at the Inter-State Championships in Bhubaneswar, Kuldeep and Meena both cleared **5.25 metres**, qualifying both for the Commonwealth Games — the first time Madhya Pradesh athletes had done so in this discipline. The milestone mattered for the state's athletics programme, which has found greater success in technical field events than in the sprint categories that attract the most visible coverage.

At the National Open, the four medals that Madhya Pradesh collected — two gold and two bronze — made the state's academy one of the more consistent individual-performance stories at the meet. Kuldeep's pole vault gold was the headline result. The championship draws athletes from across the country's services teams and state programmes, and a win here carries the weight of an open-age national title: there are no age restrictions, no categories.

The 65th edition ran from October 8 to 11 in New Delhi, bringing together over a thousand athletes. Kuldeep competed on the first day, the field events slot where results often set the tone for the week. By the time the bar reached 5.30 metres, only Kuldeep had cleared it. The 5.30 metres was also above both finalists' own earlier marks in the event, which points to a competitive meet rather than a field that backed off the higher heights.

For Madhya Pradesh athletics, the past year has confirmed what the state's Sports Academy began to suggest five years ago: that sustained, specific investment in technical disciplines can produce athletes who compete at the top of national competition. Kuldeep Kumar is the clearest example of that curve so far.
