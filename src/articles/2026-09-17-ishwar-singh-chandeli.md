When Chief Minister Mohan Yadav took to the stage at Kushabhau Thakre Auditorium in Bhopal on September 15, 2026, he had a list of names. Among those he called forward was Ishwar Singh Chandeli, Divisional Manager of the Madhya Pradesh Road Development Corporation — one of the engineers to receive the Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya Award at the state's Engineers' Day programme.

The MPRDC is the agency at the heart of Madhya Pradesh's road-building programme. It designs, funds, constructs, and maintains state highways, national highway work packages devolved to the state, and major district-level connectors. In a landlocked state where farm produce reaches urban mandis by truck and daily-wage workers commute to construction sites, the quality and reach of roads determines economic possibility as much as any policy does.

## Holding the Middle of the Road-Building Chain

As Divisional Manager, Chandeli's role sits between project design and physical execution — overseeing road works in his division, managing contractors, checking progress benchmarks, and ensuring that what gets built matches the engineering specifications. It is supervisory work that is largely invisible when done well. Its absence surfaces as potholes, delays, or cost overruns; its presence holds the whole chain together.

The PWD organised the state-level Engineers' Day ceremony to honour not just senior officials but the engineers who carry accountability on the ground. Engineers, joint teams, and construction agencies were all recognised at the Bhopal event.

Chief Minister Yadav, presenting the awards, said engineers are "key to MP's economic and social development" and that their "talent transforms development ideas into reality." For a Divisional Manager like Chandeli, who manages the working face of road construction — the kilometres laid, the bridges poured, the traffic diversions managed — that line translates directly to daily decisions made in the field.

The same ceremony also saw the PWD sign a memorandum of understanding with CSIR-AMPRI, Bhopal. The collaboration with the Advanced Materials and Processes Research Institute is aimed at bringing better materials science into the state's construction pipeline. For road engineers in particular, the prospect of improved materials — surfaces that better handle India's thermal cycle and monsoon load — means roads that hold for longer before requiring expensive rehabilitation.

The Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya Award joins the Rani Durgavati, Vikramaditya, and Raja Bhoj awards introduced by PWD to make engineering excellence a recognised milestone in a state career. Engineers who might otherwise be anonymous in a large bureaucracy are named and honoured publicly. At a time when MPRDC is running construction projects across many of MP's 55 districts simultaneously, recognising division-level managers by name signals that the state is watching how its roads are built, not just whether they are.

Ishwar Singh Chandeli built roads. Madhya Pradesh noticed.
