On India's 80th Independence Day, the Gwalior police headquarters received news that its district chief had joined a short list. Among **1,057 police, fire, and civil defence personnel** across the country recognised for gallantry and service in 2026, Dharmvir Singh — Superintendent of Police, Gwalior — received the **President's Medal for Distinguished Service** (PSM). One of only four officers from Madhya Pradesh Police to earn the PSM this year, Singh's recognition marks the apex of a career that has been formally acknowledged four times over thirteen years.

Singh is a **2012-batch IPS officer** from the Madhya Pradesh cadre, and he reached that rank through promotion from the State Police Service — a path that asks officers to demonstrate exceptional performance within the state's own hierarchy before the Centre opens the door to the IPS equivalent. The route demands patience and sustained excellence, not just a single standout moment.

## Four Recognitions, One Consistent Standard

The awards on Singh's service record form a deliberate sequence. He received a **Police Medal for Gallantry** in 2013, earned relatively early in his uniformed career, indicating recognition for conduct requiring courage in the field. Six years later, in 2019, came the **President's Police Medal for Meritorious Service** — a mark of sustained contribution that the force wishes to put formally on record. Then, in 2021, a second **Police Medal for Gallantry**, confirming that the standards of 2013 had not slipped over the intervening decade.

The **President's Medal for Distinguished Service** in 2026 sits at the top of the Indian police service award hierarchy. Where gallantry medals recognise defined moments and the meritorious service medal marks a sustained contribution of above-average quality, the PSM is reserved for officers whose entire body of work, assessed over a career, has measurably elevated the institution. Singh's receipt of it while posted as SP Gwalior — a posting that covers one of Madhya Pradesh's largest and most complex urban centres — makes the recognition concrete rather than honorary.

Madhya Pradesh Police as a whole earned **four PSMs and sixteen Meritorious Service Medals** in the 2026 Independence Day list. The state also had two civilian recipients honoured with the Sarvottam Jeevan Raksha Padak for saving lives in dangerous circumstances. Chief Minister Dr Mohan Yadav presided over the investiture ceremony at Lal Parade Ground, Bhopal, on 15 August, where the medals were formally presented.

Singh's career arc matters beyond the award itself. Officers promoted from the State Police Service into the IPS bring a quality of ground-level institutional knowledge that is different from what direct-recruit IPS officers develop in their first postings. When one of these promoted officers then goes on to receive the highest service distinction in Indian policing, it sends a clear message about what MP's own state cadre is capable of producing.

For Gwalior — a city that hosts significant military and civil establishments, carries a complex urban crime profile, and sits close to the MP-UP-Rajasthan tri-junction — steady professional leadership at the SP level is not merely desirable. The PSM on Dharmvir Singh's record suggests it has been exactly that.
