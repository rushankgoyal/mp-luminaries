Three penalty corners. Three goals. A scoreline of **5-1** and a place in the final.

On Saturday afternoon in Moqi, China, Supriya stepped up to each penalty corner and dragged the ball low and hard into South Korea's goal. Her first flick came in the 17th minute — a precise shot that gave the Korean goalkeeper no angle. She added a second to extend India's lead, and completed her hat-trick in the 56th minute as the match closed out. India's title defence in the AHF Junior Women's Asia Cup 2026 is very much alive.

The AHF Junior Women's Asia Cup is the continent's premier age-group women's hockey competition, and India arrived at it as defending champions. A third consecutive title is now within reach: the final pits India against the winner of the second semifinal between hosts China and Japan.

## What a Drag-Flicker Has to Get Right

Supriya's specialisation — converting penalty corners through drag-flicks — is among the most technically demanding skills in field hockey. The process looks simple from the stands: a team-mate pushes the ball from the post, and the flicker receives it, drops to a semi-crouch and releases a shot. What the stands cannot see is the milligram-level precision required. The flicker controls a moving ball, generates the power through a full-body rotation, and directs the shot in under a second. At the highest levels, the ball can travel at more than 130 kilometres per hour.

Supriya executed that sequence three times on Saturday, each time finding the target. Her conversion rate across the evening — three shots, three goals — is the kind of number a coach would show in a training debrief and a goalkeeper would study on video looking for a weakness she did not expose. Against South Korea, there was nothing to find.

Penalty-corner drills are repetitive, unglamorous work done in empty training sessions. What happens in a semifinal is the visible fraction of a much larger practice ledger.

India has steadily developed its women's hockey pipeline at junior level, and performances like Saturday's are evidence that the next generation is arriving on schedule. A final berth secured, a defending title to protect, and a drag-flicker who converted every chance she was given: India goes into the AHF Junior Women's Asia Cup 2026 final with reasons for confidence.
