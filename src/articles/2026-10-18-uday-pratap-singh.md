For more than two decades, a person in Bhopal, Jabalpur or Rewa who needed to travel between cities on a government-run bus had nowhere to turn. The Madhya Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation, burdened by **₹756 crore** in accumulated debt and losing nearly ₹5 crore every month, was shut down — and for **21 years** no replacement arrived.

On 25 September 2026, that changed. At BHEL Dussehra Maidan in Bhopal, **351 buses** were flagged off for seven cities under the newly launched Mukhyamantri Sugam Parivahan Seva, reviving government-backed public bus transport in Madhya Pradesh for the first time in a generation. The man who designed and delivered the scheme is Uday Pratap Singh, the state's Minister for Transport and School Education.

The scheme operates on a public-private partnership model, deliberately avoiding the state ownership structure that brought MPSRTC down. Private bus operators run the routes; the state provides the policy framework, permits and quality oversight. "The interests of private bus operators will not be affected," Singh said at the launch, signalling that the new service was designed to work alongside — not against — the existing private transport industry.

## From Closure to Coverage

The first phase connects **Bhopal, Indore, Jabalpur, Rewa, Dewas, Katni and Bhind** through a mix of intercity and intracity services — **120 intercity buses** and **231 intracity buses** in the initial rollout. The coverage area reaches the state's largest cities and several second-tier towns that had limited public transport options.

The targets Singh has set are substantially larger. The department plans to expand the fleet to **1,500 buses within the first year**, building toward a network of routes that would eventually connect **75 per cent of gram panchayats** across Madhya Pradesh. A planning document identified more than **1,164 routes** across all seven administrative regions as part of the long-term vision.

Union Minister Nitin Gadkari, who was present at the launch alongside Chief Minister Mohan Yadav, pressed the bus makers and operators on build quality and passenger safety standards, underscoring that the success of the model depends on reliable service rather than numbers alone.

The revival of public bus transport closes a gap that shaped daily life for millions of Madhya Pradesh residents — workers, students and families in cities without the means or inclination to rely solely on private vehicles. For Uday Pratap Singh, who spent more than a year working through the PPP structure, the funding model and the inter-departmental approvals, it is the signature reform of his tenure.
