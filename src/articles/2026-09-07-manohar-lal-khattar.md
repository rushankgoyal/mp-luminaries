On 6 September 2026, Union Minister for Housing and Urban Affairs Manohar Lal Khattar stepped onto the platform at Super Corridor-3 in Indore and sent a new segment of India's metro map into operation. The Indore Metro Priority Corridor-2 — Phase-I (Reach-II) — opened that morning, adding **11 kilometres** and **11 new stations** to a network that had run only a short initial stretch since 2023.

Madhya Pradesh Chief Minister Mohan Yadav and Minister of State for Housing and Urban Affairs Tokhan Sahu were present at the ceremony. For a city of more than three million people that has topped India's cleanliness rankings seven years in a row, it was the moment its metro finally began to feel like a city-scale system.

## Sixteen stations, ₹4,370 crore, one growing city

The new corridor runs from Super Corridor-3 to Malviya Nagar Chauraha, threading through Indore's fast-expanding technology zones, commercial belts and residential neighbourhoods on the city's western and southern edges. This phase was built at a cost of **₹2,850 crore**. Together with the original corridor, Indore's operational metro now covers roughly **17 km** across **16 stations**, built at a cumulative investment of approximately **₹4,370 crore**.

Before this inauguration, the metro served just five stations — enough to prove the concept, but nowhere near enough to shift how a city of Indore's scale actually moves. The 11 new stations bridge gaps that had kept large parts of the city's workforce dependent on congested roads and private transport.

> "Metro Rail is not merely a transportation project but an important instrument for economic growth, urban transformation and social inclusion."

Khattar made that case directly at the inauguration, also calling Madhya Pradesh "the heart of the country" — a formulation that carries both affection and the weight of expectation.

## What the expansion means beyond the station count

Indore is MP's largest city and its commercial engine, driving a disproportionate share of the state's industrial output and tax revenues. Its chronic congestion — a persistent contradiction for a city celebrated for cleanliness — had long increased the cost of doing business in peripheral zones.

With 16 stations now operational, workers in Malviya Nagar can reach the Super Corridor's technology campuses in minutes rather than an hour of stop-and-go traffic. Investors evaluating new facilities along the corridor see infrastructure that matches Tier-1 cities while Indore holds its advantage in land costs and quality of life. And for the city's lower-income residents, an affordable, scheduled metro changes the radius of jobs they can realistically reach.

For Madhya Pradesh, the expansion sends a signal the state has worked hard to establish: projects announced here get built, and built at scale. The ₹4,370-crore metro network in Indore is the clearest physical proof of that yet.
