On the morning of 26 July 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi addressed the 136th episode of Mann Ki Baat. Among the places he chose to name was Sidhi, a district in Madhya Pradesh's Vindhya plateau. Nearly **1,500 ponds** have been built across Sidhi under the national "Catch the Rain" campaign, Modi noted — one of the most active water-harvesting drives in central India this monsoon season.

Behind that number is Vikas Mishra, a 2013-batch IAS officer who took charge as Collector and District Magistrate of Sidhi in March 2026. Mishra has described his approach to governance in terms that are direct: the collector's role, he says, is that of a loksevak — a public servant — not an authority figure. That philosophy shaped how the pond-construction campaign was organised. Rather than directing the work from the collector's office, his administration channelled it through gram panchayats and community volunteers, making each water body a locally owned project from the start.

## 1,500 Ponds Built Across the Vindhya Plateau

Sidhi is a tribal-majority district in the Vindhya hills and receives uneven monsoon rainfall. Heavy downpours in July typically drain away before groundwater can be recharged; drier spells in late August and September leave crops at risk. The Catch the Rain campaign, running nationally since 2021 and given a fresh national push from 4 July 2026, asked each district to capture rainwater rather than watch it run downstream.

Sidhi's response under Mishra was to desilted and deepen old community ponds that had been neglected for years, and to dig new water bodies at the edges of agricultural land. Spread across the district's administrative blocks, the ponds mean that most clusters of villages now have a local water source within reach. Farmers who once sank borewells every few seasons to follow a falling water table are instead watching the soil moisture recover from above.

Madhya Pradesh featured twice in the Mann Ki Baat episode: Sidhi for pond-building, and Narsinghpur for the restoration of Amrit Sarovar water bodies. PM Modi specifically praised both districts as models for the campaign's national special push running through the current monsoon month.

The Mann Ki Baat mention drew national attention to work that had been accumulating quietly since spring. Mishra's administration is now looking at linking the new pond network to the Amrit Sarovar programme to ensure structures receive regular maintenance beyond the construction phase.

For Sidhi's farming communities — where agriculture is the main livelihood — a reliable water source in the dry months between harvests can determine whether a crop survives. Water built village by village, pond by pond: that is what earned Sidhi its name on national radio.
