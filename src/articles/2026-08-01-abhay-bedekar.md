Hyderabad, July 24, 2026. Dr. Abhay Arvind Bedekar, Additional Managing Director of the Madhya Pradesh Tourism Board, stepped to the podium at the PHDCCI 15th International Heritage Tourism Conclave to make a case for conservation as economic policy. That same month, the Madhya Pradesh Sahitya Akademi announced that his Hindi diary, *Corona Yoddha Ki Sangharsh Katha* — "The Struggle Story of a Corona Warrior" — had been selected for the **Rajendra Anuragi Award 2026**, the Akademi's prize in the diary genre, carrying a citation and **₹51,000**. Dr. Bedekar was the sole selection in that category.

The diary documents his on-ground experience managing the COVID-19 response in Alirajpur, one of Madhya Pradesh's most remote and underserved tribal districts, where he served as Collector. The Sahitya Akademi's selection of an administrative officer's pandemic account — rather than a professional writer's fiction or verse — reflects how the jury read the work: as literature that earned its place through lived specificity, not genre convention.

## From Pandemic Diary to Heritage Corridors

The award does not sit in isolation. It describes the same officer whose footprint across MP's tourism infrastructure has grown steadily since his posting to the Tourism Board: a 2014-batch IPS officer of the Madhya Pradesh cadre, Dr. Bedekar has a doctorate and a career that has ranged from tribal administration to international trade promotion. He represented Madhya Pradesh at the **FITUR** travel trade fair in Madrid, pitching the state to European tourism markets.

At the Heritage Conclave in Hyderabad, he laid out his central argument in direct terms:

> "Heritage is both tangible and intangible and preserving it requires commitment, collaboration and pride. From UNESCO World Heritage Sites to community traditions and innovative partnerships, Madhya Pradesh is working to demonstrate how conservation can generate tourism opportunities while safeguarding cultural identity."

That argument has policy behind it. Under his leadership, the Tourism Board expanded the **Safe Tourism Destinations for Women (STDW)** initiative — a programme designed to address safety perception as a structural barrier to female tourism. The initiative has spread across **20 clusters and 50 destinations** across Madhya Pradesh. By mid-2026, approximately **40,000 people** had been trained in safety awareness and self-defence protocols; **10,000 women** had been upskilled in tourism-adjacent livelihoods; and roughly **5,000 jobs** had been created in those corridors. The programme drew recognition from **UN Women India**.

Linking the two threads — the administrator who sat at the edge of a pandemic and wrote about it, and the tourism official reshaping how women experience MP's heritage circuits — is not difficult. Both require translating a government brief into something that actually reaches people: a diary that witnesses what a Collector saw at ground level; a safety programme that builds trust in places that had none.

The Rajendra Anuragi Award names Dr. Bedekar alongside a tradition of Hindi writers the Madhya Pradesh Sahitya Akademi considers worth preserving. The Heritage Conclave gave him a national platform for what Madhya Pradesh's tourism can become. Both happened within the same month. For a 2014-batch officer still in mid-career, the picture they compose together is one to watch.
