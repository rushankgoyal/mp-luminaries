On the final evening of the Commonwealth Games 2026 athletics programme at Glasgow's Scotstoun Stadium, Neeraj Chopra launched a throw of **85.83 metres** to claim the silver medal in the men's javelin. The gold went to Rumesh Tharanga Pathirage of Sri Lanka, who set a personal best of **89.75 metres**. Behind Chopra, Yash Vir Singh threw **85.41 metres** on his CWG debut to secure bronze — giving India two athletes on the podium in a single javelin final for the first time in Commonwealth Games history.

The July 31 result was not the gold that Chopra's supporters might have hoped for, but it was a performance that mattered. Competing under headwind conditions that suppressed distances across the field, both Chopra's and Yash Vir's throws were technically strong, and the margin between them — 42 centimetres — illustrated how close Indian javelin has become at the very top of the Commonwealth.

## The Final and What It Showed

Pathirage's winning throw was exceptional by any measure — it outpaced the field by four metres — but Chopra was consistent, and his 85-metre-plus mark in headwind conditions shows a level of technical discipline that still separates him from most of the world. Yash Vir's 85.41 was a personal best on a global stage, and his CWG debut bronze represents the depth that Indian athletics has been quietly building in the javelin event.

For Chopra himself, the Glasgow final adds to one of Indian sport's most decorated portfolios. He is the only Indian to hold both an Olympic gold — won at Tokyo in 2020 — and a World Championship gold, claimed in Budapest in 2023. He has also held the Asian Games gold and the Commonwealth title from Birmingham 2022. The Glasgow silver is the latest entry in a career that has systematically raised Indian athletics' ceiling.

At 30, he continues to serve as Lt. Colonel in the Indian Army, a rank that reflects both his years of service and his standing as a national figure whose achievements carry institutional weight beyond the track.

For India's athletics programme, the Glasgow final offered something beyond one man's medal: evidence that the country now sends two javelin throwers capable of clearing 85 metres on a major stage. What Chopra pioneered, others are beginning to follow.
