On 19 September 2026, Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan travelled to Vidisha — the Madhya Pradesh constituency he has represented in Parliament since the 2024 general election — and launched a programme he has been building toward for months. The Samriddh Vidisha Model has a direct goal: to make every village in Vidisha district free of poverty, beginning by connecting **15,000 young people** to skill training and employment within the district.

The model works on two tracks. The first is skills: young men and women across Vidisha are being enrolled in courses at a rural self-employment training institute, where roughly 30-day programmes cover trades including tailoring, beauty and wellness services, and small-enterprise management. After completing training, participants assess whether they want to start a micro-business or seek employment, and are then guided toward the relevant government scheme or local opportunity. The second track is measurement: the programme will be monitored using a Development Index that tracks progress at the village level across income, health, education, and access to basic services.

## A District Pilot for a National Idea

The Samriddh Vidisha Model is the district-level application of Mission Samriddh Gaon, a national programme Chouhan launched in August 2026. The national mission aims to develop poverty-free, employment-rich villages across India with assured basic amenities, converging central government schemes on agriculture, rural employment, health, education, and infrastructure into a single household-level development plan. Pilot models are running in Budni block (Sehore district) and Khategaon block (Dewas district) of Madhya Pradesh before the programme is replicated elsewhere.

Chouhan's decision to apply this approach in Vidisha first follows a pattern from his time as Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh, a role he held across four terms totalling more than fifteen years. During those years, Sehore and Vidisha — districts he knows in detail — frequently served as testing grounds for welfare schemes that were later scaled to the state. The Samriddh Vidisha launch continues that approach from his current position at the Union Cabinet.

Vidisha is a plateau district in central Madhya Pradesh with a substantial share of rural households dependent on rain-fed agriculture. Seasonal farming income and limited formal employment opportunities mean that youth unemployment has historically been a persistent issue. The **15,000** target focuses specifically on youth from lower-income households within the district, with the aim of providing them a reliable first step into the economy rather than leaving them dependent on MGNREGS wages alone.

The Development Index that will track Vidisha's progress is one of the model's more distinctive features. Rather than measuring success by the number of trainees enrolled, it will track household income over time, access to government entitlements, and improvements in health and education indicators at the gram panchayat level — a more demanding definition of what "development" means than programme headcount alone.

If the Samriddh Vidisha results are measurable and transferable, they could offer Madhya Pradesh's other rural districts a tested framework for combining skills, monitoring, and scheme convergence into a coherent local poverty-reduction plan — one that works with existing government infrastructure rather than beside it.
