On 25 September 2026 — the birth anniversary of Pandit Deendayal Upadhyaya — a fleet of **351 buses** pulled out of BHEL Dussehra Maidan in Bhopal and fanned out across seven cities: Bhopal, Indore, Jabalpur, Rewa, Dewas, Katni and Bhind. Madhya Pradesh had not had a functioning government bus service since 2005, when the state wound down the Madhya Pradesh Road Transport Corporation after years of mounting losses. On Thursday, Union Minister Nitin Gadkari stood alongside Chief Minister Mohan Yadav and waved the green flag.

The Mukhyamantri Sugam Parivahan Seva runs alongside the central government's PM e-Bus Sewa, which has placed electric buses in cities and towns across India, bringing the two programmes together under one launch event.

At the ceremony, Gadkari made clear he regarded the launch as a beginning rather than a conclusion.

> "The poor also deserve good facilities in AC buses. Their fares should be 30 per cent lower than those of diesel buses. This is my vision."

He also told the operators directly: bus companies that cut corners on safety and quality would face the same scrutiny he applied to highway contractors. "This is just a news reel," he added. "The real film is yet to come."

## What 21 Years Without Buses Looked Like

Madhya Pradesh closed its road transport corporation gradually over a decade, completing the wind-down in 2005. For the two decades that followed, intercity travel in much of the state depended entirely on private operators, many of whom avoided less profitable routes that connected smaller towns and villages. Students, daily workers, and farmers travelling to agricultural markets faced either steep private fares or no reliable service at all.

The new service addresses this directly. Fares have been set at **₹2 per kilometre** for inter-city travel — a government-regulated rate. Each bus is fitted with CCTV cameras, a panic button and a Vehicle Location Tracking Device, giving passengers and authorities real-time oversight of every trip. The buses operate under a public-private partnership model: private companies run the vehicles while the state controls routes, fares and safety standards.

The state has committed to **1,500 buses by March 2027** and a fleet of **15,000 buses across all 55 districts by 2031**, connecting 75 per cent of Madhya Pradesh's development blocks to city centres.

## Why This Moment Matters

Madhya Pradesh has invested heavily in road infrastructure over the past decade, and national highway construction in the state has accelerated significantly under Gadkari's tenure as Union Minister. The arrival of buses on those roads closes a gap that better tarmac alone could not fill. Road quality tells you where you can go; a scheduled bus service tells you when, how much it will cost, and whether it is safe.

For the seven cities in the first phase, the change is immediate. For the fifty-five districts the state intends to reach by 2031, the message from the Bhopal launch is that the plan has now moved from announcement to execution. Nitin Gadkari flagged off the first buses; the target he has set — affordable, safe, scheduled transport for every Madhya Pradesh commuter — will determine what the rest of the film looks like.
