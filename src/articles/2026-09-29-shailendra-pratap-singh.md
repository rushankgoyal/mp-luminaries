On 5 September 2026, President Droupadi Murmu conferred the National Teachers' Award on **48 school teachers** from across India at Vigyan Bhavan in New Delhi. Among them was Shailendra Pratap Singh — an English teacher and in-charge headmaster at Government Middle School Kanjwar, a small school in a village in District Sidhi, one of Madhya Pradesh's more remote districts. He was one of six teachers from the state honoured that day, chosen from thousands of nominations submitted by states and union territories.

The number that most directly describes his work is not complicated. When Singh joined as in-charge headmaster at Kanjwar, the school had **69 students** enrolled and an average attendance of **44 per cent**. By the time his name was submitted for the national award, enrolment had reached **202 students** and attendance had climbed to **96 per cent**. Both figures represent a school that families had begun to avoid — and a school that, through specific, sustained effort, families now choose.

Government Middle School Kanjwar serves Village Kanjwar, Post Pondi Jodouri, Block Majhauli — an address that captures the geographic reach of what Singh undertook. Reaching families there is not a matter of announcing events or sending letters home. It means going to people directly: engaging panchayat members, speaking with parents in their homes, making the practical case that the government school is worth attending.

## Building the Case Ward by Ward

Singh worked through community participation. Rather than waiting for trust to come to him, he rebuilt it by showing up — in the village, at the panchayat, at the homes of families who had pulled their children out or never enrolled them at all. The results were gradual and then unmistakable: a school that had lost the community's confidence became the school that community members visibly preferred.

The attendance figure matters as much as the enrolment figure. Getting children enrolled is one task; getting them present every day is another. A 96 per cent attendance rate in a rural government school in Sidhi district means that families are not just nominally registered — they are actively sending their children, day after day, in the belief that showing up is worth something.

For Madhya Pradesh's government school system, Singh's recognition is a meaningful data point. The state has invested substantially in Sandipani Vidyalayas and infrastructure upgrades, but the performance of individual schools in remote areas still depends on what a single teacher decides to do with the resources available. Singh's school in Kanjwar is proof that the gap between what a government school could be and what it sometimes is can be closed — not with additional facilities alone, but with the kind of community-level engagement that does not show up in a syllabus.

Six teachers from Madhya Pradesh stood before the President on Teachers' Day 2026. Shailendra Pratap Singh came from the most out-of-the-way address among them. That is, in its own way, the point.
