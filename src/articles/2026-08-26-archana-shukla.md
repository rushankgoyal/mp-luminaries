On September 5, 2026 — Teachers' Day — President Droupadi Murmu will honour 48 outstanding school educators at Vigyan Bhawan in New Delhi with the National Teacher Award, the highest recognition a classroom teacher in India can receive. Among those 48 is Dr. Archana Shukla, a senior teacher at the Govt CM Rise Mahatma Gandhi Vidyalaya in Bhopal — one of only two teachers from all of Madhya Pradesh to make the national list this year.

The selection is not simply for years served. The Ministry of Education's criteria look at teaching innovation, community impact, student outcomes, and the ability to carry knowledge beyond a single classroom into the broader school ecosystem. Dr. Archana Shukla's work at the CM Rise school in Bhopal has earned that recognition.

## What Happens Inside the Classroom

The school where Dr. Shukla teaches is part of the Madhya Pradesh government's flagship CM Rise programme — a network of upgraded government schools designed to provide high-quality education at no cost to students from modest families. What the CM Rise model asks of teachers is considerable: they are expected to shift from rote instruction toward problem-solving, critical thinking, and real-world application.

Dr. Shukla has embraced that shift. Her approach centres on project-based learning — the kind of teaching where students do not simply read about science, history, or civic life but design, investigate, and build something around it. Documentation of her classroom work, shared through the School of Education and Systems Change, shows students active and engaged: constructing models, debating findings, presenting results. That is the signature of project-based learning done well, and it is difficult to sustain in a government school environment where resources and class sizes do not always cooperate.

Her school is listed under Bhopal's higher secondary sector — students at the older end of the school cycle, where the pull of early employment can be strong. Keeping adolescents invested in learning through activity-centred methods in a government school is its own accomplishment.

## Why This Selection Matters for MP

The National Teacher Award is given to at most one or two teachers per state each year from government schools, competing against nominations from all 27 states and seven union territories plus central government school networks such as Kendriya Vidyalayas and Navodaya Vidyalayas. Madhya Pradesh placed two teachers on the national list this year — Shailendra Pratap Singh from Sidhi (already honoured in these pages) and Dr. Archana Shukla from Bhopal.

Together, they represent the range of the state's teaching talent: one from a remote tribal district, one from the state capital's flagship government school. That both cleared the same national bar signals that quality government school teaching in Madhya Pradesh is not confined to a single corridor or a single type of institution.

For Bhopal's CM Rise schools, the award also matters institutionally. The CM Rise programme is the state's most visible investment in government school education. An award of this kind at the national level validates the programme's premise — that a well-supported teacher in a well-resourced government school can achieve outcomes that match or exceed what better-funded private schools produce.

Dr. Archana Shukla will receive the award from the President of India on September 5 at Vigyan Bhawan. It is the country's formal acknowledgement that a Bhopal schoolteacher is among the best at work in India today.
