On July 23, 2026, Ashok Barnwal walked into the Chief Secretary's chamber in Bhopal and took formal charge as Madhya Pradesh's **36th Chief Secretary**. The General Administration Department had issued the appointment order the previous day, when his predecessor Anurag Jain was relieved to assume charge as Chief Executive Officer of the NITI Aayog. Barnwal's transition to the state's top administrative post came after more than three decades at the heart of the MP bureaucracy.

He is a 1991-batch IAS officer of the Madhya Pradesh cadre — part of a cohort that joined the service at the end of the Cold War and has now reached the highest posts in government. His educational foundation is unusual for a senior IAS officer: he graduated in Mining Engineering with First Division before earning a Post Graduate Diploma in Public Policy and Management from IIM Bangalore, combining technical rigour with structured policy training.

## A Career Built Across Every Corner of the State

What sets Barnwal apart is the sheer breadth of what he has administered. Most senior officers specialise; his career file reads like a map of Madhya Pradesh itself.

He served as District Collector in **Dewas** and **Guna**, where a collector is the state government's face at the ground level — managing land disputes, disaster response, welfare delivery and law and order simultaneously. From there he moved through a succession of senior departmental roles: Managing Director of the **MP State Cooperative Marketing Federation (MARKFED)**, Principal Secretary overseeing Horticulture and Food Processing, and later Food Civil Supplies and Consumer Protection.

His environmental record is particularly significant. He served as Environment Commissioner and Director General of the **Environmental Planning and Coordination Organisation (EPCO)**, MP's nodal body for environmental governance. He later held additional charge of the Additional Chief Secretary post for the Environment Department — meaning he was simultaneously the state's senior-most official in both forest cooperation and environmental planning.

Most recently, as Additional Chief Secretary for Public Health and Medical Education, he oversaw a department that includes MP's network of government hospitals and medical colleges, alongside holding charge of the **Bhopal Gas Tragedy Relief and Rehabilitation Department** — one of the most complex ongoing administrative responsibilities in the state, managing the health and welfare of survivors from the 1984 disaster.

He has also worked in the Chief Minister's Secretariat under four different Chief Ministers, which gives him a rare fluency in how political priorities translate into administrative action.

Barnwal will serve until January 2027, though extensions are possible. The job he now holds coordinates every department in the government — from roads and revenue to education and health. The person who holds it determines how well the machinery of state functions, day to day, for **84 million people**. Barnwal carries 35 years of that machinery's inner workings with him when he sits down each morning.
