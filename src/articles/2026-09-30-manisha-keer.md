When Manisha Keer was growing up in a village near Bhopal, she played cricket, volleyball and football. Trap shooting was not something she had ever seen or heard of. It was her elder sister Sona — herself a sportsperson — who first took her to the Madhya Pradesh State Shooting Academy, changed her life's direction, and set her on the path to an Asian Games medal.

That medal arrived at the Asian Games 2026 in Aichi, Japan. Manisha, competing alongside Preeti Rajak and Rajeshwari Kumari, helped India claim the silver medal in the women's trap team event. China took gold, setting a Games record. India finished second — a podium finish that capped years of quietly accumulating excellence at one of the country's most productive shooting academies.

The journey to that podium was not straightforward. Manisha's family had limited financial resources, and when she began travelling for competitions she had to manage tightly. During her early years at the Academy, she often trained without a dedicated coach, relying on whatever guidance she could get — and leaning heavily on her sister. The Madhya Pradesh State Shooting Academy provided the infrastructure; her own determination supplied the rest.

## A Record That Maps the Distance Covered

In **2018**, Manisha won a junior World Championship silver medal in trap, the first major signal that her game had international potential. In successive years she has built a consistent presence in India's trap programme, qualifying for multiple national squads and, in 2026, earning her place in India's Asian Games squad led by seasoned Olympian Kynan Chenai.

The women's trap team result at Aichi — silver, behind only China — places India firmly in the top tier of women's trap shooting in Asia. India's team scored a combined total that left every other continental rival behind except the gold medallists.

For Manisha personally, the medal closes a loop that began with not knowing what a clay pigeon was. The distance between that starting point and a Games podium in Japan is not something most athletes travel.

## What the Medal Means for Madhya Pradesh

The Madhya Pradesh State Shooting Academy has shaped Manisha Keer the same way it has shaped several national-level trap and skeet shooters: by giving athletes from small towns and modest backgrounds the facilities and competitive exposure to reach the top of the sport. Neeru Dhanda, who won individual trap gold at the same Games, is also supported by the same Academy.

That two MP-connected shooters stood on Asian Games podiums in the same week in Aichi is not coincidence — it is the product of sustained investment in a discipline that does not generate cricket-sized headlines but has quietly made India one of Asia's leading shotgun-shooting nations. Manisha Keer's silver is as good a demonstration of that as any.
