On 29 July 2026, the village of Bakayan in Damoh district will once again become what it has been every Guru Purnima since 1894: the site of a 48-hour unbroken stream of classical music. Musicians arrive from across India, receive no payment, and offer their performance as guru dakshina — a disciple's gift to the memory of a teacher. This is the **132nd consecutive edition** of the Guru Purnima Sangeet Mahotsav, India's second-oldest continuously running classical music festival.

Arun Palnitkar, patron of the Guru Purnima Utsav Committee and former director of the Ustad Alauddin Khan Music and Art Academy in Bhopal, represents the family thread connecting that first gathering to the one happening this year.

The festival carries a precise name: the Mridangacharya Pt. Nanasaheb Panse Smriti Guru Purnima Sangeet Mahotsav. It honours Pandit Nana Saheb Panse, the great mridangacharya who was the guru of Balakrishna Rao Topiwala — the man who founded the event. Balakrishna Rao came from the Palnitkar family, landowners with roots in Bundelkhand. After a British forest officer shot dead his elder brother Madhavrao for refusing a humiliating order, Balakrishna Rao moved to Indore and eventually returned to his family's village of Bakayan each year to honour his teacher through music. By 1894, the gathering had become an annual fixture that the family resolved to hold without interruption.

## The 2005 Decision That Secured the Festival's Future

For 111 consecutive years, the Palnitkar family ran the festival through changing generations, two world wars and India's independence. In 2005, with the family's capacity to manage the event alone naturally shrinking, they made a deliberate institutional decision: they transferred stewardship of the Mahotsav to the Ustad Alauddin Khan Music and Art Academy. The Academy, a Madhya Pradesh government institution named after the legendary musician who gave rise to the Maihar gharana, brought the organisational infrastructure to ensure continuity.

Arun Palnitkar, who has served as the Academy's director and remains the family's representative in the festival's governance, was central to that transition. Under the current structure, Vivek Shendye chairs the Guru Purnima Utsav Committee, with Arjun Patel as convener — a team that handles logistics while Palnitkar's involvement ensures the founding spirit remains intact.

The festival's defining rule — that no performer receives any fee — has held across all 132 editions. Musicians, scholars and rasikas come to Bakayan not for remuneration or recognition but to participate in a collective act of gratitude toward the guru-shishya tradition. The Hanuman Temple at the village's centre becomes the concert space; for 48 hours the music does not stop.

For a village of fewer than 2,000 people in Damoh district, that is a remarkable claim on national cultural memory. Madhya Pradesh has richer venues and more prominent music institutions, but few can say what Bakayan can: that for **132 years**, the music on Guru Purnima night has never once been silent.
