The exhibition hall at the Kushabhau Thakre International Convention Centre in Bhopal fell into expectant quiet on October 8, 2026, when Chief Secretary Ashok Barnwal stepped forward to formally open the 7th Madhya Pradesh Travel Mart alongside Union Tourism Secretary Bhuvnesh Kumar. Behind him stretched 177 stalls staffed by travel operators, hoteliers, homestay owners, and tourism entrepreneurs — from Madhya Pradesh and, for the first time at this scale, from 34 countries.

"Tourism in the state is experiencing sustained growth, accompanied by a steady rise in tourist footfall," Barnwal told the assembled delegates. He called on the travel and hospitality sector to act as brand ambassadors for Madhya Pradesh — to carry the state's cultural heritage, wildlife corridors, and rural traditions to audiences that have not yet visited, and to make the investment case to buyers who are only beginning to understand what the state offers.

## The Case, in Numbers

That pitch was not abstract. Madhya Pradesh welcomed more than **21 crore tourists in 2025** — a **57 per cent increase** over 2024. The state's wildlife circuit, running through Kanha, Pench, Bandhavgarh, Panna, and Satpura, continues to grow as a global safari destination. The heritage trail linking Khajuraho and Orchha draws a rising share of international visitors. Rural tourism, including tribal homestay programmes and community forest trails, is creating new economic activity in districts that have historically had little share of the tourism economy.

At MPTM 2026, those numbers translated into concrete commitments. Twelve strategic MoUs were signed during the four-day event, with the largest being a **₹848.40-crore agreement** between the MP Tourism Board and ARM Foods Pvt Ltd for a resort development in Ramgarh, Sehore. The project — a golf course, private villas, a wellness resort, and a luxury hotel — is projected to generate around **920 direct jobs** in a district that has not previously had significant tourism infrastructure. Total investment proposals across the event reached **₹1,100 crore**, making this the most commercially productive Travel Mart in the event's seven-year history.

Barnwal also walked the exhibition floor, holding direct conversations with tour operators, hoteliers, and international buyers — asking what packages they needed, what infrastructure was still missing, and where expansion plans could move fastest with government support.

## Building the Machinery

For Barnwal, the mart's outcomes represent an early marker of what his tenure as Chief Secretary can achieve. He took charge on July 23, 2026, as MP's 36th Chief Secretary, after a career that covered district collectorships in Dewas and Guna, forest cooperative management, environmental governance, medical education oversight, and Bhopal gas tragedy rehabilitation. The breadth of that background matters for tourism: a destination of the scale MP is targeting requires roads, power, water, wildlife management, and heritage conservation all moving together.

The event also launched a new **"3Gs Framework"** — Guides, Guidelines, and Gear — to standardise service quality across MP's tourism sector. Officials announced that a comprehensive roadmap for MP tourism through 2028 would be developed in the coming months, giving investors a clearer view of where the state intends to build.

Madhya Pradesh has grown into its tourism ambition steadily, without much of the media attention that coastal and hill destinations attract. The 7th Travel Mart's numbers — and the Chief Secretary's direct articulation of what the state is building — put that quiet growth on the international calendar for good.
