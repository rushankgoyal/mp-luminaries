On the morning of September 10, Madhya Pradesh Governor Mangubhai C Patel administered the oath of office to Justice Alpesh Yeshvant Kogje at the Lokbhavan in Bhopal, making him the **30th Chief Justice of the Madhya Pradesh High Court**. Chief Minister Mohan Yadav and senior officials of the state were present for the ceremony.

Justice Kogje steps into the role from the Gujarat High Court, where he had served as a judge for just over a decade. He was appointed Additional Judge of the Gujarat High Court on April 6, 2016, and confirmed as a permanent judge on March 15, 2018. His path to the bench was built over two decades of active practice at the Bar in Gujarat: he earned his law degree from the Faculty of Law at Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda in 1993, the same university where he had graduated in chemistry three years earlier, and joined legal practice immediately after.

In the years that followed, he took on roles that gave him a thorough view of the state's legal machinery from multiple sides. He served as Assistant Government Pleader from 2001, and as Additional Public Prosecutor from 2002 to 2007, developing deep experience in government litigation before large courts. He also served as Standing Counsel for the Central Excise and Customs Department, handling the kind of technical, high-volume government matters that demand precision and stamina in equal measure.

## From the Bar to the Bench: Thirty-Three Years in the Law

Born on July 16, 1969, Justice Kogje is 57. He spent **thirty-three years** working within the Indian legal system before being elevated to the Chief Justice's chair in Madhya Pradesh — first as a practising advocate, then as a government counsel, and finally as a Gujarat High Court judge who brought that ground-level experience to his decisions on the bench.

The Supreme Court Collegium recommended his appointment as Chief Justice of the Madhya Pradesh High Court on August 31, 2026. The Union government and the President of India cleared the appointment on September 5, and the swearing-in ceremony followed five days later. The speed of the clearance reflected the clarity of his credentials.

The Madhya Pradesh High Court, headquartered in Jabalpur, is one of the older and busier high courts in India. It serves a state of approximately 8 crore people spread across 55 districts, handling civil, criminal, constitutional, and revenue matters from across that territory. Like courts everywhere in India, it carries a significant volume of pending cases — disputes over land, family matters, commercial claims, and appeals in criminal cases — where each year's delay means a year longer for families and individuals waiting on outcomes that can change their lives.

The Chief Justice chairs both the administrative and judicial work of the court. The person who holds that chair influences how the institution is organised, how judges are allocated to cases, and what the culture of the court looks and feels like to the lawyers and litigants who walk through its doors. Justice Kogje, arriving from a different state with no prior ties to Madhya Pradesh's bench or Bar, brings the independence of fresh eyes. For the court and the people who use it, September 10 marks the beginning of his tenure — and the opening of a new chapter in Madhya Pradesh's judiciary.
