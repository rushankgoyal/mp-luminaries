The Sub-Junior Women's final of the 4th Hockey India Academy Championship in Gwalior went to a penalty shootout. Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy and Pritam Siwach Hockey Academy from Sonepat had played to a 2-2 draw through sixty minutes. What followed was three minutes of saves by Paridhi Mewada, and then a title for Madhya Pradesh.

Paridhi, the goalkeeper for Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy's sub-junior squad, faced five Sonepat shootout attempts and stopped three of them. Her teammates — Bhavika, Saroj Mansi, and Geethasri Nammi — converted their chances, and MP won the shootout **3-1**. The championship, held at the Major Dhyan Chand Hockey Stadium in Gwalior from July 26 to August 3, 2026, was over. Madhya Pradesh had their Zone A & B sub-junior title.

What made August 3 remarkable was that it was a double. A few hours earlier, Madhya Pradesh Hockey Academy's junior team had beaten the same Pritam Siwach Academy 3-2 in a hard-fought Junior Women's Final. One academy, two age groups, two championships — on the same afternoon, in their home city.

## Sixteen Years of Building This Moment

Paridhi's saves did not happen in a vacuum. They came out of a system that Madhya Pradesh has been building for sixteen years.

The MP Hockey Academies in Bhopal and Gwalior were set up to create a continuous pipeline: identify talent early, train them through the sub-junior and junior stages, and feed the best into senior state teams and, eventually, national squads. Over sixteen years, the system has produced **29 medals** at Hockey India National Championships — **23 of them in women's events**. No other state has been as consistent in women's academy hockey.

Coach Paramjeet Singh has been central to the women's programme, working with players across sub-junior, junior and senior levels. His methods have produced players who competed at the Rio 2016 Olympics — ending a 36-year absence of Madhya Pradesh-trained talent from the Olympic squad. Vivek Sagar Prasad and Nilakanta Sharma on the men's side came through this system; so did Sushila Chanu and Ishika Chaudhary on the women's.

Paridhi Mewada is now part of this lineage. She is a sub-junior goalkeeper in Gwalior — the same city, the same stadium system, the same coaching culture that has been sending players into national hockey for a generation.

The specific drama of a shootout suits a goalkeeper well as a story: it is the one moment in hockey where her role becomes the entire match. Paridhi converted that moment on August 3. Whether she goes on to represent India will depend on years of training, selection, and opportunity. What the Gwalior final showed is that the foundation is there — a young goalkeeper composed enough to save three of five attempts in a national championship final.

For Madhya Pradesh, the double title on August 3 matters beyond the scoreline. It confirms that the academy system works not just at the top — not just for the players who have already made it — but at the formative stage, where players like Paridhi are still becoming who they will be. That pipeline is what turns a state into a sustained producer of hockey talent, rather than a place that occasionally gets lucky with a single exceptional athlete.

Hockey India's zone-level championships exist precisely to catch players like Paridhi early. When those championships produce double-title days for one academy, the broader system is working.
