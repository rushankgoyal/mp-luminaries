At the Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in New Delhi last Thursday, Sunil Dawar crossed the finish tape first in the men's 5,000 metres final, running **14:01.64** to take gold at the 65th National Open Athletics Championships. Shivaji Madappa of Services finished second in 14:02.81 and Ajay Bind was third. For Dawar, a runner who has built his career on steady progress and a refusal to disappear from the picture, it was the result a half-decade of near-misses had been pointing toward.

Dawar trains out of the Madhya Pradesh Athletics Academy in Bhopal, the state's principal track-and-field development hub. He has worn the MP colours at senior national competitions for several years. At the 2024 National Inter-State Senior Athletics Championships he clocked **14:02.75** for Madhya Pradesh — a silver medal that announced him as a genuine senior contender. At the 63rd National Open in Bengaluru, he finished second again in 14:00.50, this time behind Gulveer Singh, who set a meet record. Two silvers, no gold — until October 10, when the wait ended by a margin of less than two seconds.

## A Record That Stood for 24 Years, and What Came Next

Long before Dawar reached the senior podium, he had left a mark in India's distance-running history. At the National Junior Athletics Championships in Guwahati, he ran the men's U-20 5,000 metres in **14:13.95**, erasing a national junior record that had endured for 24 years. That kind of benchmark — young, from a state-run academy, running a time that had not been touched since the 1990s — placed him in a different category of talent entirely. World Athletics tracks his career internationally and registers both that junior milestone and his subsequent climb through the senior ranks.

Dawar's personal background grounds the achievement further. Accounts of his early life describe a childhood in which resources were scarce and running was, among other things, a path forward. That detail is not incidental: it reflects the exact sort of athlete the MP Athletics Academy was built to develop — those from smaller towns and constrained circumstances who would otherwise never reach a national final. The pipeline works when athletes like Dawar come through it.

This year's National Open was itself a strong showing for Madhya Pradesh athletics as a whole. Kuldeep Kumar set a pole vault meet record with a **5.30-metre** clearance, Arjun Waskale won the men's 1500m, and the state collected medals in the high jump and 10,000m too. Dawar's gold on Day 3 was one more point in that collective performance — confirmation that Madhya Pradesh is operating at a level where national medals are a recurring outcome, not an exception.

For Dawar personally, the 14:01 range is competitive at the national senior level. Having held India's U-20 5,000-metre record, having finished second at a national open, and having now won one, the logical next horizon is international representation. A sub-14:00 performance would mark that transition. Thursday's run suggests it is no longer a distant aspiration.
