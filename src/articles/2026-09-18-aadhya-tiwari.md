Aadhya Tiwari's father played Ranji Trophy cricket. She grew up in Hoshangabad, on the Narmada River in Madhya Pradesh, and began playing tennis when she was three years old. By her late teens she was ranked Asia No. 6 in lawn tennis — a level that placed her among the continent's best in her age group.

She did not turn professional in that sport. Competitive lawn tennis at the high level requires sustained investment in coaching, equipment, and international travel that was not available to her. When the Madhya Pradesh Soft Tennis Association approached her and asked if she would try the rubber-ball version of the game, she agreed. In her very first soft tennis tournament, she won two bronze medals and a silver. She stayed.

## A Practical Change With a Historic Result

Soft tennis uses a rubber ball, bounces differently from lawn tennis, and draws its competitive depth almost entirely from East Asia. India's players have participated in international soft tennis competitions for years; until 2024, no Indian had won a medal at the World Soft Tennis Championships.

Tiwari began building her record in the sport steadily. She won bronze at the Asian Junior Soft Tennis Championship in 2018. At the 2022 National Games in Gujarat, she and mixed doubles partner Jay Meena won **gold**, and she added individual medals in the singles events. The Madhya Pradesh government recognised her results with the **Vikram Award** in 2023 — the state's highest sports honour — and separately with the Eklavya Award.

The result that placed her in the record books came in September 2024 at the World Soft Tennis Championships in Anseong, South Korea. Partnering Meena, Tiwari won the mixed doubles bronze medal, defeating the Philippines in the Round of 16 and China in the quarterfinals before losing to the eventual finalists. It was India's first podium at a World Soft Tennis Championship.

> "In my very first soft tennis tournament, I got two bronze and one silver medal, and my name came up in the Junior World Championship and Senior World Championship."

She works with the Income Tax department and continues to train alongside her job. At the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya — where soft tennis runs from September 18 to 23 at Higashiyama Park Tennis Center — she competes in women's singles and mixed doubles.

Madhya Pradesh has produced athletes across many disciplines, and the state government's awards programme has tried to bring those contributions into public view. Tiwari's path — from a small Narmada-bank city, through two different racket sports, across junior and senior international circuits, to a world podium — is exactly the kind of story that programme exists to recognise. She heads to Japan as one of India's few realistic soft tennis medal prospects, in a competition the country has never won. The switch she made years ago, from one racket to another, is why she has that chance at all.
