The number on the scoreboard at Scotstoun Stadium in Glasgow on 28 July 2026 was **9.81 metres**. That throw by Sharmila Dhankar in the women's F57 shot put final gave India its first-ever gold medal in para-athletics at the Commonwealth Games. Ghana's Zinabu Issah took silver at 8.65 metres — a gap of more than a metre. Sharmila Dhankar did not edge out a close final. She won it clearly, with a season-best performance, at the age of 40.

She contracted polio at the age of two. The disease affected one of her legs. She grew up in Chitrauli village, Mahendragarh district, Haryana, raised two daughters, and for three decades, competitive para-athletics was simply not part of her life. She took up the F57 seated shot put at the age of 34 — as a mother, without a junior career, without years of systematic coaching behind her.

Within three years of starting, she had become India's national champion in F57 seated shot put in 2021. Within six years, she stood on the top step of a Commonwealth Games podium.

## The Discipline and the Journey

In para-athletics, the F57 classification covers athletes with good arm and trunk function but impairment in the lower limbs. F57 shot put athletes compete from a seated position fixed to the ground. The technical demands — rotation of the upper body, timing of release, repeatability across multiple attempts under pressure — are distinct from standing throws, and they reward consistency above all.

Dhankar built her international career step by step from 2021 onwards, competing on the global circuit while working on the specific mechanics of the seated throw. The Glasgow final was her season's best performance. Devendra Jhajharia, President of the Paralympics Committee of India and himself a double Paralympic gold medallist in javelin, described her trajectory clearly: despite starting para-athletics only at 34, she "quickly rose through the ranks, becoming India's national champion in 2021 before achieving the biggest success of her career" in Glasgow.

Indian para-athletics has grown significantly in recent years — at the Paris 2024 Paralympics, India won 29 medals, including seven gold. But a gold in para-athletics at the Commonwealth Games had never been won before 28 July 2026. Sharmila Dhankar changed that.

On 4 August, she landed at Indira Gandhi International Airport in Delhi and was received by family, supporters, and well-wishers. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh was among those who congratulated her publicly. She told reporters that the Commonwealth gold was not an endpoint — more competitions, more goals were ahead.

## A Career Built on a Late Start

The particular weight in Sharmila Dhankar's story is the timing. Starting a new sport at 34, as a mother, with no prior competitive history, runs against the logic of how most elite athletic careers are supposed to begin. The conventional belief — that the younger the start, the longer the runway, the better the result — did not apply here.

She started at 34. She became national champion at 37. She won a Commonwealth Games gold at 40.

For Indian para-sport, her gold adds to an already compelling record of athletes proving that Indian para-athletes are competitive at the top of global competition. For Sharmila Dhankar, it is proof of a simpler thing: that when she committed to the sport, she could reach its summit. The work between 2021 and 2026 — the training sessions, the international competitions, the incremental improvements — produced 9.81 metres at exactly the right moment.
