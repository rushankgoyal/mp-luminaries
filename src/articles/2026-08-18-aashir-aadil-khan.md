The 58th minute. Coimbatore. Semi-final of the 16th Hockey India Junior Men National Championship. Jharkhand are still searching for a way back into the match. Two minutes earlier, Rajveer Singh had broken the deadlock. Now **Aashir Aadil Khan** receives the ball, reads the space, and finishes. **Hockey Madhya Pradesh 2, Hockey Jharkhand 0.**

It was the goal that killed the contest and sent MP into a national final.

## A Tournament Built Goal by Goal

**Aashir Aadil Khan** was one of the consistent scoring contributors for Hockey Madhya Pradesh throughout the 16th Hockey India Junior Men National Championship 2026, held in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. Across the tournament, he scored in multiple fixtures — including the 13th minute of an earlier group-stage match and a two-goal performance in MP's **9–0** victory. By the time the semi-final arrived, he had made himself a reliable presence in the attacking third.

The semi-final against Hockey Jharkhand on **August 6** was the defining moment. Jharkhand, one of the stronger regional hockey forces in the sub-20 bracket, held MP scoreless for more than 55 minutes. The match was tight. What MP needed was someone to convert when the moment finally opened — and Rajveer Singh did, in the 56th minute. Khan's follow-up, two minutes later, was the insurance: a goal scored with the match still delicately poised, when Jharkhand could yet have equalised and forced extra time.

It sealed MP's place in the national final.

## The Final and What It Means

On **August 8**, Hockey Madhya Pradesh faced Hockey Association of Odisha in the championship match. Odisha, who went on to win the title, struck early — Mandeep Kerketta scoring in the **second minute** — and added a second through Deepak Pradhan. MP fought back: Sohil Ali pulled one back in the **37th minute**, and the side pressed hard to level, but Odisha held. Final score: **Odisha 2, Madhya Pradesh 1.**

Runners-up. A silver medal. And a tournament to be proud of.

## MP Hockey's Upward Curve

Hockey has not historically been associated with Madhya Pradesh the way it is with Odisha, Punjab, or Jharkhand. That is changing. The MP hockey academy system — based in Bhopal and operating with district-level feeders — has been producing players who are increasingly competitive at the national age-group level. Reaching the final of a Division A national championship, against states with far larger dedicated hockey infrastructures, is evidence of that progress.

For **Aashir Aadil Khan**, this tournament is a marker. A national runners-up medal, scored goals at every level of the knockouts, and a full competitive week against India's best junior state sides — it is the kind of formative experience that separates players who go on from players who plateau.

The national selectors, who watch these championships closely, will have taken note. So have the coaches at Hockey Madhya Pradesh. The name is in the conversation now.
